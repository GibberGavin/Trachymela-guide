Morphology: Male Genitalia

If a male specimen is available, the aedeagus provides critical diagnostic information to discriminate between very similar species. In addition to its shape and structure, its length (LPE) relative to the body length (BL) is a valuable character.

1. General Shape

In the key, the general profile of the aedeagus (viewed from above) is categorized into four primary forms. Sometimes, two profiles may fit the shape - for example, it may be lanceolate in shape but also fit the definition of long and thin. In that case, either or both of the options may be checked. Click on a thumbnail below to view a representative example:

Long and thin This is defined as the length (LPE) being more than four times the width at its widest point (WPE).
Lanceolate In this shape, the aedeagus is broadest at the base and then narrows all the way to the apex.
Trapezoid This is a relatively uncommon shape in Trachymela. In this shape, the widest point of the aedeagus is very close to the apex.
Other Select this option for any shape that does not fit into one of the other categories.

5. Standard Measurements

Figure: Measurement of LPE, HPE, and WPE.

To ensure consistency, measurements should be taken as follows:

  • LPE: Length of the aedeagus. This is measured from the tip of the apex (ignoring any protruding parts of flagellum, ostial dorsal flaps or internal sac) to the extreme basal end.
  • WPE: Maximum width of the aedeagus. This measurement is taken across the widest point when the sides of the aedeagus are not sinuate. For many species (including the one illustrated), the width will decrease from the basal sulcus towards the apex, and then increase again to a maximum before the sides finally converge towards the apex. In those cases, the width (WPE) is measured across this more apical maximum.
  • HPE: Maximum height (profile view). Measured as the shortest distance between a line joining the ventral extremities of the aedeagus and its highest point dorsally. Note that the ratio HPE/LPE is an indicator of how strongly the organ is curved.
