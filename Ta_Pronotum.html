Morphology: Pronotum

Pronotum Structure and Markings

The pronotum in Trachymela provides several characters useful in species discrimination. Blackburn and some other early authors relied heavily on its proportions, the nature of its lateral margins and surface features such as roughness, puncturation and the presence or absence of verrucae to help delineate species. Here each of the relevant features is discussed and its use in the key is illustrated.

Shape and Proportions

Figure 1: Measuring Pronotal Width (PW) vs Length (PL).

The ratio of width to length (PW/PL) generally varies between 2 and 3. Another critical character is the position of the widest point along the lateral margin and whether those margins are explanate (flattened/flared) or strictly convex.

Surface Texture & Puncturation

Texture ranges from highly polished and smooth to distinctly rugose (rough). Puncturation varies significantly in density and strength.

Figure 2a: Fine, evenly distributed punctures.
Figure 2b: Strong, clumped puncturation on a rugose surface.

Verrucae: Discal & Foveal

The presence, shape, and arrangement of verrucae (wart-like elevations) are highly diagnostic.

Discal Verrucae

These are located on the main disc of the pronotum. They may be entirely absent, or present as distinct, often darker, elevations.

Foveal Verrucae

Located within the lateral foveae (depressions), these can be categorized into three primary states:

State 1: Absent
State 2: Compact
State 3: Diffuse

Colour and Markings

Figure 4: Typical colour variation.

While often a shade of brown, the pronotum may exhibit darker maculations or a paler marginal area. Note that the presence of cuticular wax can significantly obscure these underlying patterns.
