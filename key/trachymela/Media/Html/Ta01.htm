Ta01

Name

Trachymela catenata (Chapuis, 1877)

Reference specimen

2000-0062a, Boonoo Boonoo, NE New South Wales.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.2-9.2 mm, ♂ 6.8-8.5 mm.

Colour:  head: variable, sometimes uniformly reddish-brown with black-tipped mandibles, often a broad black triangular area, widest anteriorly, is present between eyes, occasionally the black area occupies almost all of space between eyes, sometimes extending forward so that mandibles are completely black; antennomeres 1-11 light to mid-brown, the distal antennomeres sometimes slightly darker; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, frequently with two black, longitudinal patches that may be indistinct or quite clearly defined; scutellum: brown, same shade as pronotum, usually broadly edged in a slightly or much darker shade of brown, sometimes the darker shade present throughout; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with several darker brown or, less often, black patches, sometimes indistinct, usually at least partially present, a roughly round patch straddling elytral summit, another continuing the black patch on pronotum and another forming an interrupted band from the humeral callus towards apex along margin of elytral disc, sometimes the marginal area is partially black, very rarely are darker markings completely absent, punctures are outlined in a fractionally darker shade than interstices, humeral callus is often darker brown; venter: mid- to dark brown, rarely almost black with legs usually a fractionally lighter shade, inside surface of elytral epipleura variable, brown to partially or fully black.  Waxy dorsal covering: usually a sparse to dense layer of uniform shade that can be from light-grey to brown.  In some specimens two distinct shades are present, with both the pronotum and elytra having relatively thin light-grey margins contrasting with darker brown away from the margins.

Head; punctures variable, usually quite large (pd~2xef) and somewhat unevenly but quite densely packed, sometimes partially obliterated by rugose interstices, rarely smaller and more evenly distributed. Antennae long (AL ♂=48-53%BL, ♀=39-44%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, with a broad but very shallow depression situated behind the outside edge of each eye, puncturation usually clearly impressed, quite evenly distributed and moderately dense in discal area, becoming much coarser at lateral margins.  Specimens from Northern Queensland have considerably smaller and less densely and evenly distributed punctures in discal area.  Much finer punctures are scattered among the larger ones.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; lightly punctured, more so anteriorly, with punctures similar in size to those on adjacent area on pronotum.  Elytra; surface evenly curved, punctures distinct and relatively evenly distributed, with much smaller punctures scattered sparingly on the interstices, several longitudinal rows of pimple-like raised interstices, each with a central puncture, are present though inconspicuous due to being the same colour as the surrounding surface, suture carinate in posterior 25%, surface continuous under humeral callus, vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.36-0.40); lateral margin usually slightly oblique between humeral callus and a point a little anterior to summit, straight and perpendicular to anterior edge elsewhere.  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, open or lightly closed anteriorly, short setae present at rear of prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate, 1st abdominal segment smooth, unpunctured.  Aedeagus; small (LPE=25-29%BL), quadrate, in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided for most of length, then broadening slightly near apex before the sides converge towards apex in a smooth arc, dorsal surface smoothly curved with faint dorso-median channel usually present, tip barely protruding, ostium transversely oval; in lateral view, almost evenly curved throughout its length, thinning towards apex over 20% of length, tip slightly reflexed, very thin and spout-like, short protruding segment of flagellum is strongly curved and moderately thick.

 

Larvae

3rd instar: light-green overall; with black head and legs, and abdominal segments showing a slight tinge of red. Black markings on prothorax.  Small black tubercles on thoracic segments, and prominent, larger tubercles on abdominal segments.  These larvae are rather flattened in appearance.  Apical abdominal segment covered in black, shield-like chitin.  4th instar: Colour becomes considerably darker, with mesothorax, metathorax and abdomen tending towards dark red-brown.  Prothorax is creamy white (with speckled black markings), with margins brownish-red.  Head is black.  The larvae are quite squat in shape, being widest about the 4th and 5th abdominal segments.

Eggs

Yellow, laid in batches of about 8 (?) side-by-side on surface of leaves.

Similar species

Ta13 – this species is most readily distinguished by the yellow brown (mustard) colour of the cuticular wax.  Dark markings on the pronotum and elytra can be very similar in the two species, and it may be almost impossible to determine female specimens.  The apical edge of the aedeagus of Ta13 is almost straight rather than rounded as in Ta01.  Ta13 seems to be restricted to ironbark species as hosts.

Ta26 – individuals of this species are larger than those of Ta01, there is almost no overlap in the male and female size distributions.  Ta26 usually has no dark markings on prothorax and elytra, although there are exceptions to this, especially in specimens from north Queensland.

Notes

The identification of Ta01 as T. catenata is by B.J. Selman, from a Boonoo Boonoo (N.E. New South Wales) specimen.  The holotype in RBINS has been examined and it is clearly a specimen of this species.

Ta01 is the most frequently encountered member of the catenata group of species.  All the species in this group differ from other Trachymela in both laying their eggs on the surface of leaves rather than cryptically under bark or in crevices in the trunk and having larvae that rest exposed on the foliage that they are feeding on.

The sex ratio of this sample of 199 specimens examined is female-biased, with 58.3% being female, which constitutes a statistically significant departure from a 50:50 sex ratio for the population (exact binomial test, p=0.023).

This is a widely distributed and commonly encountered species in coastal and highland areas of Queensland and N.S.W., where it is often present in large numbers.  It is very similar in appearance to Ta13, though usually slightly smaller and often lighter in shade.  However, in practice it can be almost impossible to distinguish between female specimens of these two species.  There is a wide variation in the extent of the black markings, with rare specimens showing none at all.

Ta01 has a remarkably wide host range across widely differing eucalypts compared with most other Trachymela.  In the northern parts of its distribution, it is most commonly found on various species of ironbark (Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria), particularly Eucalyptus melanophloia, E. crebra, E. fibrosa and E. cullenii as well as on Angophora.  Specimens from the highlands of NSW (eg, around Tenterfield in the north and Cooma in the south) have been primarily found on the snow gum, E. pauciflora (Eucalyptus : Cineraceae), E. stellulata (Eucalyptus : Longitudinales) and narrow-leaf peppermint, E. acaciiformes (Symphyomyrtus : Maidenaria). Many specimens have also been collected from Spotted Gum, Blakella maculata.  These host account for 78% of host records with unidentified Eucalyptus sp (28 records) accounting for most of the rest.  Single records each from Blakella dallachiana, B. tessellaris, E. bridgesiana, E. leptophleba and a stringybark species are likely to be incidental records or host misidentifications and not indicative of host preference.

The larvae of Ta01 are quite different to those of Ta13 or Ta26.
