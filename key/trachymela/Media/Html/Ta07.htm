Ta07

Name

Trachymela impressa (Chapuis)

Reference specimen

2003-0067, Yangan, S.E. Queensland

Adult Description

Length: ♀ (8.3)8.7-10.3 mm, ♂ 8.1-8.7 mm (9).

Colour:  head: usually uniformly reddish-brown with mandible tips dark-brown or black, frequently a thin, irregular longitudinal dark-brown to black patch is present on either side extending posteriorly from just inside antennal insertion; antennomeres 1-4 light-brown, 5-11 usually similar, sometimes slightly darker, if so, the distal portion of each segment often darker than proximate; pronotum: brown, same or fractionally darker shade as head, frequently with vaguely defined darker patches that are coincident with elevated areas; scutellum: brown, same or fractionally darker shade as pronotum, rarely edged in a slightly darker shade; elytra: brown, same or fractionally darker shade as pronotum, with areas of lighter-brown usually present over all or some of the following area: a broad transverse band extending from elytral suture between scutellum and summit to humeral callus from where another broad band extends posteriorly along discal margin to apex, deeply depressed areas such as median depression are sometimes darker in colour within that band, sometimes only the elevated interstices within that band are of the lighter colour, less commonly, the whole elytral disc is of the lighter colour; venter: mid- to dark brown, inside surface of elytral epipleurae black.  Cuticular wax: usually a sparse but quite unevenly distributed layer of uniform shade that can be from light-grey to light-brown.

Head; punctures variable, usually quite large (pd~2-3xef) and somewhat unevenly and moderately densely distributed, sometimes partially obliterated by rugose interstices, a small unpunctured, elevated area is usually present adjacent to fronto-clypeal suture and close to eye. Antennae long (AL ♂=52-54%BL, ♀=39-43%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, with a broad but shallow depression situated behind the outside edge of each eye, surface of disc rugulose with many scattered, small, irregularly shaped elevated areas, puncturation moderately dense in discal area though often lost in the rugulosity of discal surface, becoming much coarser at lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially straight but then curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; moderately densely and quite evenly punctured, with punctures similar in size or slightly smaller than those on adjacent area on pronotum.  Elytra; surface strongly curved, surface very rugose with many elevated verrucae and interstices, punctures distinct in places but more frequently lost in the elytral rugulosity, with much smaller punctures scattered sparingly on the interstices, interstices are often quite large and many are placed in roughly longitudinal bands although many transversely aligned raised interstices are also present, many have central punctures, a deep anterior-median depression is present, usually bisected by a longitudinal elevated interstice, humeral callus strongly elevated above surface, surface discontinuous under it.  Vertical extension of epipleurae considerable (HCE/PW=0.41-0.46).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, closed anteriorly, short setae present at rear of prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single large seta on anterior and median trochanter with sometimes some smaller ones also present, rear trochanters usually with a small clump of setae, rarely just one; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate; inner edge of posterior of epipleurae lacking setae.  Aedeagus; small (LPE=24-27%BL), quadrate, in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided for most of length, then broadening slightly near apex before the sides converge towards apex in a smooth arc, dorsal surface smoothly curved with dorso-median channel usually absent, rarely faintly present, tip barely protruding, ostium transversely oval; in lateral view, almost evenly curved throughout its length, thinning towards apex over 20% of length, tip slightly reflexed, very thin and spout-like, short protruding segment of flagellum is strongly curved and moderately thick.

Similar species

 Ta41 - the inner surface of the epipleura is black only in the posterior half in almost all specimens of Ta41.  The aedeagus of Ta07 is effectively indistinguishable from that of Ta41 from its measurement parameters.  It has a tendency to narrow less than that of Ta41 from its widest point near the apex towards the basal section, thus barely showing the sinuosity of the lateral edge that is quite clear on Ta41.  However, this character is quite difficult to use in practice and there is some overlap between the species in any case.

Notes

The identification as T. impressa is by B.J. Selman (pers. Comm, 1979), from a very typical Boonoo Boonoo female specimen.  There is a little doubt in my mind about this identification as the type specimen of T. impressa seems to exhibit a somewhat different kind of elytral sculpture to what is typical of Ta07.  See also notes for T. papulenta.

The sex ratio is strongly female-biased, with only 10 of 44 specimens examined being male (p<0.001 using an exact binomial test).

Ta07 is almost invariably recorded from Eucalyptus species of the stringybark group (Eucalyptus : Capillulus).  In this it is different to Ta41, where stringybarks make up just 5% of the recorded hosts.

The large size, highly convex, almost hemispherical, shape (height/length =0.49-0.56), along with the rugged sculpture of the elytra makes females of this species relatively easy to identify.  The males are much smaller than the females, with the size distributions barely overlapping, and can be rather difficult to distinguish from those of Ta41.
