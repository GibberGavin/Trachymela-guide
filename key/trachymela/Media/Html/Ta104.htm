Ta104

Name

Trachymela sp. Ta104

Reference specimen

1998-0674a, 60km S of Charters Towers, N.E. Queensland

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 6.5-7.7 mm (9), ♂ 5.8-7.2 mm [16].

Colour:  head: light- to mid-brown, usually with a pair of black patches behind fronto-clypeal suture, one on each side between eye and central suture that is variable in size, usually small and oval-shaped, rarely larger, never merging across central suture, rarely completely absent, dark-brown or black-tipped mandibles; antennomeres uniformly straw-brown; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, with two black patches usually present on each side roughly between eye and humeral callus, the inner patch is thin and situated along lateral edge of disc, the outer is broader, often “C”-shaped with open side of “C” facing outwards, sometimes the inner patch is either absent or only partially present, less often both are completely absent; scutellum: brown, same shade as pronotum sometimes with fractionally darker edges; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with several prominent black or dark-brown areas usually present: (1) along the border of elytral disc and margin (2) from anterior margin between humeral callus and scutellum, extending posteriorly for a short distance, (3) in posterior half of disc, halfway between suture and margin, and (4) a small patch around elytral summit, the black shade in these areas may be confined to elevated features such as verrucae or extend to the surrounding elytral surface, one or more of these (or even all) may be completely absent; wart-like elevations and ridges elsewhere on the elytra never darker than background; punctures are similar to or a fractionally darker shade than interstices, humeral callus same colour as surrounding area, rarely fractionally darker; venter: light- to mid-brown with edges of metaventrites usually slightly darker, legs similar or fractionally paler, inside surface of elytral epipleura brown.  Cuticular wax: mostly of a uniform greyish or light brown colour, usually quite densely and uniformly distributed among verrucae and wrinkles, elevated areas such as verrucae and ridges tend to be wax-free.

Head; punctures moderately large (pd~1.5-2xef) and slightly unevenly and moderately densely packed. Antennae short, particularly in females (AL ♂=38-43%BL, ♀=31-35%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression shallow and confined to anterior half, foveal elevation small, round and clearly elevated, discal area with surface somewhat uneven and puncturation clearly impressed, the largest fractionally larger (rarely similar) than those on head, slightly unevenly distributed and moderately dense (spacing~2-3pd), becoming much more coarse at lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a very smoothly and broadly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially straight before curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, broadest a little in front of posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; punctured, with punctures well defined, evenly or slightly unevenly distributed.  Elytra; longitudinal curvature even until close to apex where it increases slightly, punctures relatively large, closely spaced and evenly distributed over anterior two-third of surface, surface more granular in posterior third, with very fine punctures scattered on the interstices, many (less often fewer) elevated interstices are scattered over surface and frequently form short, thin ridges placed transversely and (less often) longitudinally, the most prominent of these is a transverse partially interrupted ridge just behind antero-median depression and extending from elytral summit to middle of edge of disc, a few to many small elevated, isolated verrucae are present primarily posterior to the central ridge, often extended into short transverse ridges, marginal sulcus very weak or absent, surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.37-0.41).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel, lightly closed anteriorly, a sparse scatter of setae along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on anterior, median and posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, slightly rounded; inner edge of posterior of epipleura lacking setae.  Aedeagus; moderate in length (LPE=30-36%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided for most of length from basal sulcus, becoming fractionally wider at ostium from where sides converge towards apex in a smooth arc, dorsal surface with well-defined dorso-median channel and dorsolateral channels resulting in two long ostial flaps protruding far towards apex in the tear-shaped ostial depression; tip small and smoothly triangular; in lateral view, curved through about 45⁰ near basal sulcus then ventral surface almost straight to apex, dorsal surface straight for about half this distance, then converging evenly towards ventral surface, tip deflexed.

Similar species

Ta10 – this is one of a group of species whose size, colouration and the presence of a transverse weal behind the antero-median depression can make them difficult to distinguish.  See Ta10 for a discussion of the differences within this group.

Ta112 – this species overlaps in distribution with that of Ta104, and specimens of both have been found at the same location in southern Queensland.  The aedeagus has the same “ovate ostium” form as that of Ta104, but its size is proportionately considerable larger.  The central transverse ridge in Ta112 is more poorly developed than is the case for almost all specimens of Ta104, with it being hardly differentiated from the other many short transverse ridges on the elytral disc.

Notes

Specimens from Western Queensland and the Northern Territory show much reduced black colouration compared with specimens from North-eastern Queensland.  The dark patch at base of elytra between humeral callus and scutellum is usually present, but most of the others are very reduced in size and intensity or absent completely.  In rare instances, the specimen is uniformly brown dorsally.  However, the external appearance and dimensions of the aedeagi of these specimens appear very similar.

Ta104 seems to have a wide distribution in the drier parts of the country, west of the Great Dividing Range.  It has been collected from the ironbark, Eucalyptus persistens, and box-barked species, including E. populnea, in Queensland (Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria).  One specimen from Charleville was obtained from E. camaldulensis (Symphyomyrtus: Exsertaria).  The Northern Territory specimens were collected from E. gamophylla, a eucalypt in the subgenus Eudesmia.
