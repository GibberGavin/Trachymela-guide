Name

Trachymela sp. Ta106

Reference specimen

2004-754, Jindabyne, S.E. New South Wales.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.4-7.9 mm (2), ♂ 6.8-7.4 mm (5).

Colour:  head: reddish-brown to dark-brown, clypeus often partially or even fully black, if not black, clypeal suture is often black and antennal insertions are often black, rear of head black but visible only with head slightly extended, labrum yellow-brown, mandibles black; antennomeres 1-3 uniformly light brown, 4-7 grading towards black, with darker shade sometimes confined to distal ends, 8-11 black; pronotum: uniformly brown, same shade as frons of head, often with lateral edge darker or black; scutellum: black;  elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, marginal area a slightly darker shade of brown, with small black, raised tubercles scattered quite sparsely and unevenly over much of surface of disc except for the antero-median depression, tubercles are not aligned into longitudinal series, but sometimes a number of them just behind antero-median suture line up very loosely into a transverse row, punctures are similar in shade to interstices, humeral callus black; venter: head, prosternum and central regions of  thoracic ventrites black, legs usually black, less often dark reddish-brown with black patches, abdomen dark reddish-brown, inside surface of elytral epipleura black.  Waxy dorsal covering: moderately dense and evenly distributed cover of light brown.

Shape: almost evenly rounded longitudinally over most of elytral surface, with outline disjunct at intersection of pronotum and elytra, relatively flat (H/L=39-46%BL), greatest height clearly in front of middle of elytral margin; Head; punctures moderately sized (pd~2-2.5xef), evenly and densely packed. Antennae moderately long (AL ♂=43-47%BL, ♀=36-39%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely with lateral margins noticeably explanate, particularly in males, foveal depression very shallow, an almost round slightly elevated area that is often not very compact situated behind each eye, puncturation clearly impressed, slightly more coarse than those on head and usually very evenly distributed in discal area, rarely tending to slightly acervate, becoming noticeably coarser from foveal depression to lateral margins with surface between punctures often more coarsely textured near margins, interstices smooth to slightly rough in discal area, never so rough as to obscure punctures.  Thickened front margins smoothly join thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin almost straight or barely curving about 30% of length towards rear margin, then curving more strongly towards posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; smooth or finely wrinkled, unpunctured.  Elytra; surface evenly curved transversely over discal region, sometimes slightly explanate at margins, a shallow depression extends across surface just in front of middle, the surface just posterior to depression is elevated in a shallow ridge extending from near suture to lateral edge, surface appears reticulated due to the prominent raised interstices running longitudinally and laterally, punctures set deep among these but quite evenly distributed over the surface, small round smooth verrucae, sometimes with a very fine puncture in centre, are scattered unevenly but well separated over much of surface except in anterio-median depression, verrucae just posterior to antero-median depression sometimes form a very rough transverse series, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.32-0.35).  Venter; Prosternal process narrowest anteriorly then widening gradually, lightly closed anteriorly, short setae very sparsely distributed over prosternal process and mesosternum, a single long seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; moderately long (LPE=28-33%BL), in dorsal view, sides slightly sinuous, converging fractionally from basal sulcus to middle, the flaring out slightly to the widest point at or a little posterior to ostial opening (WPE = 0.24-0.26*LPE), from where sides converge smoothly towards apex, with sometimes a slight discontinuity in this curvature near the middle, tip triangular, dorsal surface very lightly sclerotized, dorso-median channel present but usually poorly defined, dorso-lateral channels relatively long resulting in dorsal flaps that are long but not reaching anterior of ostial opening, ostium relatively large; in lateral view, quite evenly and continually curved over whole length, tip deflexed.

Similar species

 Ta81 – this species occurs in the same area as Ta106.  Its elytral surface is textured very similarly to that of Ta106, and both species have small verrucae scattered over it in a similar arrangement.  It differs from Ta106 by being smaller, having a light brown rather than black ventral surface (including the epipleura), and a narrow (HCE <= 0.3) and noticeably sinuate lateral elytral margin.

 Ta201 – another species that has similarly textured elytra and a scatter of small, black verrucae.  It differs by the lack of black elytral epipleura, straight elytral lateral margins and an aedeagus that is very similar in shape but flares out much more around its widest point.

 Ta101 – a species that occurs in the northern tablelands of New South Wales and is superficially like Ta106, though the aedeagus is very different in shape.

Notes

This is a relatively rarely encountered species from the highlands of southern New South Wales and eastern Victoria.  The only definitive host record is from the snow gum, Eucalyptus pauciflora.
