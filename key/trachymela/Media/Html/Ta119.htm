Distribution

Ta119

Name

Trachymela sp. Ta119

Reference specimen

2004-183, Streaky Bay, Eyre Peninsula, South Australia.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 4.9-6.2 mm, ♂ 4.8-5.7 mm.

Colour:  head: uniformly light- to mid-brown with (rarely) some fuscous colour around fronto-clypeal suture, dark-brown or black-tipped mandibles; labrum usually straw-brown; antennomeres straw-brown, usually uniformly so, rarely with distal antennomeres fractionally darker; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, sometimes with broad band along anterior edge between eyes a darker brown shade, often with a dark-brown or even black spot present behind each eye, very rarely the spot is more extensive and takes up much of area of foveal depression; scutellum: brown, same shade as pronotum, often broadly edged in a slightly darker shade; elytra: brown, similar or fractionally darker shade as pronotum, almost uniformly so with sometimes the marginal area slightly paler (Eyre Peninsula specimens) or with some dark-brown or (rarely) black patches present (Melrose/Quorn specimens) that include all or some of (1) area between humeral callus, scutellum and elytral summit, (2) elevated ridges or pustules anterior to antero-median depression or (3) less commonly, elevated pustules posterior to depression, punctures are a fractionally darker shade than interstices; humeral callus same colour as surrounds, or (in many Melrose/Quorn specimens) partially or fully dark-brown or black; venter: usually light- to mid-brown with legs of a similar shade, thoracic ventrites sometimes slightly or (Melrose/Quorn specimens) considerably darker, inside surface of elytral epipleura brown.  Cuticular wax: very unevenly distributed layer of mostly light-brown, dust on head, pronotum and in the scutellar corner of elytra is often a slightly paler shade, sometimes almost grey.

Shape: very evenly and strongly rounded (height=41-49%BL), greatest height well in front of middle of elytral margin.  Head; punctures moderately large (pd~1.5-2xef) and quite evenly and quite densely distributed.  Antennae short, particularly in females (AL ♂=37-39%BL, ♀=32-35%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent or exceedingly shallow with epidermis usually elevated in a rounded pustule near its centre though it may be hard to discern if not darker than surrounds, discal area with surface usually smooth, rarely slightly uneven, puncturation clearly impressed, evenly distributed and quite dense (spacing~1.5-2.5pd), becoming much coarser at lateral margins, some much finer punctures are scattered among the larger ones.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially and briefly straight before curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; sparsely punctured throughout, with punctures similar or slightly finer than those on adjacent area of pronotum, rarely smooth and unpunctured.  Elytra; surface quite evenly curved transversely with lateral margin noticeably explanate, antero-median depression absent or barely discernible, punctures mostly distinct and relatively large, closely spaced and quite evenly distributed over anterior two-third of surface, surface more granular in posterior third, sometimes interstices rougher and make punctures more difficult to discern, much smaller punctures scattered on the interstices, the disc is scattered with small to very small, wart-like elevations that are sometimes barely differentiated from the raised interstices and are quite evenly distributed with no concentration behind depression and no alignment in longitudinal series; surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura large (HCE/PW=0.40-0.43).  Venter; prosternal process with sides almost parallel, open or (less often) lightly closed anteriorly, a sparse scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single seta each on anterior, median and posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, slightly rounded.  Aedeagus; quite short (LPE=27-31%BL), in dorsal view, sides smoothly sinuate, narrowing towards anterior or parallel-sided from basal sulcus  to about middle, then widening (barely so in eastern specimens) to widest point near anterior of ostial opening, from where sides converge in a smooth arc towards apex, dorsal surface smoothly curved with dorso-median channel absent, dorsolateral channels well-defined and converging slightly towards rear, tip protruding only slightly, ostium relatively large with dorsal flaps long, extending to just past tip; in lateral view, bent very slightly near basal sulcus, then ventral surface almost imperceptibly and evenly curved towards apex before bending downwards noticeably at about 15% of distance from apex, tip small and slightly deflexed.

Similar species

Ta161 – a species that is of the same size as Ta119, with very similar shaped and sized aedeagus.  Both species occur on mallees in the Adnataria group.  The main distinction is that Ta119 lacks dorsal dark markings and the thoracic ventrites of this species are rarely darker than the rest of the ventral surface – however, these traits are often quite variable in Trachymela.  It is therefore quite likely that Ta119 and Ta161 are the same species.

Ta120 – this is a species whose geographic distribution overlaps that of Ta119 and is similar in both size and shape.  Ta120 almost invariably has the humeral callus and the adjoining elytral surface just above it black.  There are several other species that are similar to Ta120 that could also be mistaken for Ta119 (such as Ta159 and Ta174) – all are generally somewhat darker than Ta119.  These species are all easily separable on aedeagus shape.

Notes

Specimens collected east of the Eyre Peninsula (Melrose and Quorn) differ from Eyre Peninsula specimens in their possession of more extensive black or dark-brown areas on the pronotum and elytra, though this is quite variable in these specimens.  The two populations also differ slightly in the shape of the aedeagus, with it being much more noticeably widened around the ostium in Eyre Peninsula specimens.

Individuals of the eastern populations have been collected from Eucalyptus odorata (Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria).  No host records are available for the Eyre Peninsula specimens.
