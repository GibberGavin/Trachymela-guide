Ta12

Name

Trachymela sloanei (Blackburn)

Reference specimen

2001-089a, Narrandera, Southern NSW

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.9-8.9 mm, ♂ (6.9)7.3-8.4 mm.

Colour:  head uniformly mid-brown with black-tipped mandibles, a black area sometimes extends forward a small distance from centre rear; labrum usually paler; antennomeres 1-6 light to mid-brown, 7-11 similar or more commonly  slightly darker; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, almost always with two dark-brown to black, roughly longitudinal, irregular patches on each side behind the eye, the outer patches sometimes split into two; scutellum: brown, same or fractionally lighter shade as pronotum;  elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with many small similar-sized dark brown or black patches scattered quite evenly over almost the whole surface, less often the distribution of these patches is less even and/or the size of the patches less uniform, in a few specimens  some patches align either longitudinally or more rarely, transversely, to form rough bands of dark colour, punctures are fractionally darker than interstices, humeral callus is dark-brown or black; venter: mid- to dark brown tending lighter-coloured towards the sides, with legs usually a fractionally lighter shade, inside surface of elytral epipleura brown.  Cuticular wax: moderate layer of grey to grey-brown or orange-brown that rarely conceals the dark verrucae, sometimes two slightly distinct shades are present; in some specimens 3 or 4 faint, interrupted lines of wax may be discernable in the posterior half of the elytra.

Shape: rather flattened, greatest height viewed laterally is in posterior half; Head; punctures moderately sized (pd~1.5-2xef) and quite evenly and densely packed, sometimes partially obliterated by rugose interstices. Antennae short (AL ♂=37-39%BL, ♀=34-37%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression broad but very shallow with a clearly defined and almost round elevated area in middle, puncturation clearly impressed and quite evenly densely distributed in discal area, becoming coarser from foveal depression to lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; variable, punctures usually similar in size and density to those on adjacent area on pronotum, sometimes more lightly punctured posteriorly, rarely completely unpunctured, surface may be rough, frequently with a faint longitudinal central ridge.  Elytra; surface evenly curved transversely, lightly curved longitudinally in anterior half, then more strongly curved, punctures large and distinct and relatively evenly distributed, with much smaller punctures scattered sparingly on the interstices, small areas of thickened, elevated, black interstices are scattered fairly evenly over surface, surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.33-0.37).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, lightly closed (rarely open) anteriorly, short setae sparsely distributed over prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; moderate-sized (LPE=30-34%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided for most of length, sides converge towards apex in a smooth to slightly uneven arc, dorsal surface quite membranous with faint to well-defined dorso-median channel present, short dorsolateral channels present adjacent to ostium; tip protruding and smoothly rounded, ostium short and transversely, posterior surface in the form of a triangle with apex at centre; in lateral view, almost evenly curved throughout apical and central section with a more pronounced bend just in front of basal section, thinning towards apex over 15% of length, tip deflexed, flagellum very thin, almost invariably broken off.

Similar species

Ta84 – this central Australian species is remarkably similar in the markings on the prothorax and the sculpture of the elytra, though the distribution of verrucae on the elytra is a little less dense.  There is little difference in the aedeagus between the two species, that of Ta84 being a little narrower anteriorly compared to that of Ta12. (see Notes)

Ta51 - this species is superficially similar but differs by the absence of the longitudinal black patches on the pronotum and the different form and distribution of the elytral verrucae, these being more discrete in Ta51.  Ta51 has a northern Australian distribution, not overlapping at all with that of Ta12.

Notes

The identification of this species is from B.J. Selman.  This seems to be a very variable species.  Even the specimens from one location (Hay) differ considerably among themselves in size, shape, colouration (from light to dark brown) and density of verrucae.  T. sloanei is distributed in Victoria and southern NSW on eucalypts of the box group, as well as the river red gum (Eucalyptus camaldulensis).  The species is very similar to Ta84.

Three very similar species described by Blackburn (1896) are T. sloanei (Ta12), T. interioris (Ta84) and T. grossa (Ta31).  The first two of these are mostly found on Eucalyptus camaldulensis, while the latter seems to be mostly on E. tereticornis, a closely related species in the red-gum group.  There are small differences in several characters between these species, but the aedeagus is very similar in all three.  From current data, the three species seem to be allopatric, but a more detailed look at museum collections may reveal connections between these populations, particularly since E. camaldulensis is found over much of inland Australia and E. tereticornis is present along most of the eastern coastal areas.  It is thus quite likely that these are geographic varieties of a single species.
