2004-393, Bordertown, South Australia.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.0-7.3 mm (2), ♂ 7.0-7.4 mm (4).

Colour:  head: very dark reddish-brown, with clypeus and area adjacent to front of eyes often black, sometimes black extending towards rear of frons, labrum often at least partially lighter brown, mandibles black; antennomeres 1-3 light brown, 4-6 grading darker brown, 7-11 dark-brown, rarely black; pronotum: dark brown, similar shade or fractionally darker than rear of head with anterior margin behind head sometimes slightly darker, lateral edge and sometimes all or part of anterior edge black; scutellum: very dark brown, similar as adjacent area of pronotum, rimmed with a darker shade, less often completely black; elytra: very dark brown, a slightly darker shade as disc of pronotum, or black, marginal area sometimes slightly paler, 4-5 fine longitudinal lines of a slightly paler brown are present on disc and extend almost its full length, punctures similar in shade to interstices, humeral callus black; venter: dark-brown, with areas around the midline of thoracic segments often even darker, legs similar, inside surface of elytral epipleura black.  Waxy dorsal covering: light-brown, head and pronotum with a moderately thick and quite even cover, cover on elytra sparser, fractionally more dense along 4 or 5 thin longitudinal lines with wax arranged as very small dots along those lines (small verrucae form the dividers between dots).

Shape: evenly rounded from scutellum to a little past elytral summit, where curvature increases slightly, strongly convex (H/BL ♂=0.45-0.47, ♀=0.47-0.50), highest point somewhat variable, from some distance in front of to just behind middle of elytral margin (PGH ♂=54-58%BL, ♀=50-54%BL).  Head; punctures fine (pd~1.5-2.xef) and quite evenly and densely packed.  Antennae moderately long (AL ♂=35-38%BL, ♀=33-34%BL), A4~A3, filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent or present only as a small depression in surface near anterior margin just behind inner edge of eye, surface of marginal area noticeably elevated, punctures on disc clearly impressed, similar in size to those on head, closely and rather evenly packed together, punctures becoming considerably coarser from inside margin of foveal area towards lateral margin.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially either almost straight for about 30% of length or very gently curved, then curving smoothly and gradually towards the much thicker rear margin.  Scutellum; punctured, with punctures usually smaller than those on pronotal disc and almost always much less clearly impressed.  Elytra; surface evenly curved, punctures very poorly defined over almost whole of surface, lost in the closely-spaced granular interstices that form a network of short thin ridges over whole of elytral surface, a scatter of very fine punctures on the interstices especially around anterior half of elytral suture and on lateral margins, 4-5 longitudinal series of very small and inconspicuous, round verrucae are present on disc, distinguishable from surrounding interstices only by being placed within a thin row of slightly smoother (and paler coloured) interstices, surface slightly discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.38-0.40).  Venter; Prosternal process narrowest anteriorly, closed anteriorly, short setae present sparsely over prosternal process and mesosternum, one long seta on anterior and median trochanter, one on posterior trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; long (LPE=36-38%BL); in dorsal view, sides very slightly sinuous, almost parallel-sided from basal sulcus to nearly 50% of distance to apex where sides diverge almost imperceptibly to the widest point just a little behind anterior of ostium, from there the sides converge towards apex in an almost even and gradual arc, dorsal surface smoothly curved with a faint but relatively long dorso-median channel, dorsolateral channels long and at lateral margins forming long dorsal flaps, ostial opening very large, spatula moderately large, tip triangular; in lateral view, bent at basal sulcus, then very gently and evenly curved from there to just before apex where curvature increases fractionally, tip deflexed.

Similar species

 Ta107 – this species is exceedingly close to Ta123 and they can be exceedingly difficult to tell apart.  The longitudinal lines of more concentrated cuticular wax on the elytra (and the corresponding pale lines on the disc when the wax has been removed) is invariably present in Ta123 but may also be present on some specimens of Ta107.  The aedeagi of the two species are quite different.  Ta107 also has slightly longer antennae in both sexes as well as having a larger inter-antennal spacing.

 Ta124 – another species that looks almost identical in external morphology to Ta123, having the same pattern of a network of raised interstices on the elytra.  However, Ta124 is larger and lacks the fine lines of cuticular wax (or smoother interstices).

Notes

Ta123 is one of the alta-group of species that are characterized by their relatively large size and strongly convex lateral outline, as well as the very dense puncturation on the elytra.  Some of the members of the group, especially Ta107, Ta123 and to some extent, Ta139, are particularly close in external morphology and females can be difficult to distinguish.  A complicating factor is that closely related species of Trachymela are often found together in the same location and even on the same host plant.  It is therefore possible that females of Ta123 and Ta107 in particular may be mixed up in their current groupings.  The presence of the fine lines of tiny verrucae separated by slightly paler interstices (and the concentration of cuticular wax along those lines) is considered diagnostic of Ta123 within the alta species group. 

Only a single specimen of Ta123 has had its host definitively recorded, and it was found on E. arenacea (Eucalyptus : Capillulus).
