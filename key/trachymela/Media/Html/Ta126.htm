Name

Trachymela sp. Ta126

Reference specimen

2007-092, Boulia, Western Queensland.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 5.8-7.6 mm [43], ♂ 5.4-6.7 mm [40].

Colour:  head mid-brown, most commonly uniformly so, sometimes with two black, often slightly diffuse patches on frons, one on each side of centre,  that may be small or occupy much of space between eyes and central suture, rarely these are joined to form a single large patch, mandibles with black tips, labrum yellow-brown; antennomeres 1-4 straw-brown, 5-11 similar or fractionally to slightly darker, sometimes with distal extremes even darker; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, often with a thin dark-brown to black, longitudinal, irregular patch extending from anterior edge near inside edge of eye towards posterior but only rarely reaching posterior edge, in a few specimens these are connected by oblique dark lines resulting in an “M”-shaped mark with its base on the posterior margin,  a round black mark is sometimes present in centre behind outside edge of each eye, lateral marginal area often of a paler shade than disc; scutellum: dark brown to very dark brown or even black, broadly bordered in darker brown;  elytra: brown, same shade as disc of pronotum, a short black ridge or line of dark-brown or black pustules (VR3) extends posteriorly in line with black line on pronotum, and often continues as a line of more widely spaced dark-brown pustules towards apex,  other small, dark pustules, many with fine central punctures, are scattered over elytral disc, some aligned into longitudinal series, others scattered more randomly, a higher density of punctures is sometimes present just posterior to antero-median depression where some transverse wrinkles may present also, less often verrucae are only fractionally darker than surrounds and elytra appear almost uniformly brown, punctures similar or fractionally darker than interstices, humeral callus slightly or considerably darker than surrounds, sometimes black; venter: mid-brown, with thoracic ventrites sometimes fully or partially darker, legs often of a slightly paler shade, inside surface of elytral epipleura variable, sometimes completely brown, more often at least partially black, often with inside surface black outer margins brown.  Cuticular wax: somewhat variable, most often a moderately thick to quite thick and relatively evenly distributed layer whose colour can vary from light greyish-brown to mid-brown, the colour of the wax sometimes tending paler in shade along the anterior margins of elytra, sometimes dotted lines of much paler wax are present along some verrucal series, usually VR2 and VR4, greyish wax is often present at elytral base, near scutellum.

Shape: quite evenly but not very strongly rounded (height=39-47%BL), greatest height viewed laterally well in front of middle of elytral margin, dorsal surface most often almost mostly evenly rounded with curvature increasing noticeably only near apex, sometimes a slight gibbosity evident around elytral summit.  Head; punctures quite small (pd~1.5-2xef) and relatively evenly and densely packed, sometimes surface wrinkled and punctures partially obliterated. Antennae short (AL ♂=34-38%BL, ♀=29-33%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, sometimes with extreme lateral margin slightly to moderately explanate, foveal depression shallow and confined to anterior half of pronotum, with a round, smooth elevated area centred on pronotum directly behind middle of eye, discal area very slightly rugulose, sometimes bounded laterally by slightly raised, smooth ridges, a similar ridge often present on centre line, punctures clearly impressed and quite evenly and densely distributed in discal area, size fractionally larger to those on head, much finer punctures scattered among them, becoming coarser from foveal depression to lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and very gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; punctured, punctures similar to or fractionally smaller than those on adjacent area of pronotum, or, more rarely, unpunctured.  Elytra; surface evenly curved transversely, punctures moderate in size, distinct and relatively evenly distributed over anterior two-thirds of disc, with much smaller punctures scattered on the interstices, punctures tend to be larger and more widely spaced in marginal region, a small, smooth, elevated area present around elytral summit, small, smooth, elevated and mostly inconspicuous verrucae, some with small central punctures, are scattered lightly over elytral surface (see colour above for arrangement), surface continuous under humeral callus, vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.36-0.41).  Venter; Prosternal process narrowest anteriorly to almost parallel-sided, open anteriorly, a scatter of very short setae sparsely distributed over prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, rounded; inner edge of posterior of epipleurae lacking setae.  Aedeagus; moderate-sized (LPE=30-35%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided for most of length from basal sulcus, then broadening very slightly to around middle of ostial depression from where sides converge towards apex in a mostly smooth arc, dorsal surface with well-defined dorso-median channel and dorsolateral channels resulting in two long ostial dorsal processes protruding far towards apex in the tear-shaped ostial depression; tip small and smoothly triangular; in lateral view, curved smoothly through about 45⁰ around basal sulcus then ventral surface very lighly curved almost to apex to apex, where the curvature increases slightly, dorsal surface parallel to ventral until about 20% of distance from apex, then converging evenly towards ventral surface, tip deflexed.

Similar species

Ta87 – this is a species whose distribution overlaps that of Ta126 over quite a large area in the areas west of the Great Dividing Range.  The two species can be rather difficult to distinguish on external morphological characters.  The most convenient way to tell them apart is in the width of the elytral marginal area – the values of HCE/BL of Ta87 are always below 20, while those of Ta126 always exceed that value.  The aedeagus of the two species, though both of the “ovate ostium” type, is quite different, with that of Ta87 very flat and broad over its apical section.

Ta232 – of similar size and appearance, this species is found in similar habitats as Ta126 (sometimes together in the same location, with both species feeding on box-type eucalypts).  The inner surface of the epipleura is largely brown in specimens of Ta232 and the verrucae in series VR3 and to a lesser extent VR5 are very closely spaced, often merging to form an interrupted ridge.  VR4, VR6 and VR7 (and sometimes VR8) have quite prominent white streaks of wax between the verrucae that are absent or much more poorly developed in specimens of Ta126.  The aedeagus of these two species, though similar in size, is of a different form.

Ta113 – this species shares with Ta126 the flat common area around the elytral suture, though it is better defined and a contrasting black in that species.  Ta113 also differs in the rougher texture of its elytral surface and the more prominent verrucae, though these occur at a smaller density.

Notes

Ta126, as defined here, is a highly variable entity that may represent a species complex, though a reliable method for separating distinct taxa remains elusive. An initial separation based on the colour of the inner epipleural surface conflicted with other variable characters, particularly aedeagus length. Another potential character evaluated was the shape of the ostial depression, specifically its length-to-width ratio. This ratio ranges from 1.5–1.9 across specimens and was generally lower in individuals with black epipleura, though many exceptions were observed.

The chart of  LPE/BL vs BL suggests that specimens with values exceeding 33.5% form a distinct grouping. Although both measured Victorian specimens fall into this cluster, other high-value specimens occur in western Queensland, often co-occurring with individuals in the lower cluster. Further analysis incorporating host-plant utilization data may help clarify these patterns. Nevertheless, the Victorian specimens appear most distinct, possessing an aedeagus both longer and broader than that of equivalent-sized specimens from elsewhere as well as possessing a band of greyish cuticular wax along the lateral margins of the elytral disc that is absent or much less pronounced in typical specimens of Ta126.

Specimens from far western Queensland are generally paler dorsally than those from more easterly and southerly locations, always with unpigmented epipleura, though aedeagal morphology varies even within these populations. The single Birdsville male possesses an aedeagus with a slightly broader, more gradually curved apex and an ostium less posteriorly extended than in Boulia specimens. Winton specimens are darker dorsally—with some exhibiting limited black pigment on the epipleura—yet their aedeagal morphology is otherwise identical to western populations. Specimens from Richmond are relatively pale dorsally; the single male examined has an ostial depression that is slightly extended posteriorly, though well within the normal range of variation across the species' broader geographic distribution.

Two male specimens from Charleville have most of their verrucae in prominent longitudinal series, their aedeagus lengths are towards the high end of the range, and they were collected from Eucalyptus camaldulensis.  However, in other characters, they fit within the range of variation of Ta126.

Almost all specimens of Ta126 have been found on boxes (Eucalyptus section Adnataria), with most records from E. coolabah (62).  Other records are from E. normantonensis (4), E. microcarpa (4), and E. populnea (2).  The only identified host that is not Adnataria are for the two specimens from E. camaldulensis (Eucalyptus section Exsertaria) mentioned in the previous paragraph.

Three female specimens from a single location near Cobar, NSW are larger than typical Ta126 and are more uniformly brown dorsally, with VR3 much less prominent than is typical in Ta126.  However, in all other respects they fit well within the range of variation of this species.

A teneral male specimen from Plevna Downs [QM] has slightly low values of A-A and LPE but otherwise is a very typical Ta126 in morphology and morphometrics.
