Name

Trachymela sp. Ta126

Reference specimen

2007-092, Boulia, Western Queensland.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 5.8-7.4 mm, ♂ 5.3-6.7 mm.

Colour:  head mid-brown, most commonly uniformly so, sometimes with two black, often slightly diffuse patches on frons, one on each side of centre,  that may be small or occupy much of space between eyes and central suture, rarely these are joined to form a single large patch, mandibles with black tips, labrum yellow-brown; antennomeres 1-4 straw-brown, 5-11 similar or fractionally to slightly darker, sometimes with distal extremes even darker; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, often with a thin dark-brown to black, longitudinal, irregular patch extending from anterior edge near inside edge of eye towards posterior but only rarely reaching posterior edge,  less commonly, a round black mark is present in centre behind outside edge of each eye, lateral marginal area often of a paler shade than disc; scutellum: dark brown, similar shade as adjacent area of pronotum, broadly bordered in darker brown;  elytra: brown, same shade as disc of pronotum, a short black ridge or line of dark-brown or black pustules (VR3) extends posteriorly in line with black line on pronotum, and often continues as a line of more widely spaced dark-brown pustules towards apex,  other small, dark pustules, many with fine central punctures, are scattered over elytral disc, some aligned into longitudinal series, others scattered more randomly, a higher density of punctures is sometimes present just posterior to antero-median depression where some transverse wrinkles may present also, less often verrucae are only fractionally darker than surrounds and elytra appear almost uniformly brown, punctures similar or fractionally darker than interstices, humeral callus usually slightly darker in colour than surrounds, rarely black; venter: mid-brown, with thoracic ventrites sometimes fully or partially darker, legs often of a slightly paler shade, inside surface of elytral epipleura brown, rarely partially black.  Cuticular wax: somewhat variable, most often a moderately thick to quite thick and relatively evenly distributed layer whose colour can vary from light greyish-brown to mid-brown, the colour of the wax sometimes tending paler in shade along the anterior margins of elytra, sometimes dotted lines of much paler wax are present along some verrucal series, usually VR2 and VR4.

Shape: rather flattened (height=39-47%BL), greatest height viewed laterally well in front of middle of elytral margin, dorsal surface most often almost mostly evenly rounded with curvature increasing noticeably only near apex, sometimes a slight gibbosity evident around elytral summit.  Head; punctures quite small (pd~1.5-2xef) and relatively evenly and densely packed, sometimes surface wrinkled and punctures partially obliterated. Antennae short (AL ♂=34-38%BL, ♀=29-32%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, sometimes with extreme lateral margin slightly to moderately explanate, foveal depression shallow and confined to anterior half of pronotum, with a round, smooth elevated area centred on pronotum directly behind middle of eye, discal area very slightly rugulose, sometimes bounded laterally by slightly raised, smooth ridges, a similar ridge often present on centre line, punctures clearly impressed and quite evenly and densely distributed in discal area, size fractionally larger to those on head, much finer punctures scattered among them, becoming coarser from foveal depression to lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and very gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; punctured, punctures fractionally similar to or smaller than those on adjacent area of pronotum, or, more rarely, unpunctured.  Elytra; surface evenly curved transversely, punctures moderate in size, distinct and relatively evenly distributed over anterior two-thirds of disc, with much smaller punctures scattered on the interstices, punctures tend to be larger and more widely spaced in marginal region, a small, smooth, elevated area present around elytral summit, small, smooth, elevated and mostly inconspicuous verrucae, some with small central punctures, are scattered lightly over elytral surface (see colour above for arrangement), surface continuous under humeral callus, vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.38-0.41).  Venter; Prosternal process narrowest anteriorly to almost parallel-sided, open anteriorly, a scatter of very short setae sparsely distributed over prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, rounded.  Aedeagus; moderate-sized (LPE=30-33%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided for most of length from basal sulcus, then broadening very slightly to around middle of ostial depression from where sides converge towards apex in a mostly smooth arc, dorsal surface with well-defined dorso-median channel and dorsolateral channels resulting in two long ostial flaps protruding far towards apex in the tear-shaped ostial depression; tip small and smoothly triangular; in lateral view, curved smoothly through about 45⁰ around basal sulcus then ventral surface very lighly curved almost to apex to apex, where the curvature increases slightly, dorsal surface parallel to ventral until about 20% of distance from apex, then converging evenly towards ventral surface, tip deflexed.

Similar species

Ta135 – This species is darker dorsally and ventrally than Ta126 and has black inner epipleural surfaces, but is otherwise difficult to distinguish from it.  Note however, that at least partially black inner epipleura are sometimes present in specimens of Ta126 (for example, [2021-283b] and [2021-288c]).

Ta113 – this species shares with Ta126 the flat common area around the elytral suture, though it is better defined and a contrasting black in that species.  Ta113 also differs in the rougher texture of its elytral surface and the more prominent verrucae, though these occur at a smaller density.

Notes

Ta126 is most frequently found on Eucalyptus coolabah (Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria), but a number of specimens were collected from E. normantonensis (also Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria) near Winton.  Large numbers were found at two locations in far western Queensland late in the summer.  Males outnumber females by a considerable number at most of the locations and times specimens were collected.

Three female specimens from a single location near Cobar, NSW ([2022-053], etc) are larger than typical Ta126 and are more uniformly brown dorsally, with VR3 much less prominent than is typical in Ta126.  However, in all other respects they fit well within the range of variation of this species.

Specimens from far western Queensland tend to be paler in colour than those from more easterly locations.  However, even within these populations, there is variation in aedeagus shape.  The Birdsville specimen has an aedeagus that is slightly broader and more gradually curved at its apex, while its ostium is not extended as far back posteriorly as the Boulia specimens.  The specimens from Winton are somewhat darker dorsally than the specimens from further west, and some have some limited black colour on the epipleura, but the aedeagus is essentially identical in all characters to the western populations.  The specimens from Richmond, Qld are relatively pale dorsally but the aedeagus in the single male has an ostium that is somewhat extended posteriorly, though not to the extent that is typical of Ta135.

[2004-140] (Windorah) is somewhat problematical and may not be either Ta126 or Ta135 – measure cf with Charleville Ta135 specimen.

There is some diversity in both antennal lengths and LPE/BL ratio that may indicate the presence of two species in this grouping.
