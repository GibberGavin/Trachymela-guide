Distribution

Ta137

Name

Trachymela sp. Ta137

Reference specimen

25-052723, Eden Creek Falls, Toonumbar, N.E. New South Wales.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 9.32 mm (1), ♂ ? mm.

Colour:  head: uniformly dark-brown, with fronto-clypeal suture narrowly darker brown, almost black at lateral extremes, labrum straw-brown, mandibles brown with black tips; antennomeres 1-3 light-brown, 4-5 grading from light-brown to black, 6-11 black; pronotum: discal area dark-brown, considerably darker than head, foveal region and marginal region almost to lateral edge lighter in shade, similar to that of head, lateral edge darker brown; scutellum: dark-brown, same shade as adjacent region of pronotum; elytra: dark-brown, fractionally darker than disc of pronotum, marginal sulcus slightly lighter in shade, a small patch just anterior to middle of disc fractionally paler, anterior edge black, punctures similar to surrounding interstices, humeral callus concolorous with surrounds; venter: almost uniformly very dark-brown.  Waxy dorsal covering: a concentration of cuticular wax is present in the marginal sulcus of elytra, otherwise distribution, density and colour are unknown.

Shape:  quite evenly rounded viewed laterally, with curvature increasing suddenly just before apex, highest point behind middle of elytral margin, ventrites project a considerable distance below elytral epipleura.  Head; punctures quite large (pd~2xef) and slightly unevenly but moderately densely distributed. Antennae relatively long (AL ♂=?%BL, ♀=36.5%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely over discal area, foveal depression prominent and deep so that margins are prominently explanate, disc smooth with clearly defined and uniformly sized punctures that are almost uniformly distributed, a small unpunctured space present in posterior of centre line of disc, punctures become larger and surface rougher towards margins, with surface roughness obliterating punctures near margins, foveal depression with a small, relatively inconspicuous pustule at centre.  The relatively thick front margins join the thinner lateral margins in a sharply rounded right-angle, almost producing a point, lateral margin usually straight for about 20% of their length, then curving slightly inwards and continuing almost straight and becoming considerably thicker towards posterior margin.  Scutellum; smooth, unpunctured.  Elytra; surface quite rough, with punctures obscured over much of the surface, a broad band along elytral suture from base to just posterior to highest point is relatively smooth, with two prominent longitudinal parallel ridges and punctures more clearly defined, much of surface is studded with prominent well-elevated verrucae, unicolourous with surrounding surface, some with a fine puncture in centre, and many of which are roughly arranged in longitudinal series in posterior half, marginal sulcus well-defined and deep but narrow, lateral margin prominently sinuous, surface strongly discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura very small (HCE/PW=0.26), with inside vertical surface very short in anterior half, almost entirely absent in posterior half.  Venter; Prosternal process narrowest anteriorly, not (or barely) sulcate, short setae present at rear of prosternal process and on mesosternum, a small clump of more than 4 setae on anterior, median and posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; unknown.

Similar species

 Ta20 – a species occurring in the same area that has a vague resemblance to Ta137, but the much “rougher” elytral surface of the latter, and its “mucronate” front angles of the pronotum easily distinguish the species.  Also, the outline of Ta137 (viewed from side) is much more evenly arched than in Ta20.

Notes

One of those species of Trachymela where the “greatest height” of the insect is at or just behind the middle of the elytral margin.  This is a very unusual Trachymela, with its non-sulcate prosternal process, the slightly mucronate front angles of pronotum and the lack of vertical epipleura.  The only specimen known was obtained at M.V. lights, so no host information is available.
