Name

Trachymela squirescensis (Blackburn)

Reference specimen

2003-222b, Charlevue Ck, Blackdown Tablelands, Central Queensland

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.9-8.4 mm, ♂ 7.4-8.3 mm (4)

Colour:  head: very dark reddish-brown, sometimes with black around  and/or near fronto-clypeal suture, labrum yellow-brown, mandibles dark reddish-brown black tips; antennomeres 1-4 light- to mid-brown, 5-11 fractionally to considerably darker, usually with distal extremes of a paler shade; pronotum: uniformly very dark-brown, same or fractionally darker than head, extreme lateral edge and lateral section of anterior edge black; scutellum: black; elytra: very dark-brown, same shade as pronotum, with elevated verrucae of a fractionally lighter shade, humeral callus is same shade or fractionally paler than surrounding region; venter: head and thoracic and abdominal ventrites black, legs usually a very slightly paler shade, elytral epipleura black.  Cuticular wax: brown in colour, relatively evenly and densely distributed, with greyish also present and arranged in two bands that surround the elytral summit in a horseshoe shape, with the open end facing towards the anterior.

Shape: moderate-sized, noticeably flattened, greatest height viewed laterally behind middle of elytral margin; Head; punctures moderate in size (pd~2=2.5xef) and evenly and moderately densely distributed. Antennae moderate length (AL ♂=42-44%BL, ♀=33-35%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely with only extreme lateral margins becoming very slightly explanate, foveal depression either absent or very shallow, always with a broad, round, slightly elevated area near its lateral extreme, punctures clearly impressed, those on discal area similar in size to those on head, evenly (rarely with small, unpunctured spaces) and moderately densely distributed, becoming much coarser towards lateral margins.  Much finer punctures are scattered very unevenly among the larger ones.  Front margins thickened from behind inside of eye to lateral margin, joining lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially straight for a short length then curving smoothly and very gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; variable, smooth and unpunctured or surface somewhat rough with a scatter of very light punctures much smaller than those on adjacent area on pronotum.  Elytra; surface very lightly curved in anterior half, curvature becoming increasingly greater towards apex in posterior half so that dorsal surface meets ventral at close to 90⁰, punctures distinct anteriorly, becoming much less so in posterior third, and relatively evenly distributed, interstices quite convex throughout giving elytra a rough appearance, a few verrucae may be present in anterior half of disc with many more in posterior third of disc, some slightly elongated and usually placed in longitudinal series, elytral suture slightly carinate in posterior third, submarginal sulcus well-developed in posterior third, surface slightly discontinuous under humeral callus, lateral margins noticeably sinuous. Vertical extension of epipleurae small (HCE/PW=0.29-0.31).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided and broad, closed anteriorly, setae almost absent from prosternal process and from mesosternum, one long seta on anterior, median and posterior trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate, vertical section of epipleura in posterior half either absent or at most horizontal section slightly oblique .  Aedeagus; very short (LPE=22-26%BL), in dorsal view, very broad (WPE=33-37%LPE), almost parallel-sided for a very short distance from base, then suddenly becoming broader, continuing parallel-sider or sides diverging very slightly to about 25% of distance from apex, from where sides converge somewhat irregularly towards apex, close to apex sides become concave to form a smooth triangular tip,  dorsal surface smoothly curved with dorso-median channel absent, dorso-lateral channels very short; in lateral view, bent about 30⁰ near middle of length, from this point to apex almost straight and very thin, sides with an upward kink near anterior end of ostium, tip deflexed.

Similar species

 Ta38 – a slightly larger species with a radically different aedeagus.  Ta38 tends to have less well-developed verrucae on the elytra, otherwise they are difficult to distinguish.

 Ta60 - a species from North Queensland that has a structurally very similar aedeagus of similar total length but the apical broad portion of the aedeagus (in front of the kink in its sides) is longer (65-67%LPE compared with 57-58%LPE for Ta14).  It has smoother elytra that feature less well-developed verrucae. 

Notes

Identification of this species as T. squirescensis (Blackburn, 1893) is by B.J. Selman, of a specimen from the Blackdown Tablelands, in Central Queensland.  The type locality is Mt Squires, just north of the Great Victoria Desert in Western Australia.  In his re-description of the species (Blackburn, 1896), he gives the locality as N.W. Australia.  It is possible that this species has a very large distribution over the drier parts of central Australia, or the name squirescensis refers to another (though very similar) species entirely.

Specimens of Ta14 have been caught off species of “Corymbia” which could be either Corymbia or Blakella.

A single specimen from Jervois, Northern Territory [2007-137] has a slightly different texture to the elytral surface and the vertical section of the epipleura in the posterior half of elytra is completely absent.

Two female specimens, [1997-384] from Tanduringie and [2005-024] from Mount Moffat NP are very likely to be different from Ta14.  The texture of the elytral surface differs from specimens of Ta14 and these specimens both have a row of long setae on the mesosternum.  In addition, the Tanduringie specimen was found on Eucalyptus crebra, and field notes describe its distribution of cuticular wax as “arranged in a random pattern of light-grey and light-brown patches”.
