Name

Trachymela sp. Ta147

Reference specimen

2020-143, Hughenden, Queensland.

Adult Description

Length: ♂ 8.1–8.9 mm (n=4), ♀ 7.7–9.9 mm (n=7).

Colouration:

  • Head: frons and clypeus uniformly mid-brown, fronto-clypeal suture sometimes darker at its lateral extremes, labrum yellow-brown, mandibles brown with black tips; antennae completely and uniformly pale brown.
  • Pronotum: brown, disc and foveal areas fractionally darker than head, with lateral marginal areas slightly paler, a small, black or dark brown longitudinally extended macula is usually present on each side centred just behind inside edge of eye and slightly in front of centre, sometimes this macula extends as a black or dark brown band towards posterior edge, even reaching it, rarely the dark maculae are completely absent.
  • Scutellum: brown, concolorous with pronotum, sometimes with slightly darker lateral edges.
  • Elytra: brown, concolorous with pronotum, with marginal area similar in shade, elytral disc with several longitudinal rows (most prominently, VR3 and VR5, and, less so, VR4) of black tubercles, many in the form of short longitudinal weals with the basal section of VR3 prominently longer than any others, these black verrucae are mostly asynchronous between rows over the anterior half of disc, but line up very roughly in two transverse rows in the apical half, punctures concolorous with interstices, humeral callus black, the black area forming a clearly defined longitudinal streak.
  • Venter & Legs: venter orange-brown, always with lateral parts of thoracic ventrites noticeably darker, rarely tending towards black, legs almost uniformly orange-brown, epipleura light-brown.
  • Cuticular Wax: uniformly light brown, relatively sparsely and unevenly distributed.

Morphology:

  • Shape: almost uniformly and very lightly curved for anterior 75% of length, where curvature increases noticeably, moderately high, elytral summit a little in front of middle of elytral margin.
  • Head: puncture size moderate (pd ≈ 2–3×ef) with some scattered, much smaller, punctures also present, quite evenly and densely distributed. Antennae moderately long (AL/BL: ♂ 39–41%, ♀ 33–36%), filiform.
  • Pronotum: almost evenly curved transversely over discal area, with foveal depression absent and whole of foveal area almost imperceptibly elevated, puncturation on disc clearly impressed, similar in size as on head with much finer punctures scattered among the larger, quite evenly distributed and dense in discal area, interstices in discal area smooth, never obscuring punctures, punctures becoming much coarser towards margin; front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin usually straight for almost half distance before curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.
  • Scutellum: surface smooth, lightly to very sparsely punctured, punctures smaller than those on adjacent surface of pronotum and less clearly impressed.
  • Elytra: surface evenly curved transversely throughout with at most fractionally explanate margins, punctures moderately large and relatively evenly distributed over disc, noticeably larger in marginal area, 3 or 4 longitudinal series of verrucae, round or often considerably extended laterally, rarely with a very fine central puncture, are present on disc, these being VR3, VR4, VR5 and more rarely, VR7; VR3 is usually in the form of a continuous ridge from anterior margin of elytron to antero-median depression, verrucae from other rows may be present, but these are more often small and inconspicuous (see Colouration above for distribution of verrucae); elytral suture carinate in posterior third, lateral margin almost straight or very slightly ascending in anterior quarter, surface slightly discontinuous under humeral callus; vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW = 0.35–0.37), a line of relatively long and closely spaced setae extends along inner edge anterior of mesoventrite to abdominal apex.
  • Venter: prosternal process broad and almost parallel-sided throughout its length, open anteriorly, a sparse scatter of short setae on prosternal process and mesoventrite, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metaventrite beveled, truncate; inner edge of epipleura with a prominent line of long setae from level of hind coxae to apex.

Male Genitalia (Aedeagus):

  • Dimensions: moderate-sized (LPE/BL = 33–36%).
  • Dorsal view: sides of shaft almost parallel, at most very fractionally narrowed near middle, from basal sulcus to about middle of ostial opening from where the sides converge towards apex gradually and relatively smoothly, apex triangular without a projecting tip, spatula large, dorsal surface with short dorso-median channel that terminates in a membranous depression behind ostium, dorsolateral channels well defined, ostial processes with oblique anterior edges that produce a triangular indent in posterior edge of ostium, ostial opening large, about as long as broad.
  • Lateral view: ventral surface of shaft curved about basal sulcus, then almost straight, sometimes very slightly undulate, to posterior of spatula from where it curves downward noticeably, apical half of spatula deflexed, dorsal surface converges with ventral surface from near apical end of ostium, flagellum thins rapidly from where it exits ostial opening.

Immature Stages

    Unknown.

Similar species

  • Ta128: overlaps in distribution with Ta147 and is almost indistinguishable from it on external morphological characters. Known individuals of Ta147 are generally larger, though the few individuals known may not be representative of their species. Males of Ta147 have longer antennae than those of Ta128. The shape of the aedeagus is very different in the two species.
  • Ta240: this species has a very similar body shape and arrangement of verrucae but differs in its smaller body length and by either lacking setae on the inner surface of the epipleura, or, if some are present, they are sparse and quite inconspicuous. The aedeagi of the two species are quite similar in shape, but that of Ta240 is narrower.

Notes

  • Host Associations: Ta147 has been collected from the desert bloodwood Corymbia terminalis.
