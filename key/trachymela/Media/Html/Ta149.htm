Reference specimen

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 6.9-7.9 mm, ♂ 6.8-7.4 mm.

Colour:  head: dark reddish-brown, usually with fronto-clypeal suture broadly rimmed in black, rear of head black (only visible if head extended); mandibles either completely black or dark-brown with black tips; antennomeres 1-2 uniformly straw-brown or light-brown, 3-11 slightly or prominently darker, each segment grading from light-brown proximally to darker brown distally, rarely uniformly shaded; pronotum: dark-brown, same shade or fractionally darker than head, often with  slight and diffuse variations in shade, with the anterior marginal areas in particular often fractionally darker-coloured; scutellum: very dark-brown or (more usually) black; elytra: dark-brown, mostly of similar shade as pronotum with darker (usually almost black) verrucae and raised interstices scattered unevenly over disc; punctures are similar to or a fractionally lighter shade than interstices, humeral callus darker than surrounding area; venter: head black, thoracic and abdominal ventrites very dark-brown or black, legs of same shade, inner surface of epipleura black.  Waxy dorsal covering: light- brown, with a sparse covering present over most of surface, but denser patches on head, near lateral margins of pronotum and distributed unevenly over elytral surface, usually with small, dense clumps that are sometimes arranged to form 3- 4 longitudinal rows.

 Head; punctures moderately large (pd~2xef) and relatively evenly and densely packed. Antennae moderate in length (AL ♂=39-41%BL, ♀=34-35%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression shallow, rarely absent, usually with an elevated peak near its inner edge, discal area with surface smooth to very slightly uneven, and puncturation clearly impressed with punctures similar in size to slightly larger than those on head, evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~1.5-2pd), becoming much coarser towards lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curvature initially slight, then more pronounced towards rear margin, broadest about 2/3 of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; unpunctured or sometimes one or two small punctures, surface smooth to rough.  Elytra; surface mostly evenly curved, usually with a shallow, transverse depression just in front of centre, punctures lie deep within convex interstices, are closely spaced and quite evenly spaced, the thickened interstices sometimes forming small elevated verrucae at their intersections, with the larger of these often having one or two very fine punctures, these verrucae sometimes aligning in one or two longitudinal rows near the posterior half of suture, surface appears slightly more granular in posterior third due to closer spacing of punctures, surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.36-0.40).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel, lightly closed anteriorly, a very light scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, anterior and median trochanters, with 1 long seta present; front edge of metasternum beveled, flat.  Aedeagus; long (LPE=37-40%BL), in dorsal view, sides very slightly concave for most of central section with widest point about 20-25% of distance from apex (WPE ~ 0.16*LPE), narrowing smoothly towards apex in a very slightly concave arc to a prominently extended spatulate tip, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized, dorso-median channel present and may be  short to moderately long, ostium small and oval in shape; in lateral view, quite evenly and continually curved over most of length, curvature increasing noticeably near apex, the long spatula is deflexed and straight.

Similar species

Ta25 - this slightly larger species is very abundant in South-east Queensland and northern NSW and is very similar to Ta149 in external morphology.  The antennae of males of Ta25 are longer than those of Ta149, but there is no difference for females.  The aedeagus of Ta25 is more robust than that of Ta25, with a less sharply triangular spatula.

Notes

This is a relatively rarely collected species from the ranges of NE New South Wales and SE Queensland.  All known hosts are box-barked eucalypts (Eucalyptus sp., Adnataria).
