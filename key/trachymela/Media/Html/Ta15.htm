Name

Trachymela sp. Ta15

Reference specimen

2001-130c, Wilpena, South Australia.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 8.5-9.7 mm (7), ♂ 7.9-8.7 mm (4).

Colour:  head: usually uniformly mid- to dark-brown, sometimes with fronto-clypeal suture and/or parts of clypeus black, labrum usually paler in shade, less often black, mandibles dark-brown with black tips; antennomeres 1-4 light- to mid-brown; 5-11 grading to a fractionally darker or, more commonly, much darker brown or even black, distal portion of outer antennomeres usually darker; pronotum: brown, same shade or fractionally darker than rear of head, usually uniformly so, rarely with lateral margins fractionally lighter in shade; scutellum: dark brown, same shade as adjacent region of pronotum, or black; elytra: brown, same shade as disc of pronotum, often uniformly so, sometimes with some vague flecks of  fractionally lighter shade; punctures are similar to interstices, humeral callus dark-brown or black, similar to surrounding area; venter: uniformly very dark-brown or black, including legs, with lateral extremities of ventrites and distal leg segments sometimes fractionally lighter in shade, elytral epipleura black (Flinders Ranges) or brown (Eyre Peninsula).  Cuticular wax: light brown to orange-brown, with a very patchy distributions – head, lateral marginal region of the pronotum (except on foveal peak), sub-marginal sulcus of elytra, 2 small patches at base of each elytron and antero-median depression have highest concentration of wax; elsewhere the dust is more thinly and unevenly scattered.

Shape: almost evenly rounded from scutellum past greatest height, with curvature increasing in posterior third, greatest height viewed laterally is a little in front of middle of elytral margin.  Head; punctures moderately large (pd~2-2.5xef) and slightly unevenly and densely packed, interstices sometimes rough. Antennae short (AL ♂=40-42%BL, ♀=34-36%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, with foveal area barely depressed and lateral margins very slightly explanate, epidermis in foveal area behind eyes slightly elevated in a broad circular area, discal area with surface very slightly uneven, sometimes shiny, and puncturation usually clearly impressed with punctures similar in size but more regularly formed than those on head, evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~1.5-2pd), becoming noticeably coarser towards lateral margins.  Thick front margins join thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and initially very gradually and then more rapidly towards rear margin, broadest about 2/3 of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; unpunctured or with sparse, fine and unevenly distributed punctures, smaller and shallower than on pronotum.  Elytra; surface evenly curved, punctures surrounded by convex interstices, closely spaced and relatively evenly distributed, interstices are thickened in many places forming small elevated round or more irregularly shaped verrucae at their intersections joined by short ridges that may be oriented longitudinally or transversely, forming a complex granular surface, surface appears even more granular in posterior third due to closer spacing of punctures, punctures are usually more clearly defined but smaller in a smooth area along suture between scutellum and elytral summit, elytral suture not carinate, posterior third of marginal area slightly to moderately concave, surface discontinuous under humeral callus, lateral margin almost straight or slightly sinuate, with anterior 20% usually angled upwards.  Vertical extension of epipleura relatively small (HCE/PW=0.32-0.33).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel or narrowing anteriorly, closed anteriorly, at most a very sparse scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on anterior, median and posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, flat; inner edge of posterior of epipleurae lacking setae.  Aedeagus; short (LPE=28-31%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided or slightly sinuate from basal sulcus to about middle of central section, then broadening slightly with maximum width around posterior of ostial opening  (WPE = 0.30-0.32*LPE) from where sides converge smoothly and gradually towards apex, tip small and triangular, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized, dorso-median channel present but sometimes short and poorly defined, ostium relatively large, ostial flaps extend to about level of spatula; in lateral view, quite evenly and continually curved over most of length with curvature increasing slightly close to apex, tip deflexed.

Similar species

Ta23 – this eastern Australian species is very similar to Ta15 in size, distribution of cuticular wax and sculpture of the dorsal surface.  Its pronotum is coloured differently, with a dark disc grading towards lighter-coloured lateral margins.  The aedeagus of the two species is very similar in shape and dimensions but that of Ta15 does not have a truncated apex and it tends to be more expanded anteriorly compared with the central section.  The cuticular wax distribution of Ta15 never includes series of small dots of higher density in the posterior half of elytral disc, as is seen in many (if not most) specimens of Ta23 and the wax is always of uniform colour.

Ta21 -

 Ta146 – a smaller species (there is no size overlap) that has a similar distribution as Ta15.

Notes

This species is very close to Ta23 and these may turn out to be conspecific.  The Flinders Ranges population tends to have black epipleural surfaces, while these are brown in other specimens.

Host records for Ta15 are from unidentified mallee eucalypts and unspecified box-barked eucalypts, as well as a single record from Eucalyptus incrassata (Symphyomyrtus : Dumaria).
