Distribution

Ta157

Name

Trachymela biondii Daccordi

Reference specimen

2017-462, Emu Flat, Limestone Road, Victoria.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 6.3-6.9 mm [5], ♂ 5.6-6.5 mm [5].

Colour:  head: uniformly light-brown to a little behind level of rear of eyes, black further back, mandibles black-tipped; antennomeres 1-3 light to mid-brown, 4-6 grading to black or very dark brown, 7-11 black; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, often completely so, sometimes with two black, longitudinal patches, one on each side of disc behind inside edge of eye, that may be indistinct or clearly defined; scutellum: brown, same shade as pronotum, usually broadly edged in a slightly or much darker shade of brown; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, sometimes almost completely so, more often with scattered irregularly-shaped and relatively small black vittae on disc; punctures variable, some similar in shade as interstices, others darker, even black; humeral callus dark brown or black; venter: mid-brown, usually some darker colouration present on all or parts of thoracic ventrites, inside surface of elytral epipleura brown.  Cuticular wax: usually a sparse but uniformly distributed layer of uniform shade that can be from greyish to light brown. 

Head; punctures variable, moderately sized (pd~1.5-2xef) and somewhat unevenly to quite evenly but quite densely packed. Antennae long (AL ♂=48-50%BL, ♀=45-46%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely over discal area, a broad shallow depression situated behind the outside edge of each eye, margins prominently explanate; disc smooth or sometimes a little rugulose; discal puncturation variable, usually clearly impressed, even to slighly acervate in distribution and moderately dense in discal area, sometimes less clearly impressed and more uneven; punctures becoming much coarser at lateral margins; front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; smooth, shagreened or slightly rugulose, unpunctured, or, rarely, with a few small and shallow punctures.  Elytra; surface evenly curved, punctures distinct and slightly unevenly distributed, with much smaller punctures scattered sparingly on the interstices, verrucae absent, suture carinate in posterior 25%, surface continuous under humeral callus, vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.35-0.39); lateral margin usually slightly oblique between humeral callus and a point a little anterior to summit, straight and perpendicular to anterior edge elsewhere.  Venter; Prosternal process narrowing anteriorly, closed anteriorly, a sparse scatter of short setae at rear of prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate; inner edge of posterior of epipleurae lacking setae.  Aedeagus; small (LPE=23-27%BL), quadrate, in dorsal view, narrowing slightly from basal sulcus to about middle then broadening slightly towards apex before the sides converge towards apex in a smooth arc, dorsal surface smoothly curved, tip barely protruding, ostium almost circular; in lateral view, angled through about 125̊ just posterior to middle, then almost straight to apex, dorsal and ventral surfaces parallel from middle to close to apex where they converge to tip in a smooth curve, tip small and very slightly reflexed, protruding part of flagellum is almost straight and moderately thick.

Similar species

Ta158 –  this is another species that feeds on Leptospermae and is also found in the highland areas of south-eastern Australia.  It tends to be slightly larger and lacks the black patches present on the elytra of most specimens of Ta157.  The aedeagus of Ta158 is quite sharply pointed rather than rounded as in Ta157 and its flagellum is strongly bent on exit from the ostium.

Notes

The identification of Ta157 as T. biondii Daccordi is by comparison with the original description.  This is one of the very few species of Trachymela that does not feed on eucalypt hosts.  It has been recorded from Leptospermeae.  A record from Melaleuca is possibly a host misidentification.

A single female specimen (recorded from Kunzea) from Berowra, near Sydney, lack any black markings dorsally but is otherwise very similar to typical specimens from the alpine regions.  It is possible that it is Ta158 but would be exceptionally small for that species.
