Name

Trachymela spilota (Chapuis)

Reference specimen

2018-139, Burgoyne Gap, Licola, Victoria

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 8.7-9.4 mm (7), ♂ 8.2-9.0 mm (3).

Colour:  head: mid- to dark-brown with areas of black whose position and extent is variable: most commonly, the black occupies a broad band extending from eye to eye behind fronto-clypeal suture, sometimes the band is broader and includes much or even all of the clypeus, rarely, only the clypeus or a thin strip of it adjoining the labrum is black, rear of head black (only visible if head is extruded), mandibles completely black; antennomeres 1-3 light to mid-brown, 4-7 grading towards much darker brown, rarely black, 8-11 same as 7; pronotum: brown, same shade as those parts of head not black, with variable dark markings: oval patches on either side of centre, sometimes these patches are barely darker than surrounding area or they may be black or (rarely) absent, sometimes they are joined into a single patch, a small round dark (even black) patch in foveal areas is usually present, lateral (and sometimes, parts of anterior) margination black; scutellum: black; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with a black patch centred on elytral summit, suture between this patch and scutellum is usually bordered in black, mostly small but black and prominent verrucae are sparingly scattered across disc with the largest of these being two usually longitudinally extended verrucae set about 2/3 of distance to apex, often a line of two or three small verrucae extends from near anterior edge halfway between scutellum and humeral callus a short distance towards posterior, rarely these are joined into a continuous black ridge, humeral callus black; venter: uniformly black or very dark brown, epipleura black, rarely brown (northern specimen only).  Cuticular wax: light-brown, sparsely to quite densely but evenly distributed layer, making a clear contrast with the black humeral callus and verrucae.

Head; punctures quite small (pd~1.5xef), and rather evenly and densely packed. Antennae moderately long (AL ♂ = 42-44%BL, ♀=38-39%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely to foveal area, then noticeably, sometimes quite strongly, explanate at margins, foveal depression very shallow or absent and usually with a small, inconspicuous round callus at its centre, discal surface usually finely wrinkled with individual punctures somewhat obsolete and difficult to discern, sometimes disc is smoother and punctures more clearly impressed, then relatively fine, similar or fractionally smaller than on head, and evenly and densely distributed, becoming considerably coarser at lateral margins.  Front margins join thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, becoming much thicker in posterior half.  Scutellum; surface smooth and shiny or slightly roughened, unpunctured.  Elytra; surface evenly curved longitudinally, though with a slight gibbous tendency around summit, curved quite evenly transversely, sometimes with a very slight explanation near margins, punctures closely and evenly distributed with convex interstices, much smaller punctures are scattered sparingly on the interstices; small raised verrucae, most with one or more small punctures, are scattered over the surface of disc with the majority round and 1-4 puncture diameters in size, at least two are considerably larger and extended longitudinally, situated about 2/3 of distance to apex and midway between suture and margin, elytral suture not carinate, posterior half of marginal area very weakly concave, surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.38-0.42).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, sometimes narrowing anteriorly, closed anteriorly, with a very light scatter of short setae throughout, mesosternum with a few short setae, a single seta usually present on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; small (LPE=25-27%BL), in dorsal view, broadening slightly from near basal sulcus then almost parallel-sided until sides diverge very slightly to widest point at about 25% of distance from apex, from where sides converge in a smoothly rounded arc, dorsal surface with a faint dorso-median channel present, dorsolateral channels long, tip protruding slightly and smoothly rounded, ostium posterior edge triangular, with apex of triangle in middle and sides formed by protruding projections of dorsal surface; in lateral view, ventral surface almost evenly and lightly curved throughout its length, ventral surface straight in central section, then bent downward near apex, lightly sclerotised dorsal surface projects a short distance above the side edges, tip deflexed, flagellum straight.

Similar species

Ta169 is superficially very similar and sometimes occurs together with Ta167. Its scutellum is brown, not black, it lacks the black, common patch at the elytral summit and its epipleura are not black.  The round verrucae of Ta169 are more elevated and thus appear more prominent than those of Ta167.  The two species differ clearly in the shape and size of the aedeagus.

Ta02 – larger specimens of this species overlap in size with Ta167.  They can be difficult to distinguish – Ta02 has a denser scatter of less elevated verrucae on the elytral disc and lacks the two larger longitudinally extended verrucae towards posterior of elytra, it also usually lacks the black common patch around elytral summit.  The aedeagi are similar in structure, but that of Ta167 is proportionally larger and more curved.

Notes

Ta167 is identified as Trachymela spilota (Chapuis) by comparison with photos of the spilota type specimen (in RBINS).  The black marking on head and pronotum correspond almost exactly with typical Victorian specimens of Ta167, and the elytral verrucae have a similar distribution (including the two prominent post-median ones).

Almost all the specimens seen are from Victoria.  The Dundee (Northern NSW) specimen is included here but differs in several ways: its epipleura are not black, the head has much less black colouration than Victorian specimens, the pronotum lacks the central dark patch/es and the verrucae are denser and generally more extended longitudinally.  However, only a single specimen is available, and these may turn out to be very variable characters in the species.

Specimen [2022-118] from the Blue Mountains is also somewhat anomalous.  In appearance it is very similar to a typical Ta167, with the anterior portion of the elytral suture broadly black, the presence of the two larger verrucae in the posterior third of elytra and the ventral surface and inner epipleural surfaces black.  In shape, the aedeagus is essentially the same as that of a typical Ta167, but it is shorter and narrower than would be expected for a specimen of that size.

Ta167 has been collected from several Eucalyptus species.  In the southern part of the range, it has been found on E. camaldulensis and E. goniocalyx.  The northern NSW specimen was collected from E. nova-anglica.
