Name

Trachymela inops (Blackburn)

Reference specimen

PGK/5087a, Mount Ida, Victoria

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 10.0 mm (1), ♂ 8.4-8.8 mm (2).

Colour:  head: uniformly mid- to dark-brown, sometimes with some black along fronto-clypeal suture near antennal insertions, labrum yellow-brown, mandibles brown with black tips; antennomeres 1-5 light-brown, 6-11 grading towards a slightly darker shade, with the distal extremes of segments usually dark-brown; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, often with vague dark markings on disc, a small round dark (even black) patch in foveal areas is usually present, lateral (and sometimes, parts of anterior) margination dark-brown or black; scutellum: brown, same shade as adjacent area of pronotum, usually edged in darker brown; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with small, round but black and prominently elevated verrucae sparingly scattered and quite evenly across disc and into marginal area with the largest of these being no more than about 4 pd in diameter, some longitudinal alignments of these are present, including one series in posterior half and spaced a short distance from suture, humeral callus dark-brown or black; venter: head brown, with gula sometimes at least partially black, prosternal process brown with rest of prosternum black, mesosternum and anterior margin of metsternum dark-brown or black, elsewhere brown-dark-brown, sometimes with lighter patches, legs brown, epipleura brown,.  Waxy dorsal covering: light-brown, sparsely to moderately densely but very evenly distributed layer, making a clear contrast with the black verrucae.

Head; punctures quite small (pd~1.5xef), and rather evenly and densely packed. Antennae moderately long (AL ♂ = 41-43%BL, ♀=35%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely to foveal area, then noticeably, sometimes quite strongly, explanate at margins, foveal depression shallow to deep, usually with a very slightly elevated round callus at its centre that may be black and so more conspicuous, discal surface usually finely wrinkled with individual punctures somewhat obsolete and difficult to discern, sometimes disc is smoother and punctures more clearly impressed, then relatively fine, similar or fractionally smaller than on head, and evenly and densely distributed, becoming considerably coarser at lateral margins.  Front margins join thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, becoming much thicker in posterior half.  Scutellum; surface smooth and shiny or slightly roughened, unpunctured.  Elytra; surface evenly curved longitudinally, curved quite evenly transversely, sometimes with a very slight explanation near margins, punctures closely and evenly distributed with convex interstices, much smaller punctures are scattered sparingly on the interstices; small (diameter 1-4 pd), round, prominently elevated verrucae, some with a fine central puncture, are scattered over the surface of disc, elytral suture not carinate, posterior half of marginal area slightly concave, surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.38-0.41).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, sometimes narrowing anteriorly, open or lightly closed anteriorly, with a very light scatter of short setae throughout, mesosternum with a few short setae, a single seta usually present on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; long (LPE=40-43%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided from basal sulcus for about half of length, then sides diverge very slightly to widest point at about 25% of distance from apex, from where sides converge in a smoothly rounded arc, dorsal surface with a well-defined dorso-median channel, dorsolateral channels long, tip protruding slightly and smoothly rounded, ostium large with dorsal flaps protruding deep into it; in lateral view, ventral surface almost straight in central section, then bent downward near apex, tip deflexed, flagellum very thin.

Similar species

Ta02 – this species is very similar in overall appearance, but is smaller.  Its verrucae are much less elevated than those of Ta169 and usually more densely distributed.

Ta167 is a similar-sized species scattered with small black verrucae on the elytral disc.  It can be distinguished by its extensive black areas on the head, its black scutellum and the black inner surface of epipleura.

Notes

Ta169 is identified as Trachymela inops (Blackburn) by comparison with the inops type specimen in the BMNH.  Another specimen labelled T. inops in BMNH, in appearance almost identical to the type specimen, has had its aedeagus extracted.  In shape and dimensions, it is almost identical to that of Ta169.

Ta169 has been collected from Eucalyptus species.
