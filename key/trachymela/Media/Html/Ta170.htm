Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.9-9.5 mm, ♂ 7.9-8.6 mm.

Colour:  head: often uniformly dark-brown, sometimes with parts or (rarely) all of clypeus and/or area  adjacent to anterior of eyes black, labrum usually yellow-brown, mandibles with black tips, sometimes completely black; antennomeres 1-3 light-brown,  4-6 grading towards dark-brown or black, 7-11 same shade as 6, usually with proximal extreme paler; pronotum: disc dark-brown or (rarely) almost black, slightly darker than rear of head, with lateral marginal region usually lighter in shade (similar to head) becoming dark brown (even black) at extreme margin; scutellum: black; elytra: dark-brown, same or fractionally darker shade than pronotal disc with small fractionally darker or black raised interstices scattered about the surface, usually a few small indistinct flecks of slightly lighter colour are present, most frequently along elytral margin, in middle of antero-median depression and in the area behind it; punctures are similar in shade to interstices, humeral callus similar to surrounding area; venter: black or very dark reddish-brown, head, lateral extremities of prosternum and abdominal ventrites sometimes a slightly lighter shade, elytral epipleura not black.  Waxy dorsal covering: light brown to orange-brown, with a very patchy distribution - concentrations are present in two patches on the head, along the lateral margins of the pronotum, in a thin band (usually interrupted near middle) along lateral margins of elytra (in the sub-marginal sulcus), 2 small patches at base of each elytron and another (slightly larger) in the centre of antero-median depression, smaller patches arranged roughly in 2-3 longitudinal lines from antero-median depression towards apex; elsewhere the dust is very thinly scattered.

Shape:  viewed laterally, quite evenly rounded over much of surface until close to apex and relatively flat (H/L ♂=0.39-0.45, ♀=0.41-0.46), where radius of curvature increases noticeably, greatest height a little in front of or near middle of elytral margin.  Head; punctures relatively fine (pd~1.5-2xef) and quite evenly and densely packed. Antennae moderate in length (AL ♂=37-41%BL, ♀=34-38%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely to near lateral edges where it becomes quite markedly explanate, foveal depression absent or very weak, epidermis near centre usually slightly elevated in a broad circular area, discal area with surface smooth to very slightly uneven, shiny, and puncturation clearly impressed with punctures similar in size to those on head, quite evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~1.5-2pd), becoming noticeably coarser towards lateral margins.  Front margins join much thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin then curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin and becoming much thicker from about middle, broadest about 2/3 of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; sparsely punctured with punctures usually slightly smaller and more (often much more) shallow than those on pronotum.  Elytra; surface smoothly curved throughout longitudinally, evenly or very slightly explanate transversely, antero-median depression very shallow, sometimes barely discernible, punctures surrounded by very convex interstices, closely spaced and rather evenly distributed, interstices are convex and give the surface a quite uniform granular appearance though never hiding punctures, they may be thickened in some places forming a few scattered and very small and inconspicuous elevated verrucae at their intersections, much finer  punctures scattered over interstices, surface slightly more granular in posterior third due to closer spacing of punctures, elytral suture not carinate, posterior 20% of marginal area moderately to strongly concave, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura relatively small (HCE/PW=0.31-0.35).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel or narrowing anteriorly, closed anteriorly, a very sparse scatter of setae usually present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on anterior, median and posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, flat.  Aedeagus; short (LPE=27-30%BL), in dorsal view, approximately parallel-sided or very slightly sinuate with maximum width around middle of ostium (WPE = 0.30-0.33*LPE) from where sides converge smoothly towards apex, tip triangular, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized, dorso-median channel present but often short and poorly defined, ostium relatively large with a clump of short setae at apical extreme; in lateral view, quite evenly and continually curved over almost whole length with curvature increasing slightly just before apex, tip deflexed, flagellum quite thin and straight on exit.

Similar species

 Ta21 – there are very few characters that distinguish between these two species.  One of the most useful ones is that Ta170 has brown epipleura (with a few exceptions that are partially black) where those of Ta21 are always black.  There are other differences, mostly relating to colouring: there is no black patch at the elytral summit and the front of the head is usually not black.  The aedeagus is very similar to that of Ta21 in shape, but it tends to be just slightly shorter (though there is some heterogeneity in that measure, indicating that the species may not be properly defined) and is usually slightly broader (though there is considerable overlap in that measure also).  Almost all specimens have a tuft of short setae in the middle of the spatula, adjacent to the ostial opening, which seems to be less well defined (or much smaller?) in most specimens of Ta21.  This feature is at the limits of what can is resolvable with the equipment available to me, so may be unreliable to use as a species discriminator.

Notes

This species is a somewhat problematic grouping, very close to Ta21 (see above) and not well distinguished from it. 

Ta170 has been found predominantly on the red-gum group of Eucalyptus, such as E. tereticornis, E. blakelyi, E.dealbata and E. prava.  There are just a few records from ‘box-barked’ eucalypts (including E. albens).  It may be that more than one species is grouped under this code, but more specimens (with accurate host records) will be needed to resolve this.

Specimen [2017-349] from Pikedale, west of Stanthorpe, illustrates the problems in differentiating between Ta21 and Ta170.  Both species are abundant in this area, with Ta170 commonly found on species of the red-gum group such as E. blakelyi, while Ta21 is found there on boxes such as E. moluccana.  This specimen has brown epipleura, its clypeus is also brown and it lacks a black patch at the elytral summit.  A patch of setae is clearly visible on the aedeagus, at the posterior of the spatula.  All these would clearly place this specimen with Ta170.  However, the aedeagus LPE/BL ratio is 0.301, which more closely corresponds to ratios seen with similarly sized specimens of Ta21 (those of Ta170 rarely exceed 0.29).  In addition, it was found on a box species (almost certainly E. moluccana) which is more frequently associated with Ta21.
