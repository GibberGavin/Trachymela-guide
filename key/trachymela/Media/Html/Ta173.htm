Name

Trachymela sordida (Blackburn)

Reference specimen

2019-059b, Emu Bay, Kangaroo Is., South Australia

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.95-10.1 mm, ♂ 8.0-9.4 mm (4).

Colour:  head: uniformly dark to very dark brown, rarely with clypeus slightly darker, mandibles dark-brown with black tips; antennomere 1 light- to mid-brown, 2-3 straw-brown, 4-5 grading from straw-brown to black, 6-11 black; pronotum: uniformly dark to very dark brown, same shade or fractionally darker than rear of head, with lateral margins rarely slightly paler in shade; scutellum: dark brown, same shade as adjacent region of pronotum; elytra: dark brown, mostly of same shade as disc of pronotum with small inconspicuous flecks of slightly lighter colour usually present on disc, arranged in 3 or 4 longitudinal series, marginal area is also mostly of this lighter shade; punctures same shade as surrounding area, humeral callus dark-brown or black; venter: generally uniformly very dark-brown or black rarely with very slightly lighter brown areas, elytral epipleura black.  Waxy dorsal covering: light brown to orange-brown, with a patchy distributions - concentrations are present in two patches on the head, along the lateral margins of the pronotum, in a thin band along lateral margins of elytra (in the sub-marginal sulcus), 2 small patches at base of each elytron and small flecks arranged in 3-4 longitudinal lines from about middle of elytron towards apex; elsewhere the wax is scattered very sparsely or absent.

Shape:  quite evenly rounded but relatively flat, highest point about halfway along elytral margin, ventrites project a short distance below elytral epipleura.  Head; punctures moderately large (pd~2-3xef) and slightly unevenly but densely packed, interstices sometimes rough. Antennae short (AL ♂=38-40%BL, ♀=37-38%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely over all but lateral margins which are slightly explanate, foveal depression absent, instead epidermis there may be slightly elevated in a broad circular area, sometimes with slightly smaller punctures, discal area with surface very slightly uneven, shiny, and puncturation clearly impressed with punctures similar in size to those on head but usually less closely packed, evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~1.5-2pd), becoming noticeably coarser towards lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, broadest about 3/4 of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; unpunctured, sometimes surface finely wrinkled.  Elytra; viewed laterally, curvature of dorsal surface even and gentle over anterior 80%, then much tighter over posterior 25%, punctures surrounded by very convex interstices, closely spaced and rather evenly distributed, interstices are thickened in many places forming small elevated verrucae at their intersections joined by short elevated ridges that may be oriented longitudinally or transversely, forming a complex granular surface, the area adjacent to suture in front of elytral summit sometimes contains 2 or 3 smooth raised interstices extending posteriorly from near anterior edge, punctures may be more clearly defined but smaller in a smooth area straddling the elytral summit, elytral suture slightly carinate in posterior third, posterior third of marginal area moderately concave, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura relatively small (HCE/PW=0.29-0.32).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel or narrowing anteriorly, closed anteriorly, at most a very sparse scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on anterior, median and posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, flat.  Aedeagus; short (LPE=31-33%BL), in dorsal view, approximately parallel-sided or, rarely, very slightly sinuate with maximum width at or just behind posterior of ostium  (WPE = 0.28-0.31*LPE) from where sides converge smoothly and gradually towards apex, tip triangular, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized, dorso-median channel present but often short and poorly defined, ostium relatively large; in lateral view, quite evenly and continually curved over whole length, tip deflexed; flagellum relatively thick and slightly flattened, with a thin, longitudinal groove in centre of dorsal surface.

Similar species

Ta21 – a very dark-brown species that occurs on the New England Tablelands of NSW.  The pattern of wax distribution on the elytra can quite closely resemble that of Ta173, and in fact the two species are very difficult to separate morphologically.  Ta21 has its elytral disc more finely granulated and its aedeagus tends to be more slender in dorsal view and less curved in lateral view.

Ta166 – a species from the northern New England Tablelands that has the same pattern of granularity and distribution of cuticular wax on its elytra as Ta173.   It is distinguishable by its aedeagus which has a larger spatula, a broadly blunt apex and a flagellum that is very broad and deeply grooved on its dorsal surface.

Ta170 – this is another species that closely resembles Ta173 due to the similar distribution of cuticular wax but can be immediately distinguished by its brown epipleura, as well as its possession of a relatively large patch of cuticular wax in the area of the anterio-median depression. 

Ta171 – occurs in southern New South Wales and Victoria and differs from Ta173 by the possession of moderately-sized verrucae over much of the elytral disc.  It is also distinguishable by its proportionately shorter aedeagus.

Notes

Identification as T. sordida (Blackburn) is by comparison with the type specimen in the BMNH.

Ta173 has been recorded from Eucalyptus odorata and E. leucoxylon (both within section Adnataria), with a single specimen from Kangaroo Island found on E. cosmophylla.
