Distribution

Ta21

Name

Trachymela sp. Ta21

Reference specimen

2001-358, Gibraltar Range, N.E. New South Wales

Adult Description

Length: ♀ (6.9)7.5-10.0 mm, ♂ 7.2-8.8 mm.

Colour:  head: frons dark-brown or (less often) black, clypeus and adjacent to anterior half of eyes black, labrum usually yellow-brown, mandibles completely black, rarely very dark-brown away from tips; antennomeres 1-3 light-brown,  4-6 grading towards dark-brown or black, 7-11 same shade as 6; pronotum: dark-brown or (rarely) black, same shade as rear of head, with lateral margins and sometimes anterior margins behind eyes black; scutellum: black; elytra: dark-brown, same or fractionally darker shade than pronotal disc , sometimes with a black patch centred on highest point of elytral suture that is usually wider posteriorly and small black raised interstices scattered about the surface, usually a few indistinct flecks of slightly lighter colour are present, most frequently along elytral margin, in middle of antero-median depression and in the area behind it; punctures are similar in shade to interstices, humeral callus similar to surrounding area; venter: black or very dark reddish-brown, lateral extremities of ventrites rarely a slightly lighter shade, elytral epipleura black.  Cuticular wax: light brown to orange-brown, with a very patchy distribution - concentrations are present in two patches on the head, along the lateral margins of the pronotum, in a thin band (usually interrupted in the middle) along lateral margins of elytra (in the sub-marginal sulcus), 2 small patches at base of each elytron and another (slightly larger) in the centre of antero-median depression, smaller patches arranged roughly in 2-4 longitudinal lines from antero-median depression towards apex; elsewhere the dust is very thinly scattered and may be of a lighter shade or even grey.

Shape:  viewed laterally, quite evenly rounded over much of surface until close to apex and relatively flat (H/L ♂=0.40-0.46, ♀=0.42-0.49), where radius of curvature increases noticeably, greatest height a little in front of or near middle of elytral margin.  Head; punctures relatively fine (pd~1.5-2xef) and quite evenly and densely packed. Antennae short (AL ♂=38-44%BL, ♀=32-36%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely to near lateral edges where it becomes quite markedly explanate, foveal depression absent or very weak, epidermis near centre slightly elevated in a broad circular area, discal area with surface smooth to very slightly uneven, shiny, and puncturation clearly impressed with punctures similar in size to those on head, quite evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~1.5-2pd), becoming noticeably coarser towards lateral margins.  Front margins join much thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin then curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin and becoming much thicker from about middle, broadest about 2/3 of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; unpunctured or (rarely) with sparse punctures in anterior half, usually slightly smaller and more shallow than those on pronotum.  Elytra; surface smoothly curved throughout longitudinally, evenly or very slightly explanate transversely, antero-median depression present but shallow, punctures surrounded by very convex interstices, closely spaced and rather evenly distributed, interstices are convex and give the surface a quite uniform granular appearance, they may be thickened in some places forming very small and inconspicuous elevated verrucae at their intersections, surface slightly more granular in posterior third due to closer spacing of punctures, punctures are more clearly defined but smaller in a smooth area straddling the elytral summit, elytral suture not carinate, posterior 20% of marginal area moderately to strongly concave, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura relatively small (HCE/PW=0.31-0.34).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel or narrowing anteriorly, closed anteriorly, at most a very sparse scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on anterior, median and posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, flat, inner edge of epipleura lacking setae.  Aedeagus; short (LPE=28-35%BL), in dorsal view, approximately parallel-sided or very slightly sinuate with maximum width around middle of ostium  (WPE = 0.26-0.32*LPE) from where sides converge smoothly towards apex, tip triangular, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized, dorso-median channel present but often short and poorly defined, ostium relatively large; in lateral view, quite evenly and continually curved over almost whole length with curvature increasing slightly just before apex, tip deflexed, flagellum quite thin and straight on exit.

Similar species

Ta170 - very similar to Ta21 in most respects but usually lacks the black epipleura and black blotch at elytral summit.  See Ta170 for a more detailed discussion of the differences.

Ta173 – a species that occurs in South Australia and Victoria and is very similar in almost all respects to Ta21.  The pattern of granulations of the elytral disc differs between the two species with the granulation coarser in Ta173 than Ta21.  The dorsal colouring of the head of Ta173 is invariably uniformly dark-brown or black.

Ta15 - another species from South Australian that is very similar in its general appearance to Ta21.  Like Ta21, it has black epipleura and there is little to distinguish it from that species except the lack of a black, smooth common area around elytral summit and the lighter colouration of head and pronotum.  The aedeagus of the two species is very similar, but that of Ta15 tends to be slighty more expanded at its widest point near the apex.

Ta23 - can be readily distinguished from Ta21 by the lighter colouration of its epipleura.

Ta165 – a small species that does overlap in size with very small specimens of Ta21 and is very difficult to distinguish from them.  One of the few characters that distinguishes them are the rows of small and inconspicuous verrucae on the elytral disc that are present in specimens of Ta165.  The aedeagus of Ta165 is of the “ovate ostium” type, quite similar to that of Ta21.  An example of the conundrum in identification posed by these species are the two specimens [2000-385a,b].  Both males were captured from the same sapling in an area where both Ta21 and Ta165 are found.  The smaller specimen, [2000-385a] would be unusually small for a Ta21, its aedeagus is of the “ovate ostium” type and it has rows of small verrucae on the elytra – almost certainly Ta165.  The aedeagus of [2000-385b] is similar, though slightly broader and longer and its elytral surface is more uniformly rugose in its sculpture – probably Ta21.

Ta113 – is very similar in most features of its external morphology and distribution of cuticular wax and it is often found together with Ta21.  It has a noticeably wider elytral marginal area (HCE/PW=0.37-0.40) than Ta21, with the inner epipleural surface not black, the cuticular wax on the posterior half of the elytral disc forms 3 longitudinal series (no more than 2 in Ta21), and its ostium is ovate in form.

Notes

Ta21 is a member of a group of species that is characterized in part by the dense concentrations of cuticular wax along the lateral margins of the pronotum and elytra.   This species group is likely not a monophyletic grouping, containing species with differing external morphologies and aedeagus shapes.

Ta21 is a relatively abundant species on the New England Tableland and some distance north from there into Queensland, with an outlier population on Kroombit Tops, in Central Queensland.  It seems to feed on a range of species of Eucalyptus, with 81 host records being from unspecified ‘smooth-barked’ eucalypts (29), unspecified ‘box-barked’ eucalypts (10), grey gum (3), peppermint (1), Eucalyptus deanei (8), E. blakelyi (11), E. bridgesiana (1), E. radiata (3) and E. moluccana (34), and E. albens (1).

The wide range of body lengths, some variation in morphology correlated with host utilization and the wide range of hosts these have been found on indicates that more than one species may be included in this assemblage.  In particular, several of the larger specimens have been found on peppermints.  These specimens are often teneral and generally lighter in colour than is typical for the species, with a well-defined and large flat black area around elytral summit that is wider posteriorly than anteriorly.  These are likely the same species as T. arcula (Chapuis).

There is also doubt about some of the smaller specimens included, with the distribution of body lengths, particularly of males, showing a secondary peak between 7.25-7.5 mm.  These smaller males have been collected primarily from box species, particularly E. moluccana, and most have an LPE/BL ratio that is somewhat smaller that of other males of Ta21.  Two of the smaller female specimens have a different antennal segmentation pattern to other specimens of Ta21, with an (A6-A11)/AL ratio that is less than 0.44 compared with a range of 0.48-0.5 for other females of this species.
