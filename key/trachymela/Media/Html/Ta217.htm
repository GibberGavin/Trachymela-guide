Name

Trachymela sp. Ta217

Reference specimen

2020-309, Greenlands, S.E. Queensland.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.3-8.5 mm (2), ♂ 7.35 mm (1).

Colour:  head: dark reddish-brown, with clypeus and adjoining area around antennal insertions black, labrum light brown, mandibles mostly black; antennomeres 1-2 light-brown, 3-4 grading from light-brown to black, 5-11 black; pronotum: dark reddish-brown or black, if black then lateral margins and anterior margin dark reddish-brown; scutellum: black; elytra: dark reddish-brown or black, similar or darker than pronotum, sometimes with small patches of slightly lighter shade scattered about surface of disc in no clearly defined pattern, humeral callus same shade as surrounding area; venter: black with small areas of reddish-brown along lateral margins, legs black, epipleura black.  Waxy dorsal covering: brown and grey are present, the brown is concentrated along lateral margins of head and prothorax and as disconnected patches along lateral  margins of elytra, with smaller patches along anterior margin of elytra and in centre of antero-median depression, with a light presence also along posterior third of elytral suture, the grey is most obvious in central parts of elytral disc, particularly in posterior half, with a very sparse sprinkle on disc of pronotum, a broad band along elytral suture from (and including) scutellum to just past highest point is dust-free.

Shape:  quite evenly rounded to about 105 of distance from apex, where curvature becomes much greater, highest point at or a little in front of middle of elytral margin, ventrites project a short distance below elytral epipleura.  Head; punctures moderate in size (pd~1.5-2.5xef) and quite evenly but densely distributed.  Antennae moderately long (AL ♂=40.8%BL, ♀=34-38%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; evenly curved transversely over discal region, lateral margins slightly explanate, with foveal depression shallow always with a rounded elevation near its centre, puncturation clearly impressed, evenly and quite densely distributed in discal area with a mix of puncture sizes usually present, the larger fractionally larger than those on head, becoming much coarser at lateral margins.  Front margins join the thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin straight for a very short distance then curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, becoming noticeably thicker posteriorly.  Scutellum; smooth, unpunctured or with just a few very fine and shallow punctures.  Elytra; surface slightly flattened in front of highest point, punctures relatively evenly distributed but distinct only in a strip adjacent to suture from scutellum to about summit, elsewhere obscured by the network of thickened interstices, with much smaller punctures scattered very sparingly on the interstices, interstices are prominently raised to form a network of ridges and wrinkles over the whole surface of disc, with a few slightly thicker ones appearing as inconspicuous verrucae scattered among them, posterior third of marginal area prominently concave, suture carinate in posterior third, lateral margin sinuate, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura small (HCE/PW=0.29-0.31).  Venter; Prosternal process variable narrowest anteriorly or almost parallel-sided, lightly closed anteriorly, short setae present at rear of prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; moderately long (LPE=29.3%BL), in dorsal view, sides parallel from basal sulcus to about 25% of distance from apex, from there sides converge towards apex initially gradually, then more rapidly, dorsal surface smoothly curved with dorso-median channel present, though sometimes very faint, dorsolateral channels well-defined, tip prominent and truncate; in lateral view, almost evenly curved throughout most of length, then a pronounced (approx. 45°) downward bend shortly before apex, thinning towards apex over about 15-20% of length, tip strongly deflexed.

Similar species

 Ta166 – this species is very similar in external structural features and colouring to Ta217 and their distributions overlap to a large degree, but it differs in the larger size of individuals and the different distribution and colours of the cuticular wax.  The aedeagus of the two species are very similar structurally but that of Ta166 is not as strongly bent downwards (in lateral view) as that of Ta217.

Notes

In external morphology, Ta217 most closely resembles Ta166, though individuals are considerably smaller than those of the latter species.

The only host recorded for this species is Eucalyptus moluccana.
