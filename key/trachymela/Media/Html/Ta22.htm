Distribution

Ta22

Name

Trachymela pustulosa (Blackburn)

Reference specimen

2000-266a, Boonoo Boonoo, NE NSW

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 8.4-9.4 mm, ♂ (7.85)8.5-9.4 mm.

Colour:  head mid- to dark-brown, often darker, even black, towards anterior and labrum, with black-tipped mandibles; labrum similar or darker, even black, rarely slightly paler; antennomeres 1-4 light to mid-brown, 5-11 much darker, often black; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, with a near-circular black patch on either side of centre and a smaller circular black patch behind each eye; scutellum: black;  elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with prominent black raised, shiny, smooth pustules studding the whole surface, a transversely aligned concentration of these, sometimes partially confluent, is situated behind transverse depression, punctures are similar in colour as interstices, humeral callus black; venter: completely black, rarely tending dark-brown at lateral extremes, inside surface of elytral epipleura brown (northern NSW specimens) or partially to fully black (southern specimens).  Cuticular wax: moderate to thick layer of grey-brown to brown, with different shades present on most individuals, that do not conceal the dark verrucae.

 Shape: rather flattened, greatest height viewed laterally is at around middle of elytral margin; Head; punctures relatively small (pd~1.5xef), evenly and densely packed, a small more lightly punctured area sometimes present at centre rear. Antennae short (AL ♂=39-43%BL, ♀=33-35%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression very shallow or absent with a clearly defined and almost round elevated area in middle, puncturation clearly impressed, relatively fine, evenly and moderately densely distributed in discal area, becoming coarser from foveal depression to lateral margins, interstices smooth and shiny in discal area.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; smooth and unpunctured.  Elytra; surface evenly curved transversely, lightly curved longitudinally in anterior half, then more strongly curved, a distinct but shallow depression extends across surface just in front of middle, punctures large and distinct and relatively evenly distributed between the prominent verrucae, verrucae not aligned in longitudinal series, individual verrucae large and usually oval in shape, only rarely much longer than wide, sometimes merging together to form an irregular elevated surface behind transverse depression, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.30-0.34).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, lightly closed anteriorly, short setae sparsely distributed over prosternal process and mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; moderately long (LPE=32-34%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided initially then broadening very slightly at level of ostium before sides converge towards apex in a smooth arc, sometimes (some southern specimens) sides are prominently sinuate, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized with dorso-median channel very faint or absent, short dorsolateral channels present adjacent to ostium; tip protruding and slightly truncate, ostium moderately long, roughly oval, posterior shaped as a very deep triangle with apex at centre, the dorsal flaps extend just past its apical edge when extended; in lateral view, almost evenly and rather strongly curved throughout, thinning towards apex over 15% of length, tip strongly deflexed, flagellum almost invariably broken off.

Similar species

All the following species have prominent black verrucae and feature the characteristic mixture of brown and grey dust as seen in Ta22.

 Ta156 is very similar in general appearance.  It differs in the form of the elytral verrucae which are smaller in Ta156, tend to align in longitudinal rows and are not as dense behind the lateral depression.  Ta156 also usually lacks the central black patches on the pronotum.

 Ta100 is even more similar, but the verrucae also tend to both extend and align longitudinally.

 Ta83 – this is a somewhat darker species, generally slightly smaller, whose distribution of verrucae over the elytra is very similar to that of Ta22, but the verrucae tend to be more peaked rather than flattened as they are in Ta22.  Though black markings are usually present on the pronotum, these do not include a clearly defined pair of obliquely placed oval markings on either side of centre.

 Ta18 has many verrucae that are much longer than wide and align in clear longitudinal series.

 Ta99 – occurs in the alpine regions of southeastern Australia, with specimens always larger than those of Ta22.

 Ta102 – this Tasmanian species differs by its more extended and ridge-like verrucal series and the much coarser puncturation of the pronotum.  Its aedeagus is remarkably similar and the two may be varieties of the same species.

Notes

This is probably T. pustulosa (Blackburn) from comparison with the type specimen.  The specimens from southern NSW are very similar in appearance to those from the northern New England Tablelands.  However, they do tend to have a denser scatter of verrucae, have mostly black inner epipleura and show slight differences in the shape of the adeagus, with the presence of a sinuosity in the form of a marked constriction near the middle in several specimens.

Ta22 occurs most commonly on the peppermint group of eucalypts, notably E. radiata, but has also been recorded from E. pauciflora.
