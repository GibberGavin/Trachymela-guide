Name

Trachymela sp. Ta234

Reference specimen

2025-207, Oxley Wild Rivers N.P., NE New South Wales.

Adult Description

Length: ♂ 7.5-8.3 mm (n=4), ♀ 8.4-8.8 mm (n=3).

Colouration:

  • Head: uniformly mid- to dark-brown with black-tipped mandibles; labrum slightly paler, usually with a longitudinally oriented darker region in centre; antennomeres 1-3 light to mid-brown, 4-11 much darker, sometimes black, individual antennomeres may be darker towards apical end.
    • Pronotum: Brown, concolorous with or slightly darker than head, shade subuniform with only vague darker infuscations; lateral margins usually partially or fully bordered with black.
    • Scutellum: Dark brown, similar shade as pronotal disc; usually broadly edged in slightlydarker brown.
    • Elytra: Brown, concolorous with darkest areas of pronotum; humeral calli similar shade or very slightly darker brown; a common area around elytral summit often with large, dark-brown or black macula. Punctures concolorous with or slightly darker than interstices. Verrucae mostly concolorous with interstices, at most slightly darker, lateral margin usually black.
    • Venter & Legs: Head and usually all or most of prothoracic ventrite black, elsewhere either uniformly mid to dark brown, sometimes with darker areas on parts of thoracic ventrites; abdominal ventrites slightly paler; elytral epipleura usually brown, partially grading towards black, rarely completely black.
    • Cuticular Wax: DONE moderate to thick layer of a mixture of grey and brown ("mottled"), with large longitudinally extended patches of the different shades present, the densest concentrations on the anterio-median depression and as disconnected patches in the elytral and pronotal marginal area.

    Morphology:

    • Head: Punctures fine (puncture diameter ≈ 1–1.5× eye facet diameter), rarely coarser, dense and slightly unevenly distributed. Antennae relatively long, filiform (AL/BL: ♂ 38–42%, ♀ 32.4%).
    • Pronotum: Transversely evenly convex; foveal depression moderate to weak, with foveal tubercle broad and punctured. Discal surface slightly uneven, nitid; puncturation distinctly impressed, similar in size to or slightly larger than head punctures, dense (interspaces 1.5-2 x pd), becoming noticeably coarser laterally. Anterior angles rounded, sub-rectangular; lateral margins smoothly curved, widest near posterolateral angles.
    • Scutellum: Moderately punctate; punctures similar in diameter to adjacent pronotal punctures but sometimes shallower.
    • Elytra: in lateral view, evenly rounded over anterior half, curvature increasing noticeably posteriorly; anteromedian depression faint. Surface rough with mostly convex interstices and small and inconspicuous verrucae, some with a very fine central puncture, that are scattered in anterior half of disc, but aligned and relatively widely spaced along VR2-VR6 in posterior half, some verrucae may align transversely just behind antero-median depression to form short irregular ridges; punctures evenly distributed across disc, obscured by rugose interstices towards apex. Humeral callus continuous or slightly discontinuous with lateral margin. Vertical epipleural extension relatively small (HCE/PW = 0.31-0.34).
    • Venter: Prosternal process narrowest anteriorly, gradually widening posteriorly, closed anteriorly; prosternal process and mesosternum glabrous or with very sparse setae; anterior, median, and posterior trochanters each bearing a single long seta; anterior margin of metaventrite flat, bevelled; inner margin of epipleura lacking setae.

    Male Genitalia (Aedeagus):

  • Dimensions: Moderately long (LPE/BL = 34-37%), slender (WPE\LPE = 0.24-0.25).
  • Dorsal view: shaft narrowing slighly from basal sulcus to centre before widening gradually, widest around anterior of ostium, then sides converge towards apex initially in a smooth curve and then in an almost straight line to form a triangular apex, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized, posterior half of dorsal ostial processes weakly sclerotised, failing to merge smoothly with adjacent dorsal cuticle and typically collapsed into the ostial depression, the anterior extremes diverge to form a sharply triangular posterior extreme to ostium; tip triangular, ostium relatively short, roughly quadrate anteriorly.
  • Lateral view: Shaft moderately and relatively evenly curved throughout, narrowing gradually from about centre towards apex; apical tip deflexed.

Similar species

  • Ta156: This species is very similar in general appearance, with an almost identical distribution of cuticular wax. Ta156 is slighly larger (though the size distributions do overlap). The size and distribution of its verrucae on the elytra is essentially the same as those of Ta234. The aedeagus of Ta156 is broader, it is more strongly curved viewed laterally, and its spatulum is slightly smaller.  The dorsal ostial processes are better defined and sclerotized over their full length in Ta156, joining smoothly with the dorsal surface posteriorly, while those of Ta234 are rather weakly sclerotized and collapsed into the ostial depression.

Notes

  • Taxonomy: Ta234 is clearly very closely related to Ta156, and they may turn out to be the same species.
  • Distribution & Abundance: A single male specimen from Windorah in south-western Queensland (collected under bark) is identical to typical specimens, including in the structure and dimensions of the aedeagus. This record is about 300 km west of the closest known occurrence of Angophora (Atlas of Living Australia, Virtual Herbarium), but it is possible that the species may feed on one or more species of Corymbia.
  • Host Associations: Specimens of Ta234 were collected from the monocalypt Eucalyptus carnea (n=4) and the ironbark E. fibrosa (n=2).
  • Morphological Variation: The northern (Brisbane) specimens closely resembles typical Ta234, though with some small differences in the placement of verrucae.  The aedeagus is very similar (certainly in dimensions and overall shape), but seems more 'delicate' in build with a less-well developed spatular area.  Another difference is the host plant - both the male and female were found on E. fibrosa.
