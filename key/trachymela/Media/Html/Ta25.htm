Name

Reference specimen

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.4-9.6 mm, ♂ 7.1-8.8 mm.

Colour:  head: dark-brown, often with vaguely-defined darker brown or even black patches, these usually behind (and even including) labrum or around fronto-clypeal suture, mandibles either completely black or dark-brown with black tips; antennomeres 1-2 uniformly straw-brown or light-brown, 3-11 darker, each segment grading from light-brown proximally to darker brown distally, rarely uniformly shaded; pronotum: dark-brown, same shade or fractionally darker than rear of head, often with  slight and diffuse variations in shade, with the lateral marginal areas in particular usually fractionally lighter-coloured; scutellum: brown, same shade as adjacent region of pronotum; elytra: dark-brown, mostly of similar shade than darkest region of pronotum with indistinct flecks of slightly lighter colour usually present and scattered so as to give a somewhat “speckled” appearance, the marginal area is often largely of this lighter shade; punctures are similar to or a fractionally lighter shade than interstices, humeral callus similar to surrounding area; venter: dark-brown or blacks, distal leg segments rarely a fractionally lighter shade, inner surface of epipleura black.  Cuticular wax: grey-brown to brown, with a sparse to moderate covering present over most of surface, often more concentrated in patches on head, near lateral margins of pronotum, and on elytra where these denser patches may either be present as 4 longitudinal rows of small clumps (between verrucae of VR3, VR5, VR7 and VR9), sometimes poorly defined, or as more diffuse concentrations throughout but particularly in antero-median depression.

 Head; punctures moderately large (pd~1.5-2xef) and relatively evenly and densely packed. Antennae moderate in length (AL ♂=41-46%BL, ♀=34-36%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression poorly defined or absent, always with a small elevated pimple-like peak near its inner edge, discal area with surface smooth to very slightly uneven, and puncturation clearly impressed with punctures similar in size to those on head, evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~1.5-2pd), becoming much coarser towards lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, broadest about 2/3 of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; unpunctured, rarely one or two small punctures, surface rough.  Elytra; surface mostly evenly curved, usually with a shallow and broad depression just in front of centre, punctures surrounded by very convex interstices, closely spaced and fairly evenly distributed, the thickened interstices sometimes forming small elevated verrucae at their intersections with the larger of these usually having a small central puncture, the whole forming a complex granular surface, surface appears even more granular in posterior third due to closer spacing of punctures, surface slightly discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.33-0.40).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel, lightly closed anteriorly, a light scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, at least posterior trochanters, but often median and anterior, with more than 1 long seta present, often as a clump towards distal end; front edge of metasternum beveled, flat.  Aedeagus; long (LPE=37-40%BL), in dorsal view, approximately parallel-sided for most of central section with just a small widening towards base (WPE ~ 0.21*LPE), narrowing smoothly towards apex with a prominently extended spatulate tip, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized, dorso-median channel usually present but often short and poorly defined, ostium relatively large and oval in shape; in lateral view, quite evenly and continually curved over whole length, the long spatula is deflexed and straight.

Similar species

 Ta149 -  this South-east Queensland species is almost identical to Ta25 in external morphology.  Individuals of Ta149 tend to be slightly smaller and slightly lighter in colouration.  The aedeagus is more slender than that of Ta25, with a more sharply triangular spatula.

 Ta23 - very similar in most respects to Ta25 and co-occurring over a large range.  Specimens tend to be less dark ventrally and have only a single seta on each trochanter.

Notes

The species is widely distributed and quite abundant in many areas of NSW and Victoria, from the New England Tablelands in the north (including around Stanthorpe in far southern Queensland) to the south-eastern corner of South Australia, including Kangaroo Island.  It seems to occur on a wide range of hosts, most commonly on the red-gum (Symphyomyrtus : Exsertaria) or box (Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria) group of species, including Eucalyptus camaldulensis, E. blakelyi, E. prava, E. moluccana, E. melliodora, E. sideroxylon and E. odorata.  Lesser numbers have been found on other Symphyomyrtus species, including E. viminalis.  In the southern Alps, they are often found on the snow gum, E. pauciflora (Eucalyptus : Cineraceae).

The LPE/BL vs BL plot shows two relationships in the sample of 47 individuals dissected, with about 28% of specimens having an aedeagus that is 4% longer than typical specimens of the same size.  These specimens are present over the full range of the species’ distribution and show no apparent correlation with either altitude, host or other morphological traits.  It is possible that there is a correlation with cuticular wax distribution (clumps arranged in rows or not), but not enough dissected individuals have this character recorded for a determination to be made.

The type of Paropsis victoriae Blackburn in the BMNH is a female, 7.8 x 5.7 x 3.5 mm in size, with an A-A distance of 1.2 mm and a scatter of setae on all trochanters.  This makes it a relatively small female of Ta25, but the A-A/BL ratio is within the range for this species.
