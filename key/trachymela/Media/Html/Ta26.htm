Distribution

Ta26

Name

Trachymela castanea (Marsham)

Reference specimen

2003-044a, Yangan, S.E. Qld.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ (9.0)9.4-10.6(11.0) mm, ♂ 8.7-9.5 mm.

Colour:  head: uniformly dark-brown or reddish-brown with black-tipped mandibles; antennomeres 1-2 straw-brown , 3-11 similar or (rarely) fractionally darker; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, sometimes with indistinct slightly darker patches around lateral edges of discal area, rarely [NQ specimens] these are better defined and darker, even black; scutellum: brown, same shade as pronotum, sometimes broadly edged in a fractionally darker shade of brown; elytra: usually uniformly brown, same shade as pronotum, rarely [NQ specimens] with one or more darker brown (very rarely black) patches, often indistinct, one on either side of elytral summit, another positioned at base of elytron just above humeral callus and running posteriorly for a short distance, punctures usually same colour as interstices, sometimes slightly, rarely considerably darker, humeral callus same colour as surrounds, rarely darker brown; venter: mid- to dark brown, rarely with legs a fractionally lighter shade, inside surface of elytral epipleura brown.  Cuticular wax: uniformly light-brown in colour, relatively uniformly (sparsely to densely) distributed over all but lateral pronotal marginal area where it is more concentrated.

Head; punctures variable, usually quite large (pd~2xef) and somewhat unevenly but quite densely packed, sometimes partially obliterated by rugose interstices. Antennae long (AL ♂=48-50%BL, ♀=41-42%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, with a broad but very shallow depression situated behind the outside edge of each eye, puncturation usually clearly impressed, quite evenly distributed and moderately dense in discal area, sometimes partially obscured by uneven interstitial surface, becoming much more coarse at lateral margins.  Much finer punctures are scattered among the larger ones.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, widest close to or just behind midpoint.  Scutellum; puncturation variable, from very lightly to quite densely (very rarely unpunctured), usually more densely anteriorly, with punctures similar in size to those on adjacent area on pronotum or somewhat smaller.  Elytra; surface evenly curved, punctures may be distinct or lost in surface granularity, relatively evenly distributed, with much smaller punctures scattered sparingly on the interstices, several longitudinal rows of pimple-like raised interstices, each with a central puncture, are present though inconspicuous due to being the same colour as the surrounding surface, surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.35-0.40).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, open or lightly closed anteriorly, short setae present at rear of prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; small (LPE=27-28%BL), quadrate, in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided or very slightly sinuate for most of length, then broadening slightly near apex (WPE = 0.35-0.37*LPE) before the sides converge towards apex in a smooth arc, dorsal surface smoothly curved with faint and short dorso-median channel usually present, tip barely protruding, ostium transversely oval; in lateral view, almost evenly curved throughout its length, thinning towards apex, tip slightly reflexed, very thin and spout-like, short protruding segment of flagellum is strongly curved and moderately thick.

Larvae

2nd instar:  Head capsule and legs black.  Prothoracic segments green.  Abdominal segments brown, except terminal segment, which is black.  Very small tubercles on body segments.  3rd instar: (Fig 26a) Head capsule black; legs black, except for extremities of coxa and tibia, which are green.  Prothoracic shield is green with some scattered black streaks and blotches.  Mesothorax and metathorax are green.  Abdominal segments are reddish-brown, with dorsal surface of terminal abdominal segment sclerotized and black.  All except the prothoracic segment have a few very small, raised black tubercles (which are smaller than those in Ta13).  The middle abdominal segments are quite enlarged and bulbous in appearance.

Eggs

brown or red, 2.5-2.6 mm long x 1.2-1.45 mm diameter, with a fairly rough, wrinkled surface with fine puncturation.  Laid in batches of 2-4 eggs per batch, cemented side-by-side, with side one of egg cemented to leaf and covered in a film of liquid.  Debris is often found adhering to the sticky surface of the egg.

Similar species

 Ta13 - can be very difficult (if not impossible) to distinguish from that species as both larvae and adults.  Ta26 is generally somewhat larger than Ta13, and adults are usually uniform in colour, though often some of the raised vittae are lighter-coloured than surface, giving the insect a somewhat streaky appearance.  (The north Queensland specimens usually have some black markings, and black markings are also found on a few specimens in the southern part of the range).  The powdery wax is brownish in colour (never “mustard”).

Notes

The identification of Ta26 as T. castanea (Marsham) is by comparison with material in BMNH (although no type specimen was found).  A frequently encountered species in South-east Queensland and far north-eastern NSW, its distribution extends to north Queensland.  Males are usually considerably smaller than females, with only a small overlap in their size distributions.

As is the case with most, if not all, catenata group species, the sample sex ratio is strongly female biased.  Of the 94 specimens examined, 67% are female, which constitutes a statistically significant departure from a 50:50 sex ratio for the population (exact binomial test, p=0.001).

Most of the 37 host records for this species are from bloodwood species, including Blakella maculata/citriodora/henryi (8), B. bunites (1), Corymbia trachyphloia (2), C. intermedia (1), and unidentified Corymbia or Blakella sp (8), with Angophora accounting for another 3 records.  Thus Corymbia, Blakella and Angophora constitute 62% of host records.  Eucalypus acmenoides (1), E. mediocris (1), E. crebra (2), unidentified stringybark of which the bulk are very likely E. acmenoides (7) and other unidentified Eucalyptus species (3) constitute the remainder of the host records.  Both records from E. crebra are females and may be individuals of Ta13.

The aedeagus of specimen [1997-149] from Yangan, S.E. Queensland, is considerably longer than for typical specimens of Ta26 of that size.  The specimen is rather small for a Ta26, but would be exceptionally large to be either Ta13 or Ta01, with the aedeagus even further from the norm for those species.   In all other respects, including the shape of the aedeagus, the specimen is a typical Ta26.
