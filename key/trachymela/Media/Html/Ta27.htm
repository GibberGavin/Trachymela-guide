Name

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 10.3-12.2 mm, ♂ 10.0-11.7 mm.

Colour:  head: variable, generally uniformly reddish- to dark-brown with black-tipped mandibles, clypeus  sometimes partially or wholly darker or black, extreme posterior black (usually concealed by pronotum); antennomeres 1-3 light-brown, 4-11 usually darker, sometimes considerably so, often grading darker towards distal segments, rarely same shade as 1-3; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, with two large, black patches, one on each side of centre and extending almost to edge of disc, one or more smaller dark markings sometimes also present behind each eye, rarely are the black patches absent; scutellum: uniformly brown, usually darker in shade than adjacent area of pronotum, rarely black; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with interstices between alternative rows of punctures slightly to considerably darker in shade resulting giving the appearance of 5 or so longitudinal dark-brown stripes, in specimens from lower altitude locations, these darker interstices are absent, humeral callus is usually dark brown; venter: very dark brown, rarely black, with distal portions of leg segments sometimes of a fractionally lighter shade, inside surface of elytral epipleura usually brown, sometimes inner edge black, rarely black extends towards outer edge.  Waxy dorsal covering: dense layer of uniform grey to light-brown shade, evenly to somewhat patchily distributed.

 Head; punctures variable, usually quite finee (pd~1.5xef) and rather evenly and quite densely packed, rarely partially obliterated by rugose interstices. Antennae moderately long (AL ♂=43-45%BL, ♀=35-37%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely from centre to about eye level, curvature lessens slightly or (rarely) considerably from there to lateral margin, producing a broad but very shallow depression situated behind the outside edge of each eye, often with a compact central elevation, puncturation clearly impressed, quite evenly distributed and moderately dense in discal area, becoming much coarser towards lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; smooth and unpunctured.  Elytra; surface evenly curved except for a broad shallow depression extending from just below elytral summit towards the discal margin, punctures distinct, large and relatively evenly distributed, with much smaller punctures scattered sparingly on the interstices, roughly longitudinal rows of unevenly spaced, mostly small raised interstices are present though inconspicuous due to being the same colour as the surrounding surface, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.35-0.37).  Venter; Prosternal process quite narrow anteriorly, broadening gradually and evenly towards posterior, closed anteriorly, short setae present at rear of prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; large (LPE=33-35%BL), in dorsal view, broadest at base then usually almost parallel-sided for most of length, sometimes thinning very slightly, then almost imperceptibly broadening near apex before the sides converge towards apex in a smooth arc, dorsal surface smoothly curved with well-defined dorso-median channel, dorsolateral channels well-defined and long, spatula well-defined and protruding, ostium roughly semi-circular; in lateral view, quite evenly curved throughout most of its length, becoming much stronger about 20% from apex, quite thick over all but 30% of distance from apex, from where it thins evenly towards apex, spatula recurved, flagellum thin and invariable broken off.

Similar species

 Ta27 is easy to distinguish from other species due to its size and longitudinal striping on the elytra.  Specimens from the northern part of the distribution, that often lack the contrasting shades on alternate interstices on the elytra (or where the contrast is faint), may be confused with the similar-sized, though not closely related, Ta26.

Notes

The identification as Ta27 as T. graphica is by comparison with the type specimen in the RBINS collection.

The sex ratio is quite strongly female-biased, with 66% of 41 specimens examined being female.

This is a relatively abundant species on the northern Tablelands of NSW and the adjacent coastal areas, extending into Queensland to at least as far north as Childers.  Individuals from the coastal part of the distribution almost invariably lack dark markings on the pronotum and the dark elytral interstices, and the aedeagus lengths as a proportion of body length tend to be in the upper part of the range for the species as a whole.  It is possible that these coastal populations constitute a separate species, but except for these differences in colouration there is little to distinguish between them.  A single male specimen from Mt Moffat N.P. in Central Queensland [in QM] is very similar to the coastal populations in appearance.

The species feeds on Eucalyptus sp., almost exclusively stringybarks.
