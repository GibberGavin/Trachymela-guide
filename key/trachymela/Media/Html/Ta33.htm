Adult Description

Length: ♀ 4.9-6.0 mm, ♂ 4.8-5.2 mm.

Colour:  head: uniformly light-brown to mid-brown with dark-brown or black-tipped mandibles; antennomeres 1-4 straw-brown, 5-11 usually similar, sometimes grading fractionally darker towards apex, if so, often only lateral and/or distal edges of the darker shade; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, often with vague, wispy, slightly darker infuscations; scutellum: brown, same shade as pronotum, usually broadly edged in a slightly darker shade of brown; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, the disc is scattered with small, wart-like elevations that may be same colour as surrounding surface or slightly to considerably darker, transverse depression near centre of disc sometimes with dark-brown or even black patches along its anterior and/or posterior margin or these patches may be joined into a larger dark region; punctures are similar to or a fractionally darker shade than interstices, humeral callus same shade as surrounding area; venter: light- to mid-brown with legs of a similar shade, inside surface of elytral epipleura brown.  Cuticular wax: two colours are present – the antero-median depression of the elytra and the posterior region of elytra is spread with a grey or light-brown dust, while the rest of the insect is scattered with a darker, brown dust at a lower intensity.  This tends to produce quite a characteristic and quickly recognizable pattern.  Only rarely is the whole dorsal surface uniformly brown in colour.

Head; punctures moderately large (pd~1.5-2xef) and quite evenly and quite densely packed. Antennae short, particularly in females (AL ♂=39-42%BL, ♀=31-33%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent or very shallow with epidermis elevated in a rounded pustule near its centre, discal area with surface smooth, rarely slightly uneven and puncturation clearly impressed, evenly distributed and quite dense (spacing~2pd), becoming much coarser at lateral margins, some much finer punctures are scattered among the larger ones.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially straight for about 20% of length before curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; unpunctured or very lightly punctured, more so anteriorly, with punctures a little finer than those on adjacent area of pronotum.  Elytra; surface unevenly curved with a very shallow or distinct broad depression extending from just below elytral summit towards the discal margin, punctures distinct and relatively large, closely spaced and evenly distributed over anterior two-third of surface, surface more granular in posterior third, with much smaller punctures scattered on the interstices, several small elevated verrucae, concolourous with surface or slightly or much darker, scattered about the surface, three groups of these usually present, one group (sometimes quite inconspicuous) in the area between the humeral callus and elytral suture and transverse depression, a second group, often the most prominent, aligned roughly along the posterior edge of depression, while a third consists of more isolated verrucae in posterior half of disc, surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.39-0.42).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel, closed anteriorly, a scatter of long setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, 3-6 setae each on anterior, median and posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, slightly rounded.  Aedeagus; small (LPE=29-33%BL), lanceolate, in dorsal view, broadest at junction with basal section, from where sides converge very gradually towards apex, the angle of convergence becoming fractionally sharper about 25% of distance from apex, dorsal surface smoothly curved with faint dorso-median channel usually present for most of length, tip very slightly protruding, ostium small, oval; in lateral view, almost evenly and quite lightly curved throughout its length, thinning almost imperceptably towards apex before ostium, tip slightly deflexed.

Similar species

Ta06 – this usually slightly larger species has the same characteristic arrangement of dust as Ta33, though the regions of lighter-coloured dust are usually not as clearly defined.  Ta33 tends to have a short row of setae on the trochanters and its aedeagus is considerably longer as a proportion of body length.

Notes

A specimen from west of Stanthorpe does not show the characteristic pattern of cuticular wax for this species but is relatively uniformly covered in solely orange-brown wax.  In almost all other morphological characters it is within the range of variation of other Ta33, though its antennae are slightly shorter than is typical.

Ta33 has been collected from two groups of eucalypts.  Individuals in lower altitude areas have been collected from Eucalyptus crebra and other ironbark species (Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria.  Host at higher altitude locations in northern New South Wales include E. acaciiformis and E. bridgesiana (both Symphyomyrtus : Maidenaria).  Unidentified “box-barked” hosts from this area were likely one of the latter species.
