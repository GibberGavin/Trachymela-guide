Adult Description

Length: ♀ 5.1-6.4 mm, ♂ 4.8-5.8 mm.

Colour:  head: mid-brown, rarely with small and vaguely defined darker brown markings, dark-brown or black-tipped mandibles; antennomeres straw-brown, distal two segments rarely tinged darker-brown ; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, sometimes with small well-defined or diffuse dark brown or black patches situated one on each side just inside level of humeral callus; scutellum: variable, same or darker brown shade as pronotum, very rarely black, often with a darker border; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with a black or dark-brown quadrate area straddling the elytral summit (very rarely absent) and frequently extending to the elevated ridges towards and around scutellum, other dark-brown (rarely tending to black) and diffuse patches are present in apical half of disc and elsewhere, the dark shade in these areas includes the verrucae and parts of the surrounding elytral surface; punctures are similar to or rarely a slightly darker shade than interstices, humeral callus same shade or slightly darker than surrounding area; venter: mid-brown, legs very slightly paler, inside surface of elytral epipleurae brown.  Cuticular wax: light brown, lacking from the smooth patch around elytral summit.  The dust is usually evenly distributed. A small light-gray patch is sometimes present at base of elytron adjacent to and partially or fully surrounding the scutellum, contrasting prominently with the rest of dorsal surface.

Head; punctures moderately large (pd~1.5-2xef) and relatively evenly and densely packed. Antennae short, particularly in females (AL ♂=37-43%BL, ♀=32-34%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent with epidermis often slightly elevated in a small pustule behind eyes, discal area with slightly uneven surface, puncturation usually clearly impressed though sometimes slightly obscured by interstitial granularity, evenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~1.5-2.5pd), gradually becoming coarser towards lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and very gradually towards rear margin, broadest just in front of posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; unpunctured or sparsely and finely punctured, surface smooth, rarely slightly wrinkled.  Elytra; surface evenly to very slightly unevenly curved with a slightly elevated and relatively smooth, usually quadrate area around the elytral summit where punctures are less deeply impressed than elsewhere, elsewhere punctures distinct and moderately large, closely spaced with very convex interstices, evenly distributed over anterior two-third of surface, surface more granular in posterior third, with much smaller punctures scattered sparsely on the interstices, interstices sometimes form thin transverse ridges, elytral surface very vaguely depressed just anterior to centre, verrucae usually small and quite inconspicuous, rarely larger, present primarily in posterior half and scutellar angle, not aligned transversely or longitudinally and most with a small central puncture , surface continuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleurae considerable (HCE/PW=0.36-0.41).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel, open or lightly closed anteriorly, at most a very sparse scatter of setae is present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on each anterior, median and posterior trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled.  Aedeagus; short (LPE=(28.4)30-35%BL), in dorsal view, widest near junction of basal and central sections (WPE = 0.29-0.34*LPE) then narrowing gradually and evenly towards close to apex from where sides converge towards apex in a rounded curve, tip triangular and small, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized, dorso-median channel present but poorly defined, ostial flaps extend forward to a little behind level of tip, ostial opening small and quadrate; in lateral view, prominently bent at about 60˚ near centre, linear on both sides of bend, tip deflexed.

Similar species

 Ta11 - there is no reliable way to distinguish individuals of this species from those of Ta34 without the use of male genitalic characters.  Ta34 is rather variable in colouring and overlaps broadly with Ta11 in that regard.  The antennae of both males and females of Ta11 tend to be slightly longer than those of Ta34, although there is a small overlap.

Notes

This is very likely Trachymela stigma (Blackburn) – see discussion in notes section for that species.  Specimens from southern NSW and Victoria usually have more dark or black colouration on the pronotum and head.  There is enough variation in these characters as well as the shape and size of aedeagus to suggest that at least two species may be lumped under this category.  One specimen in particular, from Tharwa, near Canberra [1999-0327], differs from other specimens of Ta34 (including ones collected at other locations near Canberra) by having a considerably shorter aedeagus, that is parallel-sided for most of its length and has a blunter apex.

Individuals of Ta34 have been collected from Eucalyptus crebra, E. moluccana and unspecified ironbarks, as well as E. andrewsii and unspecified stringybarks.
