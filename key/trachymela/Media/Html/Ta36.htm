Distribution

Ta36

Name

Trachymela sp. Ta36

Reference specimen

1999-0308, Mt Crosby, Brisbane, S.E. Queensland

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 5.0-5.9 mm, ♂ 5.0-5.3 mm (4).

Colour:  head: light- to mid-brown or reddish-brown, mandibles similar with black tips; antennae uniformly straw-brown, less often with antennomeres 9-11 grading fractionally darker with the darker colouration usually confined to distal extremes; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, with two longitudinally-oriented and closely spaced black patches, the inner patch situated behind inner edge of eye is relatively thin, the outer centred behind centre of eye is broader and usually in the shape of a “c”, with open end of “c” facing lateral margin of pronotum, sometimes one of these patches is less clearly defined or even absent, rarely are they merged into a single irregular quadrate patch, very rarely both are absent; scutellum: brown, same shade or fractionally darker than adjacent area of pronotum, usually with a broad border of a darker shade; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with darker brown, rarely black, markings present throughout as small relatively well-defined patches that extend in 3 partially interrupted bands from elytral base towards apex: the most prominent band is in line with the black patches on pronotum and extends roughly along centre of disc, a second runs along elytral suture, usually broadest at elytral summit sometimes where it sometimes extends almost to first band, a third along margin of elytral disc, less often these black markings are present only in anterior half of elytra or very rarely completely absent, thin strip adjacent to scutellum sometimes black, humeral callus variable, same colour as surrounds or fully or partially black; venter: light-brown to mid-, or rarely, dark-brown, with lateral regions of metaventrite and (less often) prosternum noticeably darker, legs light-brown, epipleura brown.  Cuticular wax: often thick, usually evenly distributed layer of light brown to mid-brown colour, with only scutellum and adjoining section of suture to elytral summit more lightly dusted or dust-free; some faint greyish longitudinal streaks may sometimes be present.

Shape: small, evenly rounded, greatest height viewed laterally clearly in front of middle of elytral margin; Head; punctures moderate in size (pd~1.5-2xef) and evenly and densely distributed.  Antennae short (AL ♂=38-40%BL, ♀=33.0%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, very slightly explanate at extreme margins, lacking a foveal depression, an approximately round and very slightly elevated region is usually present in line with each eye, puncturation clearly impressed in discal area, evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and moderately dense (spacing ~2-3pd), becoming much coarser towards lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially straight or very lightly curved for a short length and then curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; surface smooth or slightly rough, lightly punctured with punctures smaller and less densely distributed than on adjacent area on pronotum.  Elytra; surface evenly curved, punctures distinct and evenly and closely distributed (pd~2pd) over almost the whole surface, interstices flat for the most part, never obscuring punctures, with much smaller punctures scattered over them, interstices in posterior half only slightly more convex, with VR3, VR5 (less often) and VR7 often present as longitudinal, pale weals, surface continuous under humeral callus, elytral suture carinate in posterior third, lateral margins straight or slightly rounded, often with anterior third oblique.  Vertical extension of epipleura considerable (HCE/PW=0.36-0.40).  Venter; Prosternal process narrowest anteriorly and gradually widening towards posterior, rarely almost parallel-sided, open or lightly-closed anteriorly, very sparse scatter of short setae at rear of prosternal process and mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; moderate (LPE=30-33%BL), in dorsal view, widest at basal sulcus then narrowing briefly before becoming almost parallel-sided (rarely becoming fractionally wider) to about middle of ostium level, from where the sides converge towards apex in a smooth, convex arc, dorsal surface smoothly curved with well-defined, broad and quite deep dorso-median channel, dorso-lateral channels broad and well developed, with pair of dorsal flaps projecting far towards and almost or just reaching level of apex, the split in the dorsal flaps is a considerable distance from rear of dorso-lateral channels, rear of ostium at 50-56% of distance from basal sulcus to apex, ostium large, tip protruding slightly; in lateral view, bent very slightly near basal sulcus, then almost straight throughout central and apical regions (rarely very slightly curved near apex), thinning towards apex over 20% of length with dorsal flaps often projecting well above upper surface, tip deflexed, flagellum very thin and very loosely curved or straight.

Similar species

 Ta35 – very similar in size and shape as Ta36, but is darker dorsally, its inner epipleural surfaces are black and it usually has longitudinally oriented grey streaks on the otherwise light brown cuticular wax on the elytra.  It occurs on the same Corymbia tessellaris host as Ta36. 

 Ta223 – although very similar to Ta36 in appearance and also found on Corymbia tessellaris, this species is considerably smaller than Ta36 in body length.  Its aedeagus is similar in shape, but relatively much longer and the ostial flaps are much shorter.

 Ta30 – this species is very similar to Ta36 and the two species overlap geographically over a wide range.  Individuals are difficult to separate reliably on external morphological characters, although the aedeagus is very different in the two species.  Ta30 often lacks the black marks on the pronotum and rarely has dark colouration on the elytra that contrasts clearly with the lighter background – any dark colouration on the elytra of Ta30 tends to be very diffuse.  Ta30 feeds on Angophora rather than Corymbia.

 Ta222 – structurally, this species is very similar to Ta36.  It differs in the shape (Ta222 has its ‘greatest height’ close to the centre of elytral margin), its lack of or reduced dark markings on the pronotum and elytra and in the structure of the aedeagus.  The dorsal ostial flaps of Ta222 are considerably shorter than those of Ta36, never getting close to the apex.  The posterior edge of the ostium is often not well-defined in Ta222, usually merging seamlessly with the basal section, but where it is visible, it is much closer to the basal sulcus than in Ta36, and the ventral surface of the aedeagus is more prominently downcurved near the apex than in Ta36.

 Ta175 – little separates this northern Australian species from Ta36, with the structure and dimensions of the aedeagus almost identical.  However, Ta175 has been recorded from Eucalyptus camaldulensis rather than Corymbia.

 Ta221 – much darker in colour.

Notes

The black markings on the pronotum and elytra are present on almost all specimens.

Ta36 has been collected from the ghost gum Blakella tessellaris, with just a single record (likely spurious) from Eucalyptus crebra.
