Distribution

Ta37

Name

Trachymela sp. Ta37

Reference specimen

1998-1122, Bluff River Rd, Tenterfield, N.E. New South Wales

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.6-9.4 mm [13], ♂ 6.7-8.3 mm [5].

Colour:  head: uniformly dark-brown, fronto-clypeal suture sometimes narrowly black, especially laterally, labrum yellow-brown, base and tips of mandibles black; antennomeres 1-6 light-brown, 7-11 grading from light-brown to darker brown, rarely only slightly darker, often with the darker colour confined to distal half of segments; pronotum: dark-brown, same shade as head, with a black patch, variable in size and somewhat irregular in shape but usually longer than broad, on either side of discal area, its outside edge lining up with inside edge of eye, a round black patch, smaller than the other, is situated in centre of each foveal area, lateral edge and lateral parts of anterior edge very dark-brown or black; scutellum: dark-brown, same or slightly darker shade as adjacent region of pronotum, bordered broadly in black, less often completely black; elytra: dark-brown, same shade as pronotum, with 6 longitudinal, relatively thin ridges of black: VR1, extends from just behind and below scutellum and joining elytral suture near summit from where it continues to apex as a black border to suture, VR3 runs from anterior edge between humeral callus and scutellum to a little way from apex, VR5 extends from a little behind and just above humeral callus towards the apex, but terminates some distance before the end of VR3 ridge, VR7 extends from humeral callus to just anterior to and above elytral apex, VR9 extends from just past middle of elytra towards but not reaching apex, and VR10 from below humeral callus to level with beginning of VR9; these ridges are sometimes partially interrupted, especially in posterior half, and have interspersed between them longitudinally running series (VR2, VR4, VR6 and VR8) of small black widely-separated verrucae (the longitudinal ridges consist of closely spaced but isolated verrucae in Carnarvon specimens), punctures often outlined in a fractionally darker shade, humeral callus black; venter: very dark brown, rarely with vaguely lighter-coloured margins on abdominal ventrites, epipleura usually dark-brown, sometimes with some black, rarely wholly black.  Cuticular wax: light-brown and concentrated (though never very densely) between the black longitudinal “ridges”, enhancing the insects striped appearance.

Shape:  rather flattened over much of central section of insect, highest point at or behind middle of elytral margin, ventrites may project a considerable distance below elytral epipleurae.  Head; punctures quite variable in size (pd~1.5-2.5xef) and slightly unevenly but moderately densely distributed. Antennae moderate in length (AL ♂=37-39%BL, ♀=34-36%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; evenly curved transversely over discal region, only extreme lateral margins slightly to quite strongly explanate, with foveal depression very shallow and always with a rounded, more weakly punctured elevation near its lateral edge, puncturation clearly impressed, evenly and moderately densely distributed in discal area with a mix of large and much smaller puncture sizes present, the larger similar in size to those on head, becoming much coarser at lateral margins.  Front margins join the slightly thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin straight for a short distance before curving smoothly and gradually and increasing in thickness towards rear margin.  Scutellum; smooth and lightly scattered with very fine punctures.  Elytra; surface noticeably flattened around highest point, curvature then increases considerably towards apex, with dorsal surface joining ventral at about 90⁰, punctures evenly distributed, large and distinct throughout, with interstices quite flat and smooth, with very fine punctures scattered throughout on the interstices, longitudinal rows of wider but only very slightly elevated interstices are coloured black and arranged as described above, a row of widely separated small verrucae is interpolated between most pairs of neighboring rows of these black interstices, posterior third of elytral suture slightly carinate, marginal sulcus well developed in posterior quarter (much less so in Carnarvon specimens), fringed on its inner side by elevated row of verrucae, surface slightly discontinuous under humeral callus, lateral margins markedly sinuous; vertical extension of epipleura slight (HCE/PW=0.29-0.32).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, closed anteriorly, short setae present at rear of prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate; inner edge of posterior of epipleurae lacking setae.  Aedeagus; relatively short (LPE=29-33%BL), in dorsal view, sides almost parallel-sided from basal sulcus to within 20% of distance from apex, from where sides converge towards apex in almost a straight line, dorsal surface smoothly curved with dorso-median channel absent or very faint, dorsolateral channels short, ostial opening very large, taking up 40% of length from basal sulcus to apex, tip broad and triangular; in lateral view, almost evenly curved throughout most of length, then a slight downward bend shortly before apex, thinning towards apex over about 25% of length, tip deflexed, flagellum relatively thick.

Similar species

Ta241 – separated by a much darker dorsal surface, with the longitudinal ridges replaced by closely and evenly spaced lines of verrucae.  The epipleural surfaces are black in this species.

Notes

Several specimens, mostly collected in the Carnarvon area (central Queensland), are smaller than typical Ta37 and have an elytral surface that is less nitid.  The longitudinal ridges of VR1, VR3, VR5, VR7 and VR9 in these specimens are replaced in a large part by relatively closely spaced but mostly separated verrucae.  VR10 is either obsolete or evident only by its slightly darker colour.  These are very likely to constitute a separate species - however, no male is available for a conclusive determination.

A female collected at Tara, south-east Queensland (on ironbark) is considerably darker than typical Ta37.  In that it resembles Ta241 quite closely but is much smaller.

Ta37 has been collected almost exclusively from box-barked Eucalyptus species (almost certainly Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria) in the south-east Queensland/northern N.S.W populations.  The Carnarvon population has a record from E. conica.
