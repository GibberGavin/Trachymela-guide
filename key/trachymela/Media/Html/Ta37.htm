Name

Trachymela sp. Ta37

Reference specimen

1998-1122, Bluff River Rd, Tenterfield, N.E. New South Wales

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 7.6-9.6 mm, ♂ 6.7-8.5 mm.

Colour:  head: uniformly dark-brown, fronto-clypeal suture sometimes narrowly black, especially laterally, labrum yellow-brown, base and tips of mandibles black; antennomeres 1-6 light-brown, 7-11 grading from light-brown to darker brown, rarely only slightly darker, often with the darker colour confined to distal half of segments; pronotum: dark-brown, same shade as head, with a black patch, variable in size and somewhat irregular in shape but usually longer than broad, on either side of discal area, its outside edge lining up with inside edge of eye, a round black patch, smaller than the other, is situated in centre of each foveal area, lateral edge and lateral parts of anterior edge very dark-brown or black; scutellum: either dark-brown, same or slightly darker shade as adjacent region of pronotum, bordered broadly in black, or completely black; elytra: dark-brown, same shade as pronotum, with 5 longitudinal, relatively thin bands of black: (1) extends from just behind and below scutellum and joins elytral suture near summit from where it continues to apex as a black border to suture, (2) runs from anterior edge between humeral callus and scutellum to a little way from apex, (3) extends from a little behind and just above humeral callus towards the apex, but terminates some distance before the end of band 2, (4) extends from humeral callus to just anterior to and above elytral apex, and (5) is actually made up of 2 bands, running roughly along edge of disc but offset from each other in the middle; these bands are sometimes partially interrupted, especially in posterior half, and have interspersed between them longitudinally running series of small black of verrucae, punctures often outlined in a fractionally darker shade, humeral callus black; venter: very dark brown, rarely with vaguely lighter-coloured margins on abdominal ventrites, epipleura usually dark-brown, sometimes with some black, rarely wholly black.  Cuticular wax: light-brown in colour and concentrated (though never very densely) between the black longitudinal “ridges”, enhancing the insects striped appearance.

Shape:  rather flattened over much of central section of insect, highest point at or behind middle of elytral margin, ventrites may project a considerable distance below elytral epipleurae.  Head; punctures quite variable in size (pd~1.5-2.5xef) and slightly unevenly but moderately densely distributed. Antennae moderate in length (AL ♂=36-39%BL, ♀=34-36%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; evenly curved transversely over discal region, only extreme lateral margins slightly to quite strongly explanate, with foveal depression very shallow and always with a rounded, more weakly punctured elevation near its lateral edge, puncturation clearly impressed, evenly and moderately densely distributed in discal area with a mix of large and much smaller puncture sizes present, the larger similar in size to those on head, becoming much coarser at lateral margins.  Front margins join the slightly thinner lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin straight for a short distance before curving smoothly and gradually and increasing in thickness towards rear margin.  Scutellum; smooth and lightly scattered with very fine punctures (punctures are coarser on Charleville and Eidsvold specimens).  Elytra; surface noticeably flattened around highest point, curvature then increases considerably towards apex, with dorsal surface joining ventral at about 90⁰, punctures evenly distributed, large and distinct throughout, with interstices quite flat and smooth, with very fine punctures scattered throughout on the interstices, longitudinal rows of wider but only very slightly elevated interstices are coloured black and arranged as described above (in the Charleville and Eidsvold specimens these rows are much more broken up into rows of discrete verrucae), a row of widely separated small verrucae is interpolated between most pairs of neighboring rows of these black interstices, posterior third of elytral suture slightly carinate, marginal sulcus well developed in posterior quarter (much less so in northern and western specimens), fringed on its inner side by elevated row of verrucae, surface slightly discontinuous under humeral callus, lateral margins markedly sinuous; vertical extension of epipleura slight (HCE/PW=0.29-0.32).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided, closed anteriorly, short setae present at rear of prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; relatively short (LPE=29-33%BL), in dorsal view, sides almost parallel-sided from basal sulcus to within 20% of distance from apex (diverging gradually from basal sulcus to within 40% of apex, then converging gradually to 20% in Charleville and Eidsvold specimens), from where sides converge towards apex in almost a straight line, dorsal surface smoothly curved with dorso-median channel absent or very faint, dorsolateral channels short, ostial opening very large, taking up 40% of length from basal sulcus to apex, tip broad and triangular; in lateral view, almost evenly curved throughout most of length (curvature much more uneven in Charleville specimen), then a slight downward bend shortly before apex, thinning towards apex over about 25% of length, tip deflexed, flagellum relatively thick.

Similar species

No other species are likely to be confused with Ta37, although the larger Ta55 also has thin black stripes on the elytra.

Notes

Some specimens are very dark brown in colour and the black patches on the pronotum and the lines of verrucae on the elytra are then not very clearly differentiated.  The species is notable for the exceptionally large ostial opening of the aedeagus.

The specimens from Charleville [25-055736] and Eidsvold [1998-0834] have aedeagi that are different to those of the specimens from northern NSW and far SE Queensland, including the type specimen.  They are widest near the centre and then narrow very gradually towards the apex before the sides converging more rapidly to form the triangular spatula, whereas the type specimen has an almost parallel-sided aedeagus before the final convergence of sides.  The A-A/BL ratios for the Charleville and Eidsvold specimens are different to those of the southern specimens, as are the texture of the elytra (which are particularly smooth in the southern specimens).  No males are available from central Queensland, and these specimens do in fact look somewhat intermediate in elytral texture.  There is little doubt that at least two and possibly three species are included in this grouping.  However, specimens are rarely collected, and more material is desirable to make this determination.

Ta37 has been collected almost exclusively from box-barked Eucalyptus species (Symphyomyrtus : Adnataria), including E. populnea and E. conica, with a single specimen from an unidentified ironbark.
