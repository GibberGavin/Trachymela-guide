Adult Description

Length: ♀ 6.8-8.6 mm [17], ♂ (6.8)7.5-8.5 mm [8].

Colour:  head: uniformly mid- to dark-brown, rarely with vague, fuscous areas, labrum similar or darker, mandibles at least with black tips, the black often extending over much larger area; antennomeres 1-4 straw-brown, 5-7 grading darker brown, usually with distal ends darker than proximal, 8-11 dark-brown or black; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, with faint and vaguely defined areas of darker colour often present along anterior and lateral margins; scutellum: variable, most often completely black, sometimes the black is confined to a broad band along lateral margins and (usually) an elevated area in centre of anterior margin, with the remaining surface dark brown or (very rarely) same shade as pronotum; elytra: brown, same shade as or fractionally darker than pronotum, with very prominent black areas that constitute the elevated ridges and verrucae, the most prominent of these is a thick elevated ridge running transversely just behind antero-median depression, in  front of the depression and posterior to the ridge are large black verrucae, some of which are roughly aligned transversely or even connected via thin transverse dark ridges; punctures are similar to or a slightly darker shade than interstices, humeral callus black; venter: head and prosternum black, thoracic ventrites dark-brown or black, grading towards mid-brown at lateral extremes, abdominal ventrites and legs mid-brown, inside surface of elytral epipleurae black.  Cuticular wax: mostly brown over the head and prothorax, with central regions quite dark in shade, tending towards a lighter shade near lateral margins, elytra covered for the most part in a brown dust, with grey dust scattered among this in a complex pattern, including concentrations in along anterior edge of antero-median depression and among the large verrucae in posterior third of elytra.  The prominent black verrucae add to the impression of a complex mix of colours.

Head; punctures variable in size and unevenly but quite densely packed, with the largest (pd~2-3xef) intermixed with much smaller punctures. Antennae short, particularly in females (AL ♂=41-44%BL, ♀=31-35%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent or very shallow with epidermis elevated in a small pustule behind eyes, discal area with surface noticeably uneven and puncturation clearly to more vaguely impressed, evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~2-2.5pd), becoming much more coarse at lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially straight for a short distance before curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, broadest about ¾ of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; surface smooth to quite rough, unpunctured or very lightly punctured with finer punctures than on adjacent area of pronotum.  Elytra; surface evenly to slightly unevenly curved with a well-defined anterio-median depression, punctures distinct and large, closely spaced and evenly distributed over anterior two-third of surface, surface slightly more granular in posterior third, a thick and continuous, rarely partially interrupted, elevated ridge runs transversely from a little behind and below elytral summit almost to elytral margin along posterior margin of antero-median depression, a number of prominent verrucae are scattered about the surface posteriorly to the central ridge and anteriorly to antero-median depression, sometimes some of these are vaguely aligned transversely or longitudinally, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleurae small (HCE/PW=0.30-0.32).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel, lightly closed anteriorly, a sparse scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on anterior and posterior trochanters, usually one, rarely 2 or 3 on median trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, slightly rounded.  Aedeagus; short and broad (LPE=30-32%BL), in dorsal view, sides convex, thinning from base towards centre and then broadening towards apex with widest point (WPE=0.36-0.38*LPE) close to apex, apical edge almost straight with the prominent triangular tip protruding from its centre, dorso-lateral channels well-defined but short, ostial flaps short and broad, a central ridge extends posteriorly from them for much of length, dorso-median channel sometimes present as a faint central depression in that ridge, ostium broad, transverse; in lateral view, bent at about 75⁰ just past base and then straight from there to apex, tip slightly deflexed, flagellum thick.

Similar species

Ta49 – this species has a very similarly sculptured elytral surface, with a prominent transverse ridge just behind antero-median depression.  It differs from Ta46 by being smaller, generally having a smoother and shinier pronotum with rather rounded antero-lateral angles, as well as a very different aedeagus.  It is possible that the smaller female specimens identified as this species may in fact turn out to belong to Ta49.

Ta71 – another species that has the thick, transverse elytral weal, same cuticular wax distribution and similar elytral sculpture.  It also shares the same form of aedeagus with Ta46.  It is, however, smaller, with the size distributions barely overlapping.

Notes

An easily recognized species because of its very prominent transverse “weal”, and the very characteristic form of the aedeagus.  It may possibly be the same species as T. transversalis (Blackburn).

Individuals of Ta46 have been collected from a range of Eucalyptus species, including E. andrewsii (2), E. pauciflora (2), E. radiata (1), E. prava (6), E. viminalis (1) and unidentified smooth-barked species (5).

A female specimen from Marysville, Victoria is placed here provisionally despite some differences from typical specimens of this species.  Its body length is 7.56 mm, on the small side of the size distribution of females of Ta46, but it would be a very large specimen of Ta49.  Its pronotal puncturation is more even and better defined than either of these species.  The prosternum, the central part of the metasternum and the inner epipleral surfaces are black, traits that are the norm in both Ta46 and Ta49.  Its principal difference from these two species is in the sculpture of the elytra, with the median transverse weal, which is uninterupted and rather thick in both Ta46 and Ta49, composed of roughly aligned verrucae in this specimen.  It is clearly closely related to these two species but is very likely to be different.
