Name

Trachymela sp. Ta49

Reference specimen

1998-0367, Basket Creek Swamp, N.E. New South Wales

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 6.7-7.2 mm [7], ♂ 6.5-6.9 mm [2].

Colour:  head: most commonly uniformly mid- to dark-brown, sometimes with black in area between eye and antennal insertion, labrum usually yellow-brown, sometimes darker, mandibles at least with black tips, the black often extending over much larger area; antennomeres 1-5 straw-brown, 6-11 similar or grading fractionally to considerably darker towards apex; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, sometimes with black colouration present along midline and near scutellum and/or as a thin border at lateral margins; scutellum: either completely black, or very dark-brown with the black confined to a broad band along lateral margins or (very rarely) same shade as pronotum; elytra: brown, same shade as or fractionally darker than pronotum, with very prominent black areas that constitute the elevated ridges and verrucae, the most prominent of these is a thick elevated ridge running transversely just behind antero-median depression, in  front of the depression and posterior to the ridge are large black verrucae, two or three of the largest of which are situated a short distance behind central ridge and are usually fully or partially joined to form a short, thick, transverse ridge, punctures are similar to or a slightly darker shade than interstices, posterior third of suture usually bordered in black, humeral callus black; venter: head with at least gula, but more often completely, black, prosternum black, thoracic ventrites dark-brown or black, grading towards mid-brown at lateral extremes, abdominal ventrites and legs mid-brown, inside surface of elytral epipleura black.  Cuticular wax: regions of contrasting brown and white, with head and pronotum, anterior margin of pronotum and elytral disc just posterior to transverse ridge scattered with brown, greyish-white dust is concentrated in antero-median depression, along much of marginal area of elytra and in apical region between verrucae, as well as along part of elytral suture.  The greyish-coloured dust is also present ventrally.

Head; punctures quite large (pd~2-2.5xef) and relatively evenly and densely packed. Antennae short, particularly in females (AL ♂=42%BL, ♀=32-34%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent or very shallow with epidermis almost imperceptibly elevated in a small pustule behind eyes, discal area smooth and shiny, with surface slighly uneven and puncturation clearly to more vaguely impressed, evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~2-2.5pd), becoming much more coarse at lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle (sometimes very rounded), with lateral margin initially straight for a short distance before curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, broadest about ¾ of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; smooth and quite strongly punctured with punctures similar or slightly finer than on adjacent area of pronotum.  Elytra; surface evenly to slightly unevenly curved with a well-defined anterio-median depression, punctures distinct and large, closely spaced and evenly distributed over anterior two-third of surface, surface slightly more granular in posterior third, a thick and continuous, rarely partially interrupted, elevated ridge runs transversely from a little behind and below elytral summit almost to elytral margin along posterior margin of antero-median depression, a number of prominent verrucae are scattered about the surface posteriorly to the central ridge and anteriorly to antero-median depression, sometimes some of these are vaguely aligned or joined transversely or longitudinally, posterior third of elytral suture carinatesurface discontinuous under the prominent humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleurae small (HCE/PW=0.32-0.34).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel in anterior half, lightly closed anteriorly, a sparse scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on anterior and posterior trochanters, usually one, rarely 2 or 3 on median trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, slightly rounded; inner edge of posterior of epipleurae lacking setae.  Aedeagus; relatively short (LPE=33%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided from base to about 25% of distance from apex where it broadens fractionally (WPE=0.295*LPE) before the sides converge very gradually and smoothly to apex, dorso-median channel short, dorso-lateral channels well-defined but short, ostium quite large, spatula large and triangular, with a longitudinal depression along its centre; in lateral view, evenly and gently curved along its full length, tip very slightly deflexed.

Similar species

 Ta46 – this species is larger and generally has an unpunctured or very lighly punctured scutellum as well as a very different, spade-shaped aedeagus, but is otherwise remarkably similar to Ta49.

 Ta71 – another species that is superficially similar because of the prominent, transverse weal on the elytra.  Ta71 differs by its spade-shaped aedeagus, it is slightly smaller than Ta49 and the transverse ridge on the elytron tends to be jagged and sometimes even partially interrupted unlike the very solid ridge of Ta49.  In addition, the gula of Ta71 is brown rather than black.

 Ta61 – superficially similar to Ta49, though with a much thinner transverse ridge and a greater vertical extension of epipleura ((HCE/PW>=0.35).

Notes

This species is one of those with a very prominent, transverse “weal” and is one of the species that could be T. transversalis (Blackburn).  The most northerly of the specimens included from Nipping Gully, near Gayndah, southern Queensland) looks very similar to the others but has a much less sinuous elytral margin.  The Mole River specimen is narrower than the other two.

The only host records for Ta49 are a record from Eucalyptus moluccana, another from E. melliodora (both Symphyomyrtus: Adnataria) and two from “smooth-barked eucalypt”.  A record from Leptospermum is likely not indicative of host preferences.
