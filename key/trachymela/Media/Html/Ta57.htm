Adult Description

Length: ♀ 6.9-8.5 mm [56], ♂ 6.2-7.4 mm [58].

Colour:  head very dark-brown, sometimes black in anterior to fronto-clypeal suture, mandibles dark-brown with black tips; labrum similar but often with slightly paler sides; antennomeres 1-4 light to mid-brown, 5-11 much darker, often black; pronotum: very dark brown or black, similar shade as head, rarely mid-brown, but if so, with 4 dark brown near-circular small black spots present on lateral midline, one level with inside of each eye, the others centred in foveae; scutellum: dark-brown or black, similar or darker than adjacent area of pronotum;  elytra: dark-brown, same or often a slightly lighter shade as pronotum, in the latter case scattered with a network of black areas that coincide with pustules and raised interstices, a transversely aligned concentration of these often situated behind transverse depression and a large black raised area is situated in centre of edge of disc, punctures are similar in colour as interstices, humeral callus black; venter: black, inside surface of elytral epipleura black.  Cuticular wax: elytra with moderately thick layer of brown wax, interspersed with grey in two distinct patterns (1) the grey present as thick, conspicuous patches interspersed with the brown, sometimes without apparent pattern, but more often the grey patches form longitudinally aligned thick interrupted streaks along all or some of VR3, VR5 and VR7 and VR9 with a broad gap usually present just posterior to antero-median depression, or (2) the grey is present as thin, longitudinal, interrupted lines along rows of verrucae, particularly VR3, VR5, VR7 and VR9; pronotum unevenly scattered in brown, sometimes with grey patches also present.

Shape: slightly flattened, greatest height viewed laterally is just in front of middle of elytral margin; Head; quite coarsely punctured (pd~2-2.5xef), somewhat unevenly. Antennae short (AL ♂=31-38%BL, ♀=30-34%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent with a clearly defined and almost round elevated area in middle in its stead, puncturation on disc clearly impressed, moderately sized, noticeably larger anteriorly, evenly and moderately densely distributed in discal area, becoming much coarser from level of inner edge of eye to lateral margins, interstices smooth and shiny in discal area.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; sparsely to densely punctured, often more densely near anterior edge, with punctures similar in size to those on adjacent area of pronotum.  Elytra; surface evenly curved transversely, lightly curved longitudinally in anterior half, then slightly more strongly curved, a very shallow depression extends across surface just in front of middle, punctures very large and distinct and relatively evenly distributed between the verrucae, verrucae not aligned in longitudinal series, individual verrucae vary in size and elevation with smaller often more elevated and prominent than larger ones, usually more concentrated behind transverse depression, sometimes merging in places to form an irregular elevated surface, surface continuous or very slightly discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.37-0.39).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided and broad, rarely broadest anteriorly, open or (rarely) lightly closed anteriorly, setae scattered over prosternal process and mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; moderately long (LPE=27-32%BL), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided over most of its length, sometimes converging  almost imperceptibly over central section towards anterior, rarely very slightly sinuate, then converging towards apex in a smooth arc, dorsal surface lightly sclerotized with dorso-median channel very faint, short dorsolateral channels present adjacent to ostium; tip protruding and almost triangular, ostium short, roughly oval, posterior shaped as a very deep triangle with apex at centre, the dorsal flaps extend to its apical edge when extended; in lateral view, lightly and quite evenly curved throughout, thinning towards apex over 15% of length, tip strongly deflexed, flagellum almost invariably broken off.

Similar species

The four species Ta50, Ta51, Ta57, Ta83 and Ta145 are clearly very closely related and can be difficult to distinguish if male genitalia are not available.  Ta50, Ta83 and Ta145 have a type of sculpture that is more robust, with verrucae more elevated and prominent than those of Ta57. These three species also have epipleurae with a smaller vertical extension (HCE/PW<=0.35) than Ta57 (HCE/PW>=0.37).

Ta50 is found in North Queensland.  It has very robust lateral lobes on the mesothoracic process and its aedeagus has a sharply pointed spatula.

Ta51 is also a northern Queensland species and is very similar in external morphology to Ta57.  It tends to be slightly paler than Ta57, both dorsally and ventrally, and the verrucae are sparser, larger and more discrete though some specimens are very similar to those of Ta57.  The antennae of Ta51 are usually completely light brown.  Specimens tend to be smaller in size, and its aedeagus, though of the same shape as that of Ta57, is proportionally smaller.

 Ta83 has larger males, but females are about the same size as those of Ta57. Its prosternal process is usually broader and prominently expanded anteriorly.  The aedeagus of the two species are broadly similar, but that of Ta83 is widest near the apex and the spatula is prominently pointed.

Ta145 has a prosternal process whose shape is very variable, usually being narrowest anteriorly, but that of some specimens can be broadest anteriorly.  Its aedeagus has almost the identical shape to that of Ta57 but is proportionally somewhat longer.  Ta145 also tends to prefer ironbark species.

Ta29 is similar in size and general appearance, though lacking the contrasting “dust” colours.    The two species are very similar in their elytral sculpture.  However, Ta29 has more compact antennae, less rounded pronotal front angles and less coarse punctures on the pronotal disc.

Notes

Ta57 is a relatively abundant species over a wide area in the northern half of eastern Australia.  It is usually easy to recognize in the field by its small size and the presence of cuticular wax of contrasting colours.  The males are considerably smaller than the females, with almost no overlap in the size distributions.  The species has been mostly collected from ironbark species (86 records), including Eucalyptus crebra (53), E. fibrosa (8) and E. melanophloia (3).  West of the Great Dividing Range, it is more usually collected from box species (Symphyomyrtus: Adnataria), with records from E. populnea (11), E. moluccana (1) and a few records from unspecified box species (6).  A few scattered records from stringybark and Angophora are likely incidental.

There is notable variation in several characters in this species.  One of the more striking ones is the distribution of cuticular wax.  Typical of the species is for it to be arranged in a random-seeming patchwork of grey with interspersed smaller amounts of brown (for example, specimen [2017-236]).  Some specimens, however, have a much more regular arrangement.   Two different arrangements are present: (1) most commonly, the greyish wax is present as small spots arranged in longitudinal series along the rows of verrucae (for example, specimen [2022-132), or (2) the greyish wax is present as extended longitudinal streaks, interspersed with bands of either brown wax or wax-free surface.  The latter pattern is also reflected in the arrangement of verrucae, which are more regularly spaced within the series in these specimens.  There is a tendency for specimens collected from the box Eucalyptus populnea to present with the latter pattern, while those on ironbark (such as E. crebra) tend to show the former pattern, though this is not completely consistent.  It is likely that these two cuticular wax patterns indicate the presence of two distinct species grouped within Ta57.

The large spread of the LPE/BL values and the presence of a few individuals with noticeably narrower aedeagi (WPE<0.6 mm), where the bulk of specimens of Ta57 have WPE values between 0.62 and 0.66, also may indicate the presence of more than one species in this grouping.  However, these differences in aedeagus measurements do not seem to correlate with the different patterns of cuticular wax.

The length of the antennae of the males also forms two groups, one being >= 36%BL, the other group <35%BL.

A few specimens from a north Queensland location (Lappa), all obtained from the ironbark Eucalyptus cullenii and all teneral, are included in this grouping.

The specimen [2020-181a] from Miles in southern Queensland has a slightly more pointed apex, with the sides of the spatula very slightly concave.  It was caught off a box species (not E. populnea) and is also possibly different.  A specimen from Charleville is well within the range of variation in essentially all characters, though the aedeagus is more gently rounded apically than is typical.
