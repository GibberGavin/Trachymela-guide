Ta71

Name

Trachymela sp. Ta71

Reference specimen

2002-059b, Yeleba State Forest, Miles, Queensland

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 5.8-6.6 mm (3), ♂ 5.8-6.3 mm (2).

Colour:  head: uniformly mid- to dark-brown, often with anterior margin of clypeus darker or black, labrum yellow-brown or partially or fully black, mandibles at least with black tips, the black often extending over much larger area; antennomeres 1-6 straw-brown, 7-11 similar, sometimes with distal extremes darker than proximal; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, with at least posterior half of lateral margin black (the Narrandera specimens lack the black margin, but have a small, black spot in foveal area that is lacking in the Yuleba specimens); scutellum: brown, similar shade to pronotum, often with a thin margin slightly darker; elytra: brown, same shade as or fractionally darker than pronotum, with very prominent black areas that constitute the elevated ridges and verrucae, the most prominent of these is a black elevated ridge, usually entire though often slightly irregular, running transversely just behind antero-median depression from just a little below greatest height almost to lateral margin, in  front of the depression and posterior to the ridge are large black verrucae, some of which may be aligned longitudinally or may even form roughly aligned, oblique transverse rows, a usually uninterrupted black ridge extends from anterior margin about halfway between scutellum and humeral callus towards central depression, area of suture around greatest height is black, posterior third of elytral suture bordered in black (not so in Narrandera specimens), humeral angles are black and this black extends posteriorly for some distance along elytral margin (absent in Narrandera specimens) ; punctures are similar to interstices, humeral callus black; venter: head mostly dark brown with small black areas posteriorly on either side of gula, prosternum black, thoracic ventrites dark-brown or black, often grading towards mid-brown at lateral extremes, abdominal ventrites and legs lighter brown, inside surface of elytral epipleura black.  Waxy dorsal covering: greyish in colour and mostly lightly sprinkled over surface, but there is a higher concentration in the antero-median depression (description applies to Narrandera specimens).

Head; surface uneven, punctures variable in size and unevenly but quite densely packed, with the largest (pd~2-3xef) intermixed with smaller punctures. Antennae short, particularly in females (AL ♂=41-42%BL, ♀=32-35%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent or very shallow with epidermis elevated in a small pustule behind eyes, discal area with surface noticeably uneven and puncturation clearly to more vaguely impressed, evenly to very slightly unevenly distributed and relatively dense (spacing~2-2.5pd), becoming much more coarse at lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with much thicker lateral margin initially straight for a short distance before curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin, broadest at more than ¾ of distance to posterio-lateral angle.  Scutellum; surface rough, unpunctured or very lightly punctured with much shallower punctures than on adjacent area of pronotum, or, much more strongly and deeply punctured (Narrandera specimens).  Elytra; surface evenly to slightly unevenly curved with a well-defined anterio-median depression, punctures distinct and large, closely spaced and evenly distributed over anterior two-third of surface, surface slightly more granular in posterior third, a prominent transverse ridge and many verrucae present with positions as described in “colour” section above, posterior third of elytral suture prominently carinate on each side, surface slightly to strongly discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.35-0.37).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided and quite broad in anterior half, lightly closed anteriorly, a sparse scatter of setae present along prosternal process and on mesosternum, a single long seta present on anterior and median trochanters, usually 2 or 3 (rarely 1) on posterior trochanters; front edge of metasternum beveled, slightly rounded.  Aedeagus; short and broad (LPE=26-29%BL), in dorsal view, sides concave, thinning from base towards centre and then broadening towards apex before becoming parallel again, with widest point (WPE=0.43-0.44*LPE) close to apex, apical edge concave on each side of middle, with the prominent triangular tip protruding from its centre, dorso-lateral channels well-defined but short, ostial flaps quite long and broad, not separated until close to apex, dorso-median channel usually absent or very faint, ostium broad, transverse; in lateral view, bent at about 45⁰ just past base and then straight from there to apex, tip slightly deflexed, flagellum thick.

Similar species

 Ta49 – this species has a similarly sculptured elytral surface, with an even more prominent transverse ridge just behind antero-median depression.  It differs from Ta71 by being slightly larger, the posterior half of ventral surface of head being completely black, the much more robust elytral verrucae and its very different aedeagus.

 Ta61 – this species is very variable, as is Ta71, with the variation proving a large amount of overlap in characters and making differentiation of these species difficult if male genitalia are not available.  All the dissected males of Ta61 (i.e., clearly attributable to that species) have the inner epipleural surface brown, while all those of Ta71 have black inner epipleural surfaces.  Most specimens of Ta71 lack the black pronotal markings that are present on most specimens of Ta61.  Note that both species are sometimes present in the same location.

Notes

The aedeagus is almost identical to that of the much larger Ta46, which this species also resembles very closely in general appearance (especially in the head and pronotum being a noticeably lighter shade than the elytra).  The Narrandera specimens (2 females) do not have as noticeable a difference in shade between pronotum and elytra and differ in several other characters (indicated in the description).  It is likely that they belong to a different species.

Individuals of Ta71 have been collected from unidentified, smooth-barked Eucalyptus species.
