Name

Trachymela sp. Ta73

Reference specimen

2011-074, Carpie Puntha Hill, Eyre Peninsula, South Australia.

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 11.9-14.1 mm [6], ♂ 12.3-12.9 mm [3].

Colour:  head dark reddish-brown, either completely or with variable areas of black, most frequently confined to area around fronto-clypeal suture and on clypeus, sometimes extending onto frons, rarely almost all of clypeus and frons black, labrum straw-brown, mandibles substantially black or at least with black tips; antennomeres 1-3 straw-brown, 4-5 grading towards dark-brown or black, 6-11 dark-brown or black, often with only leading edge of antennomeres black; pronotum: brown, same or fractionally darker shade as head, often with fuscous, vaguely defined and slightly darker patches, especially in area of disc, anterior margin often black; scutellum: dark brown, similar or slightly paler or darker than adjacent area of pronotum, with margins broadly lined with darker brown, rarely completely black;  elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with black or darker brown ridges and small verrucae scattered over surface in a way that is very variable within the species, commonly, at least one or two ridges, sometimes in the form of closely spaced verrucae, extend from anterior margin between scutellum and humeral callus, with one of these often continuing as a row of verrucae almost to apex,  other small dark-brown or black verrucae are scattered over disc with the most prominent of them usually within longitudinal series that are sometimes emphasized by black interstices between verrucae, dark-brown or black short transverse ridges may also be present, suture often narrowly black from scutellum to at least elytral summit, with its thickness greater around summit, humeral callus similar to surrounds or much darker; venter: mid-brown to dark brown, legs sometimes slightly paler, abdominal sternites similar or fractionally paler, inside surface of elytral epipleura black.  Waxy dorsal covering: light-brown or greyish, distributed lightly and rather evenly over whole of dorsal surface, with larger verrucae and elytral suture dust free and making a prominent contrast with rest of surface.

Shape: slightly flattened (height=0.40-0.47%BL) almost evenly curved from scutellum to at least 80% of distance to apex where curvature usually increases noticeably, especially in males, greatest height viewed laterally well in front of middle.  Head; punctures variable, most often relatively fine (pd~1.5-2xef), but often a mix of sizes may be present from 1xef to >3xef, distribution relatively uniform. Antennae moderately long (AL ♂=41-45%BL, ♀=38-41%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; evenly curved transversely over discal area, with a deep foveal depression behind eyes and a strongly explanate margin, foveal elevation absent, discal area smooth or slightly rugulose, sometimes with a small elevation at each lateral boundary, sometimes with a thin, unpunctured ridge along centre line, punctures clearly impressed and evenly and densely distributed in discal area, size similar or fractionally larger than those on head becoming considerably coarser from foveal depression to lateral margins.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded but tight right-angle, with lateral margin initially straight for about half their length, then curving smoothly towards rear margin.  Scutellum; surface usually smooth and shining, less often more coarsely textured, unpunctured.  Elytra; surface curved evenly over disc transversely, often prominently explanate at margins, whole surface, including margins, very granular with punctures almost hidden by a network of raised interstices over much of the surface, many smooth, elevated verrucae, some with small central punctures, some extended into short ridges are scattered over elytral surface (see colour above for arrangement), posterior third of elytral suture carinate, surface discontinuous under humeral callus; posterior half of marginal area prominently concave, separated from anterior half by a broad transverse ridge.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.33-0.36).  Venter; Prosternal process narrowest anteriorly to (rarely) almost parallel-sided, closed anteriorly, short setae sparsely distributed over prosternal process and mesosternum, 1 or 2 setae on anterior and 1 on median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate; inner edge of posterior of epipleura lacking setae.  Aedeagus; very long and narrow (LPE=37-44%BL, WPE/LPE=0.20-0.23), in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided, widest near basal sulcus and near anterior end of ostium, with the sides between these points very slightly sinuous so that the narrowest point is around middle of central section, from the widest point anteriorly sides converge smoothly towards apex in a tight arc, dorso-median channel very faint past ostium, dorsolateral channels broad but short resulting in two relatively narrow ostial flaps protruding far towards apex in the tear-shaped ostial depression; spatula relatively large, tip small and smoothly triangular; in lateral view, curved sharply through about 45⁰ near base then ventral surface almost straight to apex, dorsal surface straight and parallel to ventral surface from basal sulcus to rear of ostium, then converging evenly towards ventral surface, tip deflexed.

Similar species

Ta235 – a species that is exceedingly close in appearance but occurs not in the mallee areas of southern Australia but in S.E. Queensland west of the Great Dividing Range.  The aedeagus of the two species is quite similar structurally but differs in dimensions.

Ta151 - this species, which occurs in south-western Australia is very similar morphologically and clearly closely related, but is even larger, with individuals all exceeding 14mm in length.

Notes

This is one of the largest of the Trachymela species, exceeded in body length only by the closely related Ta151 (T. explanata) from Western Australia.  It is found on Eucalyptus sp. in mallee areas, with the only (though somewhat doubtful) specific record being from E. oleosa.
