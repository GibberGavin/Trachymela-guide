Name

Trachymela cribrata (Blackburn)

Reference specimen

2011-166, Pooncarie, S.W. New South Wales

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 10.0-10.3 mm (3), ♂ 9.4-10.3 mm (5).

Colour:  head: frons mid- to reddish-brown, clypeus, and region adjacent to eyes black, labrum straw-brown, mandibles usually completely black, sometimes reddish-brown with black base and tips; antennomeres 1-3 straw-brown, 4-11 similar or slightly darker in shade, individual antennomeres usually with anterior edge darker; pronotum: brown, similar shade as head, posterior half of lateral edge and most of anterior edge behind head black, sometimes the black extends to most or even all of anterior and lateral edges; scutellum: dark brown or (rarely) black, always darker than adjacent area of pronotum, sometimes broadly edged in an even darker shade; elytra: brown, similar shade or (rarely) fractionally paler than disc of pronotum, punctures similar in shade to interstices, several longitudinal series of small black verrucae are present on elytral disc, some of these black spots rarely (very frequently in Western Australian specimens) extended longitudinally, humeral callus black; venter: mid-head substantially black, thoracic and abdominal segments dark-brown, rarely mid-brown, with areas around the midline of thoracic segments often slightly darker, legs similar or fractionally darker, inside surface of elytral epipleura dark-brown or black.  Waxy dorsal covering: light-brown and somewhat unevenly distributed, with a relatively thick coating on head and towards the lateral margins of pronotum; on the elytra, it is usually scattered much more sparsely though fairly evenly; dots of lighter-coloured, greyish wax are present in the spaces between the black verrucae within the longitudinal series (in the Western Australian specimens these dots are very sparse or even absent and less constrained within the series).

Shape: quite evenly rounded and almost hemispherical (H/BL ♂=0.44-0.47, ♀=0.46-0.50), highest point around or a just in front of middle of elytral margin.  Head; punctures moderately sized (pd~2-2.5xef) and quite evenly and densely packed.  Antennae moderately long (AL ♂=40-43%BL, ♀=37-38%BL), A4≥A3, filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely, foveal depression absent or very weak with foveal elevation absent, puncturation clearly impressed, similar in size or fractionally coarser than those on head, sometimes slightly uneven in size, closely and evenly packed together, rarely with a few smooth flat interstices interrupting, punctures become considerably coarser from about foveal area towards lateral margin.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin initially almost straight for about 25% of length, then curving smoothly and gradually towards the much thicker rear margin.  Scutellum; surface usually uneven, with a number of coarse but shallow punctures.  Elytra; surface evenly curved, punctures distinct in anterior two-thirds, less so in posterior third where they become lost in the closely-spaced granular interstices, relatively evenly distributed, a scatter of smaller punctures on the interstices, interstices raised and form an interrupted network of short, thin ridges, many running transversely, several longitudinal series of small, raised, black and quite evenly spaced verrucae are present, individual verrucae are most often round but a few may be extended slightly longitudinally, verrucae usually fewer on portion of series anterior to antero-median depression, surface discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura moderate (HCE/PW=0.32-0.36).  Venter; Prosternal process narrow anteriorly or almost parallel-sided, closed anteriorly, short setae present sparsely over prosternal process and mesosternum, one to several setae on anterior and median trochanter, one on posterior trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; quite long, round in cross-section and thick (LPE=37-41%BL); in dorsal view, almost parallel-sided from basal sulcus to about 75% of distance to apex with sides smooth or slightly uneven, from there convergence of sides towards tip is uneven, rapid initially then more gradually before finally increasing in rate to form a triangular spatula, dorsal surface smoothly curved with a well-defined and long dorso-median channel, dorsolateral channels relatively short and along lateral margins, dorsal flaps large with anterior edges oblique, the most anterior point at or close to centre line, ostial opening large, tip strongly protruding; in lateral view, curved slightly just anterior to basal sulcus, then almost straight to about 25% from apex where ventral surface bends through almost 45⁰ and its thickness decreases towards apex, tip prominent and deflexed.

Notes

The determination of this species as Trachymela cribrata (Blackburn) has been made by comparison with the male type in the BMNH (length, 9.75mm) and with the original description.  A second specimen [length: 9.7 mm and with labels: “comp. w. type”, “Jacoby coll. 1909-28a”, “Yorkton”(?), “spilota Chap”] in the same tray at the BMNH has apparently been compared with the type of  spilota Chapuis by Jacoby.  It appears very much like Blackburn’s cribrata type, but not at all like the type specimen of spilota (Ta167)!  Its aedeagus has at some time been dissected out and its shape and size confirm its identity as Ta78, i.e., T. cribrata.  The synonymy of T. cribrata with T. spilota is clearly in error.

The species is one of a group of relatively large species found in the mallee regions of southern Australia.  Ta78 is distinguished by the longitudinal series of black verrucae that are present on the elytral disc.

The Western Australian specimens [2019-071/072] are likely different, but more material is needed to say so definitively.  Although black, interrupted longitudinal ‘series’ are present on the elytral disc in these specimens, these are not as clearly defined by discrete elevated verrucae as on eastern Australian specimens, but tend to be composed of extended short lines.  The white spots of cuticular wax are present in these WA specimens, but are sparser and less obviously placed within the longitudinal series.  The aedeagus of the two varieties differs in the shape of the spatula, which is broader and more triangular in the Western Australian specimen (this is clear even though the specimen in question is slightly tenereal and its aedeagus somewhat distorted).  The aedeagus of the Western Australian specimen is about 5% longer than would be expected for a specimen of its size.  Finally, the host of the Western Australian specimen is in a different group of eucalypts to the eastern Australian specimens (see below).

Ta78 has been recorded from unspecified mallee eucalypt species, including Eucalyptus conglobata conglobata and E. dumosa (both Symphyomyrtus : Dumaria, series Rufispermae).  The specimens from Western Australia were found on E. terebra (Symphyomyrtus : Bisectae, subsection Glandulosae) and possibly E. urna (Symphyomyrtus : Bisectae, subsection Destitutae).
