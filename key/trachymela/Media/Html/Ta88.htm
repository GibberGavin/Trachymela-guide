Distribution

Ta88

Name

Trachymela strigosa (Chapuis)

Reference specimen

2017-076, Pinaroo, South Australia

Adult Description

Length: ♀ 6.9-8.9 mm, ♂ (5.3)6.0-8.1 mm.

Colour:  head: mid-brown, almost always with fronto-clypeal suture at least partially outlined in black, rarely the black area is broader in centre, even more rarely it is completely absent, labrum yellow-brown, mandibles black-tipped; antennomeres 1-3 straw-brown, 4-11 similar or fractionally darker, outer 2-5 antennomeres usually with distal extremes dark-brown or (rarely) black; pronotum: brown, same shade as head, almost always with a roughly quadrate black patch on each side in the foveal area, sometimes extending laterally to almost margin on one side and onto discal area on the other, very rarely completely brown; scutellum: brown, same shade as pronotum; elytra: brown, same shade as pronotum, with 4 thin and sometimes partially interrupted (rarely completely absent) longitudinal bands of black or dark brown, one of which extends from just below humeral callus along inside edge of disc and continues the black area on pronotum, another extends from the anterior margin, halfway between scutellum and humeral callus almost to apex where it joins with the first, the other two are spaced equally between these (i.e., VR3, VR5, VR7 and VR9), usually the dark colour in these bands is confined to the larger verrucae along them, sometimes it extends to the interstices between them, sometimes the anterior margin of elytra is black between these bands; elytral margin narrowly bordered in black from summit to apex; humeral callus black, punctures same shade as interstices; venter: completely orange-brown, sometimes with parts of ventrites, especially margins, slightly darker, legs similar shade, epipleura light-brown, very rarely with some black along inner edge.  Cuticular wax: uniformly light-brown or greyish-brown and evenly or slightly unevenly distributed layer that can be quite dense but does not conceal the larger, black verrucae on elytra.

Shape: uniformly curved and moderately high, elytral summit clearly in front of middle of elytral margin; Head; puncture size variable (pd~1.5-3xef), mixed sizes often present, and slightly evenly but densely packed.  Antennae moderate to long (AL ♂=40-49%BL, ♀=33-36%BL), filiform.  Pronotum; almost evenly curved transversely over discal area, with foveal depression usually absent, sometimes present as a shallow crease, a small, round pimple-like elevation present behind centre of each eye, puncturation usually clearly impressed, similar in size as on head, very evenly distributed and dense in discal area, interstices in discal area relatively smooth, never obscuring punctures, punctures becoming much coarser towards margin.  Front margins join lateral margins in a smoothly rounded right-angle, with lateral margin usually straight for some distance before curving smoothly and gradually towards rear margin.  Scutellum; surface slightly rough, lightly to densely punctured, punctures usually similar in size or somewhat smaller than those on adjacent surface of pronotum.  Elytra; surface almost evenly curved in lateral view, evenly curved transversely throughout with at most very slightly explanate margins, punctures moderately large and relatively evenly distributed but sometimes partially obliterated by rugose interstices, 4 longitudinal series (VR3, VR5, VR7 and VR9) of small, round verrucae, some sometimes slightly extended laterally, rarely poorly defined, some with a very fine central puncture, are present on disc, other, usually smaller verrucae are scattered between these rows, interstices variable, sometimes relatively smooth, more often more convex, tending to obliterate small verrucae, with a few fine transverse wrinkles often present near middle of discal margin, one of these sometimes extending some distance onto disc, surface slightly discontinuous under humeral callus.  Vertical extension of epipleura large (HCE/PW=0.36-0.41).  Venter; Prosternal process almost parallel-sided before widening towards posterior, open or closed anteriorly, a scatter of short setae on prosternal process, absent from mesosternum, a single seta on anterior and median trochanter; front edge of metasternum beveled, truncate.  Aedeagus; moderate-sized (LPE=30-38%BL), in dorsal view, either almost parallel-sided for much of length to slightly sinuate with sides concave, always clearly broader at ostium from where sides converge towards apex in a smooth arc, dorsal surface with well-defined dorso-median channel and dorsolateral channels resulting in two long ostial flaps protruding far towards apex in the tear-shaped ostial depression; tip small and smoothly triangular; in lateral view, curved sharply through about 45⁰ near base then ventral surface almost straight before an almost imperceptible downward bend near apex, dorsal surface straight for about half this distance, then converging evenly towards ventral surface, tip deflexed.

Similar species

Ta86 – this species also feeds on mallee eucalypts and overlaps in distribution with Ta88. It is is very similar in its possession of four longitudinal rows of verrucae on the elytra, but it lacks the large quadrate black patch on the pronotum behind the eyes that is present in Ta88 and it has black inner epipleura.  Its humeral callus is closer to the elytral margin (HCE/PW<0.35) than is the case for Ta88 (HCE/PW>0.35).  The aedeagi of these two species are very similar in shape, but that of Ta86 has a longer spatula and its ostial depression tends to be a clearly defined oval.  Most specimens of Ta86 have 4-5 prominent ‘dashed’ lines of greyish cuticular wax on the elytra.

Ta110 – some specimens of this species can be rather similar to Ta88 in both size and in the presence of four series of black verrucae on the elytra.  The aedeagus is almost identical in shape and size to that of Ta88.  However, the pronotum almost invariably lacks the large black patches behind the eyes, while the verrucae of Ta110 are frequently much more extended laterally over much of the elytral disc.  Note, however, that Ta110 is currently known from just a few specimens with only a single dissectible male and the morphological characters that separate these closely related species are not well characterized.

Ta74 – this species shares the black patch in foveal areas and the longitudinal dark banding of the elytra, but is always larger and lighter-coloured, and its aedeagus is much larger proportionally and differently shaped.

 

Notes

This species is identified as T. strigosa (Chapuis) by comparison with the holotype of the species in the RBINS.  The type locality for strigosa is given as “Parao River”, presumably Paroo River.  The most northerly known specimens of Ta88 have been collected north-east of Ivanhoe, about 200km from the Paroo River, where they were found in large numbers.  Records in the Atlas of Living Australia (ala.org.au) indicate that at least two of its known host species (see below) are widespread in the lower Paroo River area.

The body lengths of males of this species span an exceptionally large range.  A male from Calgoorlie, Western Australia (5.29mm) and three collected at Euston, in south-western New South Wales (5.75, 5.85 and 6.38 mm), are particularly small, with the next smallest male (collected elsewhere) at 6.62mm.  However, other males (and females) collected at each of these locations on the same occasions are more typical in size and there is no consistent difference in other morphological characters between these small specimens and others.

There are also slight variations in the shape of aedeagus across individuals of this species: for example, the apical curvature in [2017-380] is more gradual than in typical specimens.  The LPE/BL relationship is consistent with a single species being represented here with the possible exception of the Western Australian specimens, whose aedeagi are slightly shorter than expected for specimens of that size.

A specimen from the Nullarbor Plains [PGK/5680] completely lacks the black markings on the pronotum and its inner epipleura are substantially black (only a narrow lower margin is brown).  Its ostium is noticeably more extended longitudinally than is typical of Ta88 (including other specimens from Western Australia).   However, both the aedeagus LPE/BL ratio and elytral margin width (HCE/PW=0.38) are as would be expected for a specimen of Ta88 of its size and it is tentatively placed with that species.

Ta88 occurs on mallee eucalypts.  Eucalyptus dumosa (Symphyomyrtus : Dumosa), and  E. socialis and E. oleosa (both Symphyomyrtus : Bisectae) have been recorded as hosts.
