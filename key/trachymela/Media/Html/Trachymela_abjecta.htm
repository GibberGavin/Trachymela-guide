Trachymela abjecta

Name

Trachymela abjecta (Blackburn, 1896)

Type specimen

Location: BMNH

Label data: “Type” (red-bordered circle), “Blackburn coll. 1910-236.”, “Paropsis Adelaidae Blackb.”, “6199 T. N.S.W.” (on card with insect)

Female; carded; Size: 5.4 mm; A-A: 1.0 mm, HCE/BL: 21% (approx, estimated from photo of lateral view).

Head and pronotum uniformly brown; foveal peaks present but same colour as derm; elytra brown with dark-brown irregularly arranged verrucae, a few (especially near elytral suture and apex) falling into longitudinal series.  A small area anterior to elytral middle is verruca-free.

Original description

[Original description shown in figure, translated from Latin using ChatGPT, 03/2026]

♀. Somewhat ovate, rather broad, moderately convex, the greatest height (seen from the side) placed about the middle of the elytra; moderately shining; obscure reddish, the underside and elytra clouded with pitchy; antennae (in the typical specimen) wanting; head closely, somewhat rugulose-punctulate.

Prothorax wider than long in the ratio about 2½ : 1, dilated from the apex for a considerable distance beyond the middle, near the apex transversely impressed, rather closely and somewhat rugulose, finely (at the sides a little more coarsely) punctulate; sides rather rounded, rather narrowly flattened, posterior angles almost absent; scutellum finely rugulose.

Elytra slightly depressed below the humeral callus, behind the base with a rounded impression, finely and rather closely somewhat in rows (scarcely more so at the sides, scarcely less so behind, rather strongly) punctate; furnished with rather numerous, less distinct verrucae, somewhat arranged in rows; interstices rather rugulose; marginal portion scarcely distinct from the disc; humeral callus with its inner margin very much farther from the suture than from the lateral margin of the elytra; basal ventral segment sparsely and finely punctulate.

Length 2½ lines (~5.3 mm); width 1⅘ lines (~3.8 mm).

Notes

Blackburn (1896) deals with P. abjecta as part of his 'subgroup ii' (i.e., those species without "strongly marked characters", whose greatest height is not or scarcely in front of the middle of the elytral margin and whose elytra are depressed under the humeral callus).  Within that group, he characterises it as:

(1) inner edge of humeral callus distinctly nearer to lateral margin of elytra than to suture;

(2) sides of prothorax more or less explanate;

(3) elytra not having well-defined continuous costae;

(4) puncturation of the elytra decidedly fine;

(5) prothorax much narrowed in front, widest considerably behind middle;

(6) size very small (2½ lines, ~5.3 mm).

T. abjecta is very likely to be Ta224, a species that occurs in southern Queensland and northern N.S.W.  The form of the elytral sculpture is the same and the size of the type is within the size range of female specimens of Ta224.  The measured value of A-A/BL is in the expected range for that species.  The estimated value of HCE/BL is slighly lower than is typical for Ta224, but not unreasonably low.  Another N.S.W. species of that size with very similar elytral sculpture is Ta66, but is a less likely candidate as its A-A/BL values are much lower than those of this type specimen.

References

Blackburn, T. 1896, "Revision of the genus Paropsis. Part I", Proceedings of the Linnean Society of New South Wales, vol. 21, no. 4, 637-693 (page 666).
