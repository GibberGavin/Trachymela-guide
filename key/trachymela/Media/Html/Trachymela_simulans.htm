Trachymela simulans

Name

Trachymela simulans (Blackburn, 1896)

Type specimen

Location: BMNH

Label data: “Type” (red-bordered circle), “6198 T. Sydney”, “Blackburn coll. 1910-236.”, “Paropsis simulans Blackb.”

Female; Length: 8.4 mm (approx.);  A-A: 1.39 mm; HCE/BL = 19.5% (rough estimate from photo of lateral view of type specimen).

A uniformly brown species with quite inconspicuous verrucae on elytra; scutellum smooth.

Original description

[Original description shown in figure, translated from Latin using ChatGPT, 03/2026]

♀. Somewhat ovate, rather broad, less convex, the greatest height (seen from the side) placed about the middle of the elytra; moderately shining; chestnut-coloured, the antennae toward the apex and the breast pitchy; head rather finely, rather densely, scarcely roughly punctulate; prothorax wider than long in the ratio about 2¾ : 1, dilated from the apex for a considerable distance beyond the middle, near the apex transversely impressed, rather densely and finely, not rugulose (but at the sides coarsely rugulose) punctulate; sides rather rounded, rather narrowly flattened, posterior angles almost absent; scutellum smooth.

Elytra depressed below the humeral callus, behind the base transversely slightly impressed, finely (with even finer punctures intermixed, a little less fine at the sides, a little finer posteriorly) somewhat in rows punctulate; furnished with rather numerous verrucae (these less elevated), sparsely arranged in rows (obsolete toward the base); interstices not rugulose (toward the apex scarcely evidently so); marginal portion narrow, clearly separated from the disc (by a continuous groove); humeral callus with its inner margin a little more distant from the suture than from the lateral margin of the elytra; basal ventral segment rather densely and somewhat strongly punctulate.

Length 3¾ lines (~7.95 mm); width 2⅘ lines (~5.9 mm).

Notes

Blackburn (1896) deals with P. simulans as part of his subgroup ii (i.e., those species without "strongly marked characters", whose greatest height is not or scarcely in front of the middle of the elytral margin and whose elytra are depressed under the humeral callus).  Within that group, he characterises it as:

(1) inner edge of humeral callus distinctly nearer to lateral margin of elytra than to suture;

(2) sides of prothorax more or less explanate;

(3) elytra not having well-defined continuous costae;

(4) puncturation of the elytra decidedly fine;

(5) prothorax much narrowed in front, widest considerably behind middle;

(6) size moderate ( 3¾ lines, ~8 mm).

 This is very possibly Ta24 - most of the characteristics in the description fit, though the HCE/BL ratio is a little on the low side (though the photo has the specimen oriented poorly for this measurement to be very accurate).

References

Blackburn, T. 1896, "Revision of the genus Paropsis. Part I", Proceedings of the Linnean Society of New South Wales, vol. 21, no. 4, 637-693 (page 666).
