Trachymela sternalis

Name

Trachymela sternalis (Blackburn, 1896)

Type specimen

Location: BMNH

Label data: “Type” (red-bordered circle), “5519 N.Terr. T”, “Blackburn coll. 1910-236.”, “Paropsis sternalis Blackb.”

Female; Size: 8.7 x 6.75 x 3.5 mm; A-A: 1.69 mm, HCE/BL = 20% (approx, obtained from measurement on a photo of a specimen).

The specimen is tenereal and the head is covered in fungal hyphae.  Colour yellow-brown, pronotum and head slightly darker, black spots on pronotum as in photos; verrucae are aligned in longitudinal rows.

 

Original description

[Original description shown in figure, translated from Latin using ChatGPT, 03/2026]

(Female) Oval, moderately convex, the greatest height (seen from the side) placed before the middle of the elytra; less shining; yellowish-chestnut; on the prothorax four spots (placed transversely) and on the elytra numerous verrucae black; head rather closely and strongly punctulate; prothorax rather closely and strongly (coarsely at the sides) punctulate; this longer than broad by more than double (about 2 4/7 to 1), widened from the apex far beyond the middle, behind the apex transversely very faintly impressed, sides slightly arcuate, not flattened, posterior angles rounded; scutellum smooth and almost polished; elytra scarcely depressed beneath the humeral callus, behind the base scarcely transversely impressed, rather closely subseriately strongly (a little more than the pronotum, at the sides scarcely more than on the disc) punctate, interstices scarcely rugulose, marginal area scarcely distinct from the disc (submarginal groove obsolete, not reaching the apex, toward the apex slightly impressed), humeral callus much more distant from the suture than from the lateral margin; basal ventral segment sparsely somewhat strongly punctate; prosternum broadly concave in the middle.  Length 4 lines (~9 mm), width 3 lines (~6.75 mm).

Notes

Blackburn (1896) deals with T. sternalis as part of his subgroup i (i.e., those species with "strongly marked characters").  Within that group, he characterises it as:

(1) prosternum sulcate down the middle, but very wide, and scarcely narrowed in front;

(2) colour testaceous or red [not black]; elytra moderately punctured;

(3) prothorax at its widest much behind the middle;

(4) sides of elytra slope obliquely outward, no subhumeral depression.

This species seems close in structure to Ta133, but is probably not that species, as the humeral callus seems too far from the lateral edge on the type of T. sternalis.

References

Blackburn, T. 1896, "Revision of the genus Paropsis. Part I", Proceedings of the Linnean Society of New South Wales, vol. 21, no. 4, 637-693 (page 646).
