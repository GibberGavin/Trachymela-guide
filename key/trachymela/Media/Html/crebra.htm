crebra

Name

Paropsis crebra Blackburn, 1897

Corresponds to

This does not seem to correspond to any of the current species codes.

Diagnosis and Notes

In his revision of the genus Paropsis (Blackburn, 1897), he places crebra into his "Group iv", a mixed bag of species characterized by (1) the sides of the prothorax evenly arched (not mucronate in front), (2) the puncturation of the elytra in about 20 rows, and (3) the elytra devoid of verrucae.   This group includes just a few species of non-verrucose Trachymela - the bulk of its species are now placed in Peltoschema or Paropsisterna.  In his key to the species in this group, crebra is identified by:

A. Elytra with a distinct subbasal impression;

BB. Lateral margins of prothorax not or scarcely explanate;

CC. The subbasal impression of the elytra very feeble;

DD. Size much larger [than P. simplex].  The scutellum unpunctured and very nitid.

This is most likely an orbicularis-group species.

Type specimen

Not examined.

Original description

Translation of original description (Figure 1) by ChatGPT, 04/2026:

Rather broadly ovate; fairly shining; fairly convex; the greatest height (seen from the side) situated about the middle of the elytral margin; reddish-brown, with the sterna, the apical part of the antennae, and some indefinite small marks on the pronotum somewhat darkened.  Head densely and moderately strongly punctulate.  Pronotum about 2½ times as broad as long, scarcely impressed behind the apex, from the apex dilated far beyond the middle, doubly punctulate (more densely very finely, and less densely somewhat more strongly; at the sides coarsely rugulose); sides fairly strongly rounded, not flattened; posterior angles rounded.  Scutellum smooth.  Elytra slightly depressed beneath the humeral callus, behind the base transversely less distinctly impressed, more densely and moderately strongly punctulate in somewhat serial arrangement (at the sides more strongly, posteriorly less strongly so); the marginal part moderately broad (and posteriorly expanded fairly strongly obliquely outward) and fairly distinct from the disc; the inner margin of the humeral callus scarcely more distant from the suture than from the lateral margin of the elytra.  Basal ventral segment almost smooth.  Length 3½ lines [~7.4 mm], width 2½ lines [~5.3 mm].

References

Blackburn, T. 1897, "Revision of the genus Paropsis. Part II", Proceedings of the Linnean Society of New South Wales, vol. 22, 166-189 (page 184).
