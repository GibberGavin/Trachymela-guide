lugubris

Name

Trachymela lugubris Weise, 1916

Corresponds to

No known correspondence with any of the species codes.

Diagnosis and Notes

This is a small, black species lacking verrucae, possibly a solivagus-group species. 

Type specimen

Not examined.

Original description

Translation of original description (Figure 1) by ChatGPT, 04/2026:

Somewhat oval, convex, black, fairly shining; antennae and labrum fulvous; head densely doubly punctulate; pronotum convex, densely finely punctate, more strongly punctate toward the sides; elytra densely, fairly strongly punctate with weak striation. - Length 5.5 mm. Northwest Australia: Derby (October); Kimberley (January, February). 6 specimens.

Var. a: immature, brownish-red, with paler legs.

In size fairly similar to the preceding species [T. fusconotata], but more slender in build and with the elytra more strongly and almost uniformly densely punctured in rows, without granules; only the first three intervals near the base and a few on the declivity toward the apex somewhat thickened and slightly convex, especially the first, which is somewhat more strongly curved there than in related species.  The body is black, moderately shining; antennae, labrum, and often to a greater or lesser extent the tarsi of the fore legs reddish-yellow.  The head is densely and very finely doubly punctate. The pronotum more than twice as broad as long, with greatest width at or slightly before the middle; sides rounded; the disc fairly strongly transversely convex and also markedly sloping toward the anterior angles; in the middle finely and densely punctate, near the sides more strongly punctate.  The punctures of the elytra are considerably larger than those on the sides of the thorax.

References

Weise, J. 1916. Results of Dr. E. Mjöberg's Swedish scientific expeditions to Australia 1910-1913. 11. Chrysomeliden und Coccinelliden aus West-Australien. Arkiv för Zoologi 10(20): 1-51 (page 35)
