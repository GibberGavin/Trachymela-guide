raucipennis

Name

Paropsis raucipennis Blackburn, 1896

Corresponds to

Ta177.

Diagnosis and Notes

Blackburn (1896) deals with P. raucipennis as part of his 'subgroup i' (i.e., those species with "strongly marked characters").  Within that group, he characterises it as:

AAA. Prosternum normal, but other characters exceptional, as follows:-;

BBB. The exceptional characters lie in the elytral epipleurae;

CC. Inner (horizontal) part of epipleurae nearly reaches the apex as a distinct ledge;

D. Basal ventral segment coarsely punctured;

E. Sides of prothorax strongly explanate;

FF. Underside black;

GG. interstices of elytral punctures strongly rugulose, almost concealing the punctures.

Only the last item in his key separates raucipennis from latipes.  This is almost certainly the same species as Ta177, and therefore conspecific also with karattae.  The aedeagus is almost identical, though the elytrae of the type do not have verrucae as clearly arranged in series as the type of karattae.  However, this character is rather variable in Ta177. Blackburn's revision is based on very limited material, so that he was unaware of the extent of variation in many of the species he dealt with.  For example, he states that he had a single female (although the type in the BMNH is a male!) of raucipennis and a single male of chapuisi.  With karattae, he does not state so explicitly, but it seems he was basing his description on a single female specimen.

Type specimen

Location: BMNH

Label data: /“Type” (red-bordered circle)/ “6149 S.A. T”/ “Blackburn coll. 1910-236.”/ “Paropsis raucipennis Blackb.”/.

Male (aedeagus extracted and glued to card), but in the description Blackburn mentions that his material consists of a unique female specimen; Size: 10.3 (L) x 9.2 (W) x 5.1 (H) mm;  A-A: 1.60 mm; Aedeagus: LPE=4.475 mm.  HCE/BL: ~23.8% (estimated from photo of lateral view of type specimen).

Head brown; pronotum brown, strongly explanate, black mark in foveal area, elytra with punctures almost hidden in strongly wrinkled surface, the wrinkles taking a general lateral orientation, only a few small black verrucae present near apex, elytra strongly explanate.

Original description

Translation of original description (Figure 6) by ChatGPT, 03/2026:

♀. Broadly oval, very convex, the greatest height (seen from the side) placed just before the middle of the elytra; less shining; chestnut, with some spots on the prothorax, the suture of the elytra (and some verrucae), and the underside of the body (except the coxae and apex of the abdomen) black; antennae (except the base) darkened; head rather closely and somewhat finely punctulate; prothorax much wider than long, by considerably more than double (nearly 2¾ to 1), widened a little beyond the middle from the apex, rather closely and somewhat finely (on the disc a little less closely, at the sides rather coarsely) punctate; the rest as in the preceding species (P. chapuisi); scutellum dull in the middle, closely punctate; elytra closely granulate-rugulose (so that the punctures are scarcely visible), behind the base scarcely distinctly impressed, otherwise as in the preceding; epipleura and basal ventral segment sculptured as in the preceding.  Length 5 lines [~10.6 mm], width 4⅕ lines [~8.9 mm].

References

Blackburn, T. 1896, "Revision of the genus Paropsis. Part I", Proceedings of the Linnean Society of New South Wales, vol. 21, no. 4, 637-693 (page 650).
