rustica

Name

Paropsis rustica Blackburn, 1896

Corresponds to

No known correspondence with any of the species codes.

Diagnosis and Notes

Blackburn (1896) deals with T. rustica as part of his 'subgroup i' (i.e., those species with "strongly marked characters").  Within that group, he characterises it as:

AAA. Prosternum normal, but other characters exceptional, as follows :-

BBB. The exceptional characters lie in the elytral epipleurae;

CC. inner (horizontal) part of epipleurae nearly reaches the apex as a distinct ledge;

DD. Basal ventral segment feebly punctulate;

E. Elytra with a postbasal discal impression;

F. The marginal part of elytra moderately wide and more or less vertical;

GG. Size much smaller (Long. 5 l. [~ 10.6 mm]), suture concolorous with general surface.

This relatively large species does not seem to be any of the coded species (Ta01-238), and it is likely I have not seen it.  Blackburn indicates its type locality as Forest Reefs, in central NSW, not far from the town of Orange.

Type specimen

Location: BMNH

Label data: /“Type” (red-bordered circle)/ “6166 N.S.W. T”/ “Blackburn coll. 1910-236.”/ “Paropsis ruficollis Blackb.”/.

Female; Size: 10.15 (L) x 7.8 (W) x 5.1 (H) mm; A-A: 1.55 mm; HCE/BL: ~22.3% (obtained by measurement from lateral view in photo).

Head brown; pronotum brown, surface noticeably wrinkled; scutellum almost black, smooth; elytral surface brown, noticeable anterior-median excavation, many black verrucae, often small, many with a central puncture, many in middle extended a little in lateral direction, those above humeral callus in short longitudinal rows, excavate under humeral callus.

Original description

Translation of original description (Figure 3) by ChatGPT, 03/2026:

Oval, rather narrow; moderately convex, the greatest height (seen from the side) situated far before the middle of the elytral margin; rather dull; reddish-brown (with the elytra bearing numerous verrucae arranged fairly evenly but not regularly in rows, and with several indistinct streaks, the sterna and epipleura black; antennae darkened towards the apex).  Head and prothorax nearly as in P. laeviventris, but the latter more transverse (about 2¾ to 1), the sides scarcely flattened and less strongly rounded.  Humeral depression of the elytra, the submarginal groove (this less abruptly interrupted in the middle), the subbasal impression, and the epipleura as in P. Chapuisi.  Elytra rather strongly and fairly closely punctured in somewhat serial arrangement; the interstices on the disc scarcely rugulose (if the verrucae are disregarded), the marginal area rather coarsely rugulose.  Basal ventral segment finely punctured.  Length 5 lines [~10.6 mm], width almost 4 lines [~8.5 mm].
