simulans

Name

Paropsis simulans Blackburn, 1896

Corresponds to

Likely Ta24, but see Diagnosis and Notes.

Diagnosis and Notes

Blackburn (1896) deals with P. simulans as part of his subgroup ii (i.e., those species without "strongly marked characters", whose greatest height is not or scarcely in front of the middle of the elytral margin and whose elytra are depressed under the humeral callus).  Within that group, he characterises it as:

A. inner edge of humeral callus distinctly nearer to lateral margin of elytra than to suture;

B. Sides of prothorax more or less explanate;

C. Elytra not having well-defined continuous costae;

DD. Puncturation of the elytra decidedly fine;

EE. Prothorax much narrowed in front, widest considerably behind middle;

F. Size moderate (Long. 3¾ lines [~8 mm]).

This is very likely Ta24 - most of the characteristics in the description fit well, though the pronotum width/length ratio (2.2-2.5, n=22) is much lower than the 2.75 given by Blackburn (his given values are often considerably on the high side).  The strong narrowing of the pronotum anteriorly is typical of Ta24.  The estimated HCE/BL ratio is a little on the low side (though the photo has the specimen oriented poorly for this measurement to be very accurate).  The type specimen seems slightly more flattened, and the rows of verrucae towards the posterior of the elytra, though low, appear more prominent than is typical in specimens of Ta24.

Type specimen

Location: BMNH

Label data: /“Type” (red-bordered circle)/ “6198 T. Sydney”/ “Blackburn coll. 1910-236.”/ “Paropsis simulans Blackb.”/.

Female; Length: 8.4 mm (approx.);  A-A: 1.39 mm; HCE/BL ~19.5% (rough estimate from photo of lateral view of type specimen).

A uniformly brown species with quite inconspicuous verrucae on elytra; scutellum smooth.

Original description

Translation of original description (Figure 4) by ChatGPT, 03/2026:

♀. Somewhat ovate, rather broad, less convex, the greatest height (seen from the side) placed about the middle of the elytra; moderately shining; chestnut-coloured, the antennae toward the apex and the breast pitchy; head rather finely, rather densely, scarcely roughly punctulate; prothorax wider than long in the ratio about 2¾ : 1, dilated from the apex for a considerable distance beyond the middle, near the apex transversely impressed, rather densely and finely, not rugulose (but at the sides coarsely rugulose) punctulate; sides rather rounded, rather narrowly flattened, posterior angles almost absent; scutellum smooth.  Elytra depressed below the humeral callus, behind the base transversely slightly impressed, finely (with even finer punctures intermixed, a little less fine at the sides, a little finer posteriorly) somewhat in rows punctulate; furnished with rather numerous verrucae (these less elevated), sparsely arranged in rows (obsolete toward the base); interstices not rugulose (toward the apex scarcely evidently so); marginal portion narrow, clearly separated from the disc (by a continuous groove); humeral callus with its inner margin a little more distant from the suture than from the lateral margin of the elytra; basal ventral segment rather densely and somewhat strongly punctulate.  Length 3¾ lines [~8 mm); width 2⅘ lines (~6 mm).

References

Blackburn, T. 1896, "Revision of the genus Paropsis. Part I", Proceedings of the Linnean Society of New South Wales, vol. 21, no. 4, 637-693 (page 666).
