sternalis

Name

Paropsis sternalis Blackburn, 1896

Corresponds to

Possibly Ta128 or Ta147, but not with high confidence.

Diagnosis and Notes

Blackburn (1896) deals with sternalis as part of his 'subgroup i' (i.e., those species with "strongly marked characters").  Within that group, he characterises it as:

AA. Prosternum sulcate down the middle, but very wide, and scarcely narrowed in front;

B. Colour testaceous or red [not black]; elytra moderately punctured;

C. Prothorax at its widest much behind the middle;

DD. Sides of elytra slope obliquely outward, no subhumeral depression.

There are several species that are very similar to the sternalis type distributed in central Australia.  The prosternal process is particularly wide in Ta133, but the humeral callus seems too far from the lateral edge on the type of T. sternalis and the distribution of verrucae is somewhat different.  Two other species that have a wide distribution in central Australia are Ta128 and Ta147.  Both have a broad prosternal process whose sides do not converge anteriorly, though it is not as broad as that of Ta133.  However, the distribution of verrucae is very similar to that of the sternalis type.  Black discal spots on the pronotum are present in some specimens.  However, the A-A/BL ratios are significantly lower in these species than the sternalis type.

Type specimen

Location: BMNH

Label data: /“Type” (red-bordered circle)/ “5519 N.Terr. T”/ “Blackburn coll. 1910-236.”/ “Paropsis sternalis Blackb.”/.

Female; Size: 8.7 (L) x 6.75 (W) x 3.5 (H) mm; A-A: 1.69 mm, HCE/BL: ~20% (approx, obtained from measurement on a photo of a specimen).

The specimen is tenereal and the head is covered in fungal hyphae.  Colour yellow-brown, pronotum and head slightly darker, black spots on pronotum as in photos; verrucae are aligned in longitudinal rows.

Original description

Translation of original description (Figure 3) by ChatGPT, 03/2026:

♀. Oval, moderately convex, the greatest height (seen from the side) placed before the middle of the elytra; less shining; yellowish-chestnut; on the prothorax four spots (placed transversely) and on the elytra numerous verrucae black; head rather closely and strongly punctulate; prothorax rather closely and strongly (coarsely at the sides) punctulate; this wider than long by more than double (about 2 4/7 to 1), widened from the apex far beyond the middle, behind the apex transversely very faintly impressed, sides slightly arcuate, not flattened, posterior angles rounded; scutellum smooth and almost polished; elytra scarcely depressed beneath the humeral callus, behind the base scarcely transversely impressed, rather closely subseriately strongly (a little more than the pronotum, at the sides scarcely more than on the disc) punctate, interstices scarcely rugulose, marginal area scarcely distinct from the disc (submarginal groove obsolete, not reaching the apex, toward the apex slightly impressed), humeral callus much more distant from the suture than from the lateral margin; basal ventral segment sparsely somewhat strongly punctate; prosternum broadly concave in the middle.  Length 4 lines [~8.5 mm], width 3 lines [~6.4 mm].

References

Blackburn, T. 1896, "Revision of the genus Paropsis. Part I", Proceedings of the Linnean Society of New South Wales, vol. 21, no. 4, 637-693 (page 646).
