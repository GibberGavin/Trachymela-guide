Taxonomic History of the Genus Trachymela

Taxonomic History

The first Trachymela species described were three species by Marsham (1808) as Notoclea verrucosa, N. punctata and N. castanea. Marsham created Notoclea to include a number of species of paropsine beetles that are now variously included in the genera Paropsis, Paropsisterma, Trachymela and Dicranosterna. Marsham’s descriptions are all very brief and, even though a rather small drawings of a specimen of each species is included, are not sufficient to identify which of the currently known species was being referred to.

More species were described as species of Paropsis by other early describers including Erichson (1842), Germar (1848), Boheman (1859), Stål (1860), Clark (1865) and Chapuis (1877). The Reverend Thomas Blackburn, in a series of papers (1896-1901) revised the genus Paropsis. He kept this by now large genus intact but split it into several “Groups” which largely followed and extended a schema first used by Chapuis (1877). It is ”Group iii” within this schema that contained almost all of the species that are now attributed to Trachymela. Blackburn dealt with this group in the first two papers of his revision of Paropsis (Blackburn, 1896 & 1897) and described many new species.

It was the German entomologist Julius Weise who in 1908 erected the genus Trachymela, though characterizing it very poorly:

“Those Paropsis species with doubled series of punctures on the elytra, mostly red-brown in colour, possess tubercles (verrucae) and therefore have a more or less rough dorsal surface, and comprising roughly Group iii of Blackburn” (translated from German).

However, Weise did not nominate a type species for the genus; Trachymela sloanei was eventually designated as type species by Kelly and Reid (1999).

Weise (1915) described a genus Chondromela for the single species Chondromela mjoebergi, which was synonymized with Trachymela by Reid (2006). Only five further species (Daccordi, 2003) have been described in the genus since the time of Weise, making a total of about 120 described species. Reid (2006) revised the genera of Chrysomelini, providing a detailed synopsis of and diagnostic key to all the paropsine genera, including Trachymela. His key and diagnosis of the genus should be used to confirm that a species belongs to the genus.

The only key to the species of Trachymela has been the one from Blackburn (1896). However, Blackburn made no use of either genitalia or the nature or distribution of cuticular wax in his key, and it is therefore almost impossible to use in practice for both dead or live specimens. Blackburn (1896) divided the genus (his Group iii) into 4 subgroups, and this has been the only attempt at a sub-generic arrangement.
